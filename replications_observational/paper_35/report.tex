\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Moon That Orbits Backwards}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Neptune's giant moon \textbf{Triton} is one of the weirdest objects in the Solar System. While almost all moons orbit in the same direction their planet spins, Triton orbits \textbf{backwards} (a retrograde orbit). How did this happen?

\subsection*{The Cosmic Kidnapping}
Triton did not form around Neptune! It was originally a large, icy dwarf planet (a twin of Pluto) orbiting in the Kuiper Belt with a companion. Billions of years ago, the pair passed too close to Neptune. Neptune's gravity violently kicked the companion into deep space and captured Triton into an extremely elongated, backward orbit!

\subsection*{The Great Melting}
During the first $100\,\text{million years}$ after capture, Neptune's ferocious tidal forces warped and squeezed Triton, dissipating enough heat to melt the entire moon into a global liquid ocean before its orbit settled into a neat circle!

\subsection*{1. Binary-Single Exchange Capture Kinematics}
When a Kuiper Belt binary $(T_1, T_2)$ of mutual semi-major axis $a_{\text{bin}}$ encounters Neptune with impact parameter $b$, the condition for exchange capture is that the orbital speed relative to Neptune $v_\infty$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
v_\infty \le \sqrt{\frac{G (M_1 + M_2)}{a_{\text{bin}}}}
\end{equation}
Energy transfer ejects $T_2$ at hyper-velocity, binding $T_1$ (Triton) into a bound orbit with initial pericenter $q \approx 7 - 10\,R_{\text{Nep}}$ and eccentricity $e_0 \approx 0.99$.

\subsection*{2. Tidal Circularization with Constant Angular Momentum}
Because tidal dissipation occurs primarily at pericenter, orbital specific angular momentum $h = \sqrt{G M_{\text{Nep}} a (1 - e^2)}$ is conserved:
\begin{equation}
a_{\text{final}} = a(t) [1 - e^2(t)] \implies a(t) = \frac{a_{\text{final}}}{1 - e^2(t)}
\end{equation}
The total gravitational orbital energy dissipated as heat within Triton is:
\begin{equation}
\Delta E_{\text{tide}} = \frac{G M_{\text{Nep}} M_{\text{Tri}}}{2 a_{\text{final}}} \approx 4.5 \times 10^{28}\,\text{J}
\end{equation}
which is enough to completely melt Triton's ice mantle 10 times over!
