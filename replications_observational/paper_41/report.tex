\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Best Candidate for Alien Life}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 40 light-years away, \textbf{TRAPPIST-1e} is an Earth-sized rocky world orbiting right in the "Goldilocks Zone" of an ultra-cool red dwarf star. It receives about $66\%$ of the sunlight Earth gets—almost the exact same amount of energy as Mars receives from our Sun!

\subsection*{A Temperate World with Oceans}
Because TRAPPIST-1e has a dense rocky core and strong surface gravity ($93\%$ of Earth), climate models show that a modest atmosphere containing carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{CO_2}$) and water vapor provides enough greenhouse warmth to support vast, glistening liquid water oceans and temperate blue skies!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Stefan-Boltzmann Greenhouse Warming}

\subsection*{1. Bare-Rock vs. Greenhouse Equilibrium Temperature}
The bare-rock equilibrium temperature with Bond albedo $A_B \approx 0.10$ and uniform redistribution is:
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{bare}} = \left( \frac{S_\star (1 - A_B)}{4 \sigma_{\text{SB}}} \right)^{1/4} \approx 230\,\text{K} \quad (-43^\circ\text{C})
\end{equation}
With a $\mathrm{CO_2}$ atmospheric greenhouse optical depth $\tau_L \approx 1.5$ in the thermal infrared, the surface temperature warms via the Eddington grey slab model:
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{surf}}^4 = T_{\text{bare}}^4 \left( 1 + \frac{3}{4}\tau_L \right) \implies T_{\text{surf}} \approx 278\,\text{K} \quad (+5^\circ\text{C})
\end{equation}
This comfortably places TRAPPIST-1e in the stable liquid water regime ($273\,\text{K} \le T \le 373\,\text{K}$).

\subsection*{2. Tidal Heating vs. Radiogenic Basal Flux}
Resonant eccentricity forcing ($e \approx 0.005$) generates steady viscoelastic tidal heating in TRAPPIST-1e's silicate mantle:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{tide}} = \frac{21}{2} \frac{k_2}{Q} \frac{G M_\star^2 R_p^5 n e^2}{a^6 4\pi R_p^2} \approx 0.045\,\text{W/m}^2
\end{equation}
Comparable to Earth's internal radiogenic heat flux ($0.08\,\text{W/m}^2$), maintaining active mantle convection and volcanic outgassing to replenish atmospheric volatiles.

\begin{equation}
