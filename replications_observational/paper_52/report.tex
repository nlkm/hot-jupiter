\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Solitary Ice Volcano of Dwarf Planet Ceres}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Rising abruptly out of the dark, cratered plains of dwarf planet \textbf{Ceres} stands \textbf{Ahuna Mons}, an enormous solitary mountain \textbf{2.5 miles tall} ($4\,\text{km}$) and \textbf{11 miles wide} ($17\,\text{km}$) with brilliant white salt streaks cascading down its steep flanks.

\subsection*{How Salt-Water Magma Builds Mountains in the Asteroid Belt}
Ahuna Mons is a \textbf{cryovolcano} (an ice volcano). Instead of red-hot molten silicate lava, Ahuna Mons erupted a dense, viscous slush of salty liquid water, mud, and water ice from a subsurface reservoir. Under Ceres's frigid temperatures and vacuum, the slush froze as it oozed upwards, piling up like thick toothpaste into a gigantic volcanic dome!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Bingham Plastic Dome Mechanics}

\subsection*{1. Cryomagma Extrusion \& Yield Stress Equilibrium}
A volcanic dome formed by the extrusion of a non-Newtonian Bingham fluid with yield strength $\tau_0$ and density $\rho$ in gravitational acceleration $g$ maintains a characteristic parabolic radial profile:
\begin{equation}
h(r) = H_0 \sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{r}{R_0}\right)^2}
\end{equation}
The maximum dome height $H_0$ is governed by the balance between hydrostatic basal driving stress and plastic yield resistance:
\begin{equation}
H_0 \approx \sqrt{\frac{2 \tau_0 R_0}{\rho_{\text{slurry}} g_{\text{Ceres}}}}
\end{equation}
For Ahuna Mons on Ceres ($g = 0.28\,\text{m/s}^2$, $\rho_{\text{slurry}} = 1350\,\text{kg/m}^3$, $R_0 = 10.0\,\text{km}$, $H_0 = 4.0\,\text{km}$):
\begin{equation}
\tau_0 \approx \frac{\rho_{\text{slurry}} g H_0^2}{2 R_0} \approx \frac{(1350)(0.28)(4000)^2}{2(10000)} \approx 3.0 \times 10^4\,\text{Pa} \quad (30\,\text{kPa})
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Slurry Viscosity \& Upwelling Speed}
The effective dynamic viscosity of the salt-rich slurry is $\eta_{\text{eff}} \approx 10^9 - 10^{11}\,\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, yielding an extrusion timescale of $\tau_{\text{ext}} \sim 10^2 - 10^4\,\text{years}$.

\subsection*{1. Diapiric Buoyancy \& Ascent Dynamics}
Cryomagma ascent is driven by the density contrast $\Delta\rho = \rho_{\text{crust}} - \rho_{\text{slurry}} \approx 1400 - 1350 = +50\,\text{kg/m}^3$ between hydrated crustal phyllosilicates/salts and liquid-rich brine diapirs. The Poiseuille conduit flow rate $Q$ through a conduit of radius $a_{\text{conduit}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
Q = \frac{\pi a_{\text{conduit}}^4}{8 \eta_{\text{eff}}} \left( \Delta\rho g_{\text{Ceres}} - \frac{2\tau_0}{a_{\text{conduit}}} \right)
\end{equation}
