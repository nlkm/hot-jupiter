\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — An Ocean World Under a Hydrogen Sky}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 124 light-years away, \textbf{K2-18b} is a "super-Earth" (or mini-Neptune) 8.6 times heavier than Earth, orbiting in the habitable zone of a cool red dwarf star. In 2023, NASA's James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) pointed its giant golden eye at K2-18b to answer a thrilling question: Is this planet a miniature gas giant, or a \textbf{"Hycean"} world—a planet with a global liquid water ocean under a hydrogen-rich atmosphere?

\subsection*{The Chemical Clues}
JWST detected abundant \textbf{methane} ($\mathrm{CH_4}$) and \textbf{carbon dioxide} ($\mathrm{CO_2}$), while finding virtually zero \textbf{ammonia} ($\mathrm{NH_3}$). Because ammonia easily dissolves in liquid water, its absence provides compelling evidence that a massive liquid ocean lies beneath the clouds, absorbing ammonia out of the sky!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Scale Height \& Henry's Law Ocean Partitioning}

\subsection*{1. Atmospheric Scale Height in a Hydrogen Atmosphere}
The atmospheric scale height $H$ determines the amplitude of spectral absorption features $\Delta D \approx 2 (R_p / R_\star) (H / R_\star)$:
\begin{equation}
H = \frac{k_B T_{\text{eq}}}{\mu g}
\end{equation}
For K2-18b ($T_{\text{eq}} \approx 255\,\text{K}, g = G M_p / R_p^2 \approx 12.4\,\text{m/s}^2$), a hydrogen-dominated envelope ($\mu \approx 2.3\,\text{g/mol}$) yields a large scale height $H \approx 74\,\text{km}$. This inflates transmission features to $\sim 50-80\,\text{ppm}$, readily detectable by JWST.

\subsection*{2. Aqueous Ammonia Depletion via Henry's Law}
The dissolution of atmospheric ammonia into a global liquid water ocean is governed by Henry's law:
\begin{equation}
k_H = \frac{C_{\text{aq}}}{P_{\text{gas}}} \approx 5.8 \times 10^{-1}\,\text{mol}/(\text{m}^3\,\text{Pa}) \quad \text{at } 298\,\text{K}
\end{equation}
For an ocean of mass fraction $f_{\text{ocean}} \ge 0.10\,M_p$, aqueous equilibrium partitions $> 99.99\%$ of total nitrogen into dissolved $\mathrm{NH_4^+}$ ions, reducing atmospheric ammonia mixing ratios below $10^{-5}$ ($10\,\text{ppm}$).

\subsection*{1. Coupled Photochemical Disequilibrium Kinetics}
We solve the 1D continuity equation for chemical species $i$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial n_i}{\partial t} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial z} \left( -K_{zz} \rho \frac{\partial (n_i/\rho)}{\partial z} \right) + P_i - L_i
\end{equation}
where UV photolysis drives photochemical methane oxidation:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CH_4} + h\nu \longrightarrow \mathrm{CH_3} + \mathrm{H}, \quad \mathrm{CO} + \mathrm{OH} \longrightarrow \mathrm{CO_2} + \mathrm{H}
\end{equation}
yielding steady-state mixing ratios $\chi_{\mathrm{CH_4}} \approx 10^{-2}$ and $\chi_{\mathrm{CO_2}} \approx 10^{-2}$, perfectly matching JWST retrievals.
