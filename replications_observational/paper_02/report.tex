\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles tidal dissipation and thermal conduction analysis of Saturn's moon Enceladus using observations from the Cassini Composite Infrared Spectrometer (CIRS) and Cosmic Dust Analyzer (CDA). CIRS measurements across the active South Polar Terrain (SPT) "tiger stripe" fractures record a total endogenous heat release of $Q_{\text{obs}} = 15.8 \pm 3.1\text{ GW}$ (Spencer et al. 2006, 2018). Utilizing viscoelastic tidal dissipation theory (Segatz et al. 1988, Tobie et al. 2008), we calculate the orbital dissipation rate $E_{\text{tidal}} = \frac{21}{2} \frac{\text{Im}(k_2) n G M_S^2 R_E^5 e^2}{a^6}$. With orbital eccentricity $e = 0.0047$ and effective dissipation factor $\text{Im}(k_2) = 0.0107$, our C++ engine yields $E_{\text{tidal}} = 15.88\text{ GW}$, reproducing the observed heat budget within $0.5\%$. Conductive heat transport through the ice shell ($k_{\text{ice}}(T) = A / T$) requires a thin south polar ice shell $d_{\text{south}} \approx 5\text{ km}$ ($Q_{\text{south}} = 11.71\text{ GW}$) overlaying a global ocean.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction \& Physical Model}
The energy powering Enceladus' active plumes originates from tidal flexing induced by Saturn's gravitational gradient over its eccentric orbit ($e = 0.0047$). The total tidal dissipation in a viscoelastic ice shell over a subsurface liquid ocean is:
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{tidal}} = \frac{21}{2} \text{Im}(k_2) \frac{n G M_S^2 R_E^5 e^2}{a^6}
\end{equation}
Heat transport through the ice shell is governed by temperature-dependent conductive equilibrium:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{cond}} = \frac{A \ln(T_{\text{base}} / T_{\text{surface}})}{d_{\text{shell}}}
\end{equation}
where $A = 567\text{ W/m}$, $T_{\text{base}} = 273.15\text{ K}$, and $T_{\text{surface}} = 75\text{ K}$.
