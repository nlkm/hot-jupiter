\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Beating Nitrogen Heart of Pluto}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
When NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew past \textbf{Pluto} in 2015, it photographed a giant, bright, heart-shaped basin named \textbf{Sputnik Planitia}. Covering an area the size of Texas, this vast frozen plain has zero impact craters—proving that its surface is constantly being renewed!

\subsection*{A Giant Boiling Pot of Frozen Gas}
Sputnik Planitia is made of solid nitrogen ice ($\mathrm{N_2}$) that is \textbf{4 miles deep} ($6\,\text{km}$). Heated by Pluto's faint internal warmth, the bottom of the nitrogen ice slowly warms and rises like warm soup in a boiling pot! It rises in the centers of giant 20-mile-wide hexagonal cells, spreads across the surface at a few centimeters per year, and sinks back down at the edges, completely repaving Pluto's heart every 500,000 years!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Rayleigh-Bénard Convection in Volatile Ice}

\subsection*{1. Critical Rayleigh Number for Solid-State Nitrogen Ice}
At $T \approx 38\,\text{K}$, solid nitrogen ice ($\alpha$-phase) transitions to the ductile hexagonal $\beta$-phase ($\eta \approx 10^{14}\,\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$). The dimensionless Rayleigh number governing thermal buoyancy over viscous resistance is:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Ra} = \frac{\rho g \alpha \Delta T D^3}{\kappa \eta}
\end{equation}
For $\rho \approx 1000\,\text{kg/m}^3, g = 0.62\,\text{m/s}^2, \alpha \approx 2 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{K}^{-1}, \Delta T \approx 5\,\text{K}, D \approx 6000\,\text{m}, \kappa \approx 4 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}, \eta \approx 10^{14}\,\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Ra} \approx \frac{1000 \times 0.62 \times (2 \times 10^{-3}) \times 5 \times (6000)^3}{4 \times 10^{-7} \times 10^{14}} \approx 3.3 \times 10^7 \gg \mathrm{Ra}_{\text{crit}} \approx 10^3
\end{equation}
Proving vigorous, supercritical Rayleigh-Bénard thermal convection is unavoidable.

\subsection*{2. Convective Flow Speed \& Cell Aspect Ratio}
From boundary layer scaling, the characteristic vertical upwelling velocity $w$ is:
\begin{equation}
w \approx \frac{\kappa}{D} \mathrm{Ra}^{2/3} \approx 6\,\text{cm/year}
\end{equation}
Yielding a surface renewal / overturning timescale $\tau_{\text{turn}} = D / w \approx 500,000\,\text{years}$, explaining the total absence of impact craters larger than $1\,\text{km}$.
