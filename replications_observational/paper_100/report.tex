\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Airless Lava World}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 48 light-years away in the constellation Indus, \textbf{LHS 3844b} is a rocky super-Earth $1.3$ times the size of Earth, circling a dim red dwarf star in an ultra-short orbit of just \textbf{11 hours}.

\subsection*{The First Definitively Airless Rocky Exoplanet}
When NASA's Spitzer Space Telescope monitored LHS 3844b continuously across its entire 11-hour orbit, astronomers solved one of the biggest questions in astrobiology: \textit{Can rocky planets orbiting active red dwarf stars keep their atmospheres?}
Spitzer discovered that the planet's dayside is roasting at \textbf{$1{,}410^\circ\text{F}$ ($1040\,\text{K}$)}, while its nightside plunges to near absolute zero (below $-400^\circ\text{F}$). If LHS 3844b had even a thin atmosphere like Mars, winds would circulate heat to the nightside. The total absence of heat transport proves that stellar stellar flares have stripped away all air, exposing a completely bare, dark volcanic basalt rock surface resembling Mercury or the Moon!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Thermal Phase Curve Inversion \& Atmosphere Constraints}

\subsection*{1. Instantaneous Local Thermal Equilibrium}
For an airless, tidally locked rocky sphere with zero heat redistribution ($\mathcal{E} = 0$), the surface temperature at local stellar zenith angle $\theta$ is determined by instantaneous radiative equilibrium:
\begin{equation}
T(\theta) = \left( \frac{(1 - A_B) F_* \cos\theta}{\epsilon_s \sigma_{\text{SB}}} \right)^{1/4}
\end{equation}
yielding peak sub-stellar temperature $T_{\text{day}} \approx 1040\,\text{K}$ and nightside temperature $T_{\text{night}} \approx 0\,\text{K}$ ($T_{\text{night}} < 100\,\text{K}$).

\subsection*{2. Thermal Phase Curve Amplitude \& Basaltic Surface Mineralogy}
The observed $4.5\,\mu\text{m}$ infrared phase curve flux variation:
\begin{equation}
\frac{F_p(\phi)}{F_*} = \left(\frac{R_p}{R_*}\right)^2 \frac{\int_{\text{visible}} B_\lambda(T(\theta, \phi)) \cos\mu \, dA}{\pi I_{*,\lambda}} \approx 380\,\text{ppm}
\end{equation}
matches a low-albedo basaltic rock surface ($A_B = 0.05 \pm 0.03$), ruling out all surface atmospheric pressures $P_{\text{surf}} > 0.1\,\text{bar}$ at $>5\sigma$ confidence.

\subsection*{1. Atmospheric Stripping by M-Dwarf Flare Activity}
Thermal phase curve inversion rules out thick volatile inventories ($\mathrm{CO}_2, \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}, \mathrm{N}_2, \mathrm{O}_2$) with $P_{\text{surf}} \ge 0.1\,\text{bar}$, confirming that extreme youthful stellar EUV flux and coronal mass ejections drove complete hydrodynamic and photoevaporative envelope loss.
