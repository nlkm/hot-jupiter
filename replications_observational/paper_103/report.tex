\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Ice Volcano of the Asteroid Belt}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Rising solitarily out of the heavily cratered plains of dwarf planet \textbf{(1) Ceres}, \textbf{Ahuna Mons} is a majestic, steep-sided mountain towering \textbf{2.5 miles (4.0 km) high and 12 miles (20 km) across}.

\subsection*{Erupting Salty Ice Slurry Instead of Red-Hot Lava}
Unlike Earth volcanoes that erupt molten basalt rock at thousands of degrees, Ahuna Mons is a \textbf{cryovolcano} (ice volcano). Hundreds of millions of years ago, a pressurized pocket of subterranean muddy brine—super-chilled salty water rich in sodium carbonate salts ($\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{CO}_3$)—squeezed up through fractures in Ceres's crust. Because Ceres is so cold, this thick, toothpaste-like cryomagma froze into rock-hard ice as it emerged, piling up into a towering dome with gleaming, salt-encrusted slopes!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Bingham Plastic Extrusion \& Viscoelastic Slump}

\subsection*{1. Cryomagma Rheology \& Dome Morphometry}
Ahuna Mons is a construct of extruded viscous cryomagma behaving as a Bingham plastic with yield stress $\tau_y \approx 1.5 \times 10^4\,\text{Pa}$. For Ceres microgravity ($g = 0.28\,\text{m/s}^2$) and slurry density $\rho \approx 1300\,\text{kg/m}^3$, the characteristic dome height $H_0$ and radius $R_0$ satisfy:
\begin{equation}
H_0 \approx \sqrt{\frac{2 \tau_y R_0}{\rho g}} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2(1.5 \times 10^4)(10^4)}{(1300)(0.28)}} \approx 4.0\,\text{km}
\end{equation}
The steep flank slopes reach $\theta_{\text{flank}} \approx 35^\circ - 40^\circ$.

\subsection*{2. Viscoelastic Ice Relaxation Timescale}
Over geological epochs, ductile flow of ice-brine mixtures flattens the dome. The Maxwell relaxation timescale $\tau_{\text{relax}} = \eta_{\text{eff}} / G \approx 100 - 300\,\text{Myr}$ indicates Ahuna Mons is geologically young ($t_{\text{age}} < 250\,\text{Myr}$), confirming recent internal cryomagmatic activity.

\subsection*{1. VIR Hyperspectral Salt Inversion}
Dawn Visible and Infrared (VIR) spectrometer spectra identify bright flank faculae as hydrated sodium carbonates ($\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{CO}_3 \cdot \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$) and ammonium chloride, confirming diapiric ascent from a localized relict subsurface mantle brine reservoir.
