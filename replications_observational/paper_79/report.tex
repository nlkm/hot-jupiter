\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — A Cobalt-Blue Planet Blasted by a Star Storm}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 64 light-years away in the constellation Vulpecula, \textbf{HD 189733b} is a gorgeous deep cobalt-blue gas giant where high-altitude silicate clouds rain molten glass sideways in $5{,}000\,\text{mph}$ howling supersonic winds.

\subsection*{Caught in the Crossfire of an Alien Superflare}
In April 2010, NASA's *Swift* space telescope detected a titanic, explosive \textbf{X-ray superflare} blasting from the host star. Just 8 hours later, when the planet passed in front of the star, the Hubble Space Telescope watched the superflare's intense radiation heat the planet's upper atmosphere to over $20{,}000^\circ\text{F}$, triggering a violent atmospheric blow-off that hurled over \textbf{450,000 metric tons of gas per second} into space!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Time-Dependent Hydrodynamic Flare Heating}

\subsection*{1. Flare XUV Radiation Pulse \& Mass Loss Rate}
During quiescent conditions, the XUV stellar flux at $a = 0.031\,\text{AU}$ is $F_{\text{XUV}} \approx 9.3 \times 10^4\,\text{erg}/(\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{s})$, driving baseline mass loss:
\begin{equation}
\dot{M}_{\text{quiescent}} = \frac{3 \epsilon F_{\text{XUV}} R_p^3}{4 G M_p K_{\text{tide}}} \approx 4.8 \times 10^{10}\,\text{g/s}
\end{equation}
During the X-ray superflare observed by Swift, the ionizing flux spiked nearly an order of magnitude to $F_{\text{XUV, flare}} \approx 8.7 \times 10^5\,\text{erg}/(\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{s})$, surging the hydrodynamic escape rate to:
\begin{equation}
\dot{M}_{\text{flare}} \approx 4.5 \times 10^{11}\,\text{g/s} \quad (450{,}000\,\text{metric tons per second})
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Hydrodynamic Expansion Lag Timescale}
The acoustic transit timescale for the heated thermospheric layer to expand across planetary scale $H \sim c_s^2 / g \approx 3000\,\text{km}$ ($c_s \approx 10\,\text{km/s}$) is $\tau_{\text{sound}} \sim 1 - 2\,\text{hours}$, causing the peak exospheric cloud to emerge $\approx 6 - 8\,\text{hours}$ post-flare.

\subsection*{1. Charge-Exchange \& Stellar Wind Interactions}
The expanding hydrogen cloud interacts via resonant charge exchange $\mathrm{H} + \mathrm{H}^+ \to \mathrm{H}^+ + \mathrm{H}_{\text{energetic}}$ with the stellar coronal wind ($\dot{M}_* \approx 10^{-14}\,M_\odot/\text{yr}$, $v_{\text{wind}} \approx 450\,\text{km/s}$), producing high-velocity neutral atoms (HNs) with blue-shifted Doppler velocities up to $-230\,\text{km/s}$.
