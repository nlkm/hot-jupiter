\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Great Canyon of Saturn's Icy Moon Tethys}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Saturn's icy moon \textbf{Tethys} is circled by an enormous rift valley named \textbf{Ithaca Chasma}. This colossal canyon stretches for \textbf{1,200 miles} ($2,000\,\text{km}$) across nearly three-quarters of the entire moon's circumference, plunging over \textbf{3 miles deep} ($5\,\text{km}$) and spanning $60\,\text{miles wide}$ ($100\,\text{km}$)!

\subsection*{Why Freezing Oceans Split Worlds}
Billions of years ago, Tethys possessed an interior liquid water ocean beneath its cold ice shell. As the moon lost heat to deep space, the ocean froze solid into water ice. Because water expands by $9\%$ upon freezing, the expanding ice generated immense internal pressure, tearing open Tethys's surface in a gigantic global scar!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Cryospheric Freezing Pressurization \& Rupture}

\subsection*{1. Volumetric Phase Expansion \& Overpressure}
The freezing of a liquid ocean shell of initial thickness $h_{\text{ocean}}$ into Ice Ih ($\Delta V/V \approx +0.09$) inside an elastic shell of thickness $h_{\text{ice}}$ generates an ocean overpressure $\Delta P_{\text{ocean}}$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta P_{\text{ocean}} = \frac{ \frac{\Delta V}{V} \frac{h_{\text{ocean}}}{R_{\text{body}}} }{ \frac{1}{K_{\text{water}}} + \frac{3 R_{\text{body}}}{4 \mu_{\text{ice}} h_{\text{ice}}} }
\end{equation}
For Tethys ($R = 531.1\,\text{km}$, $h_{\text{ice}} = 60\,\text{km}$, $\mu_{\text{ice}} = 3.2\,\text{GPa}$), freezing just $5\,\text{km}$ of ocean yields $\Delta P_{\text{ocean}} \approx 1.5 - 3.0\,\text{MPa}$.

\subsection*{2. Thin-Shell Surface Hoop Stress}
The resulting tensile hoop stress $\sigma_{\theta\theta}$ in the outer brittle ice lid is:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\theta\theta} = \Delta P_{\text{ocean}} \left( \frac{R_{\text{body}}}{2 h_{\text{ice}}} \right) \approx (2.0\,\text{MPa}) \left( \frac{531.1}{120} \right) \approx 8.8\,\text{MPa}
\end{equation}
This substantially exceeds the tensile fracture strength of cryogenic polycrystalline water ice ($\sigma_{\text{tensile}} \approx 2.0\,\text{MPa}$), initiating extensional normal faulting and graben subsidence.

\subsection*{1. Maxwell Viscoelastic Lithospheric Relaxation}
Under Maxwell rheology with temperature-dependent viscosity $\eta(T) = \eta_0 \exp[A (T_{\text{melt}}/T - 1)]$, the stress tensor evolves as:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma_{ij}}{dt} = 2\mu \dot{\epsilon}_{ij} - \frac{\sigma_{ij}}{\tau_M(T)}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_M(T) = \eta(T)/\mu$. For cold near-surface ice ($T \le 110\,\mathrm{K}$), $\tau_M > 10^8\,\mathrm{yr} \gg \tau_{\text{freeze}}$, preventing ductile stress relief and forcing brittle catastrophic failure.
