\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Fastest Winds in the Solar System}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located nearly 3 billion miles from the Sun on the ice giant \textbf{Neptune}, the **Great Dark Spot (GDS-89)** was an Earth-sized anticyclonic storm churning through the fastest atmospheric jet streams ever recorded in the Solar System.

\subsection*{Supersonic Winds and Methane Cirrus Clouds}
While Jupiter's storms crawl in comparison, Neptune's equator screams with **retrograde jet streams moving at nearly 900 to 1,000 mph (400 m/s)**! Perched at $22^\circ\,\text{S}$ latitude, the Great Dark Spot acts like an enormous atmospheric mountain. As howling winds hit the vortex, air is forced upward by 30 miles (50 km), condensing invisible methane gas into brilliant, bright white **methane ice cirrus companion clouds** that float above the dark indigo storm!

\subsection*{1. Quasi-Geostrophic Vortex Drift \& Beta-Plane Physics}
On a rapidly rotating planet with differential planetary vorticity $\beta = \frac{2\Omega\cos\phi}{R_N}$, an anticyclonic vortex ($q' > 0$) experiences an asymmetric Rossby-wave radiation torque inducing a poleward and prograde drift:
\begin{equation}
v_{\text{drift}} \approx \frac{\beta R_d^2}{2} \approx 15.0\,\text{m/s}
\end{equation}
where the Rossby deformation radius $R_d = \sqrt{g' H} / f \approx 2000\,\text{km}$.

\subsection*{2. Orographic Methane Cloud Condensation}
