\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Lava Lamp Heart of Pluto}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Spanning over 600 miles across Pluto's famous heart-shaped region lies \textbf{Sputnik Planitia}, a colossal glacier of solid nitrogen ice. High-resolution photos from NASA's *New Horizons* spacecraft revealed that the entire frozen sea is divided into giant \textbf{polygonal cells} 20 to 30 miles wide with elevated centers and shallow perimeter troughs, with zero impact craters!

\subsection*{How Solid Ice Boils Like Boiling Oatmeal}
Even though Pluto's surface is $-390^\circ\text{F}$ ($38\,\text{K}$), solid nitrogen ice is extraordinarily soft and plastic. Modest geothermal heat from Pluto's radioactive rocky core warms the bottom of the 4-mile-deep ice sheet. The warm, buoyant nitrogen ice slowly floats up in the center of each cell and sinks at the edges—acting like a gigantic, slow-motion planetary \textbf{lava lamp} that completely repaves Pluto's surface every 500,000 years!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Rayleigh-B\'enard Solid-State Convection}

\subsection*{1. The Rayleigh Number for Solid Nitrogen Ice}
Thermal buoyancy overcomes viscous resistance when the dimensionless Rayleigh number $Ra$ exceeds the critical value $Ra_c \approx 10^3$:
\begin{equation}
Ra = \frac{\rho g \alpha \Delta T H^3}{\kappa \eta}
\end{equation}
For Pluto's nitrogen ice sheet ($\rho = 1000\,\text{kg/m}^3$, $g = 0.62\,\text{m/s}^2$, $\alpha \approx 2 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{K}^{-1}$, $\Delta T \approx 5\,\text{K}$, $H = 6.0\,\text{km}$, thermal diffusivity $\kappa \approx 2 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}^2\text{s}^{-1}$, and effective solid-state viscosity $\eta \approx 10^{14}\,\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$):
\begin{equation}
Ra \approx \frac{(1000)(0.62)(2 \times 10^{-3})(5)(6000)^3}{(2 \times 10^{-7})(10^{14})} \approx 1.3 \times 10^7 \gg 10^3
\end{equation}
Confirming vigorous solid-state thermal convection.

\subsection*{2. Cell Wavelength \& Dynamic Topography}
The aspect ratio for high-$Ra$ convection cells is $\lambda_{\text{cell}} \approx 3 - 5\,H \approx 20 - 30\,\text{km}$.
The dynamic topographic elevation $\Delta h$ at the upwelling center is:
\begin{equation}
\Delta h \approx \frac{\alpha \Delta T H}{2} \approx \frac{(2 \times 10^{-3})(5\,\text{K})(6000\,\text{m})}{2} \approx 30 - 50\,\text{meters}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{1. Dislocation Creep \& Convective Overturning Rate}
Solid nitrogen ice ($\mathrm{N_2}$) deforms via dislocation power-law creep $\dot{\epsilon} = A \sigma^n \exp(-Q/RT)$ ($n \approx 3.0$). The vertical upwelling convective velocity $u_z$ is:
\begin{equation}
u_z \approx 0.15 \frac{\kappa}{H} Ra^{2/3} \approx 0.15 \left( \frac{2 \times 10^{-7}}{6000} \right) (10^7)^{2/3} \approx 2.3 \times 10^{-9}\,\text{m/s} \quad (7.0\,\text{cm/year})
\end{equation}
Yielding a convective turnover timescale of $\tau_{\text{overturn}} = H / u_z \approx 5 \times 10^5\,\text{years}$, fully explaining the total absence of impact craters.
