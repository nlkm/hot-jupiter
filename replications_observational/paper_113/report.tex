\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Alien Snowball from Deep Space}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Discovered in late 2019, \textbf{2I/Borisov} was only the second interstellar visitor ever confirmed to cross our Solar System from another star, screaming through on an unbound, hyperbolic trajectory at over 72,000 mph (32.2 km/s).

\subsection*{A Comet Built in Deep Freeze}
When astronomers aimed the powerful ALMA submillimeter radio telescope and the Hubble Space Telescope at Borisov, they discovered something extraordinary: the alien comet was spewing out **vastly more poisonous carbon monoxide (CO) gas than water vapor ($Q_{\text{CO}} / Q_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \approx 145\%$)**—nearly 10 to 20 times more CO than typical comets born in our Solar System! Because carbon monoxide ice only condenses at extreme deep-freeze temperatures below $-420^\circ\text{F}$ ($20\,\text{K}$), Borisov must have formed in the outer frigid reaches of an alien star system before being violently ejected into interstellar space!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Sublimation Thermodynamics \& Volatility}

\subsection*{1. Volatile Gas Production \& Energy Balance}
The steady-state surface energy balance governing cometary sublimation at heliocentric distance $r_h$ is:
\begin{equation}
\frac{F_\odot (1 - A)}{r_h^2} \cos\theta = \epsilon \sigma T^4 + L_{\text{sub}} Z(T)
\end{equation}
where the Hertz-Knudsen sublimation flux $Z(T) = P_{\text{sat}}(T) / \sqrt{2\pi m k_B T}$. For carbon monoxide, the latent heat of sublimation $L_{\text{CO}} \approx 3.0 \times 10^5\,\text{J/kg}$ is an order of magnitude lower than water ice ($L_{\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}} \approx 2.8 \times 10^6\,\text{J/kg}$), enabling intense CO outgassing at large heliocentric distances where water ice is completely inactive.

\subsection*{2. Hyperbolic Excess \& Non-Gravitational Dynamics}
With orbital eccentricity $e = 3.36 \pm 0.01$ and asymptotic interstellar velocity $v_\infty = 32.2\,\text{km/s}$, asymmetric outgassing rocket forces impart non-gravitational acceleration:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{a}_{\text{ng}} = A_1 g(r) \hat{\mathbf{r}} + A_2 g(r) \hat{\mathbf{t}}
\end{equation}
with radial coefficient $A_1 \approx (4.0 \pm 0.5) \times 10^{-8}\,\text{AU/day}^2$.

\subsection*{1. Protoplanetary Disk Frost Line \& Chemical Heritage}
The extreme CO enrichment ($>100\%$ relative to water) indicates that Borisov condensed beyond the CO snow line in a protoplanetary disk around a cool M-dwarf or in a cold giant molecular cloud core ($T \le 20\,\text{K}$).
