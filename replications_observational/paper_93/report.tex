\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Beating Icy Heart of Pluto}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
When NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew past \textbf{Pluto} in July 2015, it discovered a vast, brilliant white nitrogen glacier shaped like a giant heart, officially named \textbf{Sputnik Planitia}.

\subsection*{A Giant Simmering Lava Lamp of Nitrogen Ice}
To scientists' astonishment, Sputnik Planitia has zero impact craters: its surface is brand new and completely rejuvenated. The entire basin is divided into a cosmic jigsaw puzzle of \textbf{20-mile-wide (30 km) polygonal cells}. Like oatmeal simmering in a slow-cooker or a giant cosmic lava lamp, warm nitrogen ice rises up in the center of each cell at a speed of an inch per year, cools at the surface, and sinks back down along the dark border troughs every 500,000 years, constantly repaving the face of Pluto!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Solid-State Rayleigh-B\'enard Thermal Convection}

\subsection*{1. Critical Rayleigh Number in Volatile Nitrogen Ice}
Solid nitrogen ($\mathrm{N}_2$) ice at $T \sim 40\,\text{K}$ undergoes ductile dislocation creep with very low effective viscosity ($\eta \sim 10^{14}\,\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$). In a glacier layer of thickness $d \approx 6\,\text{km}$, the convective Rayleigh number is:
\begin{equation}
Ra = \frac{\rho g \alpha \Delta T d^3}{\kappa \eta} \approx \frac{(1000)(0.62)(2 \times 10^{-3})(5)(6000)^3}{(10^{-7})(10^{14})} \approx 1.3 \times 10^7
\end{equation}
Because $Ra \gg Ra_{\text{crit}} \approx 10^3$, vigorous Rayleigh-B\'enard convection is unavoidable.

\subsection*{2. Convective Aspect Ratio \& Overturning Timescale}
The cell aspect ratio $\lambda = D / d \approx 5$ produces polygonal diameters of $D \approx 30\,\text{km}$. The buoyant upwelling velocity is:
\begin{equation}
v_z \approx \frac{\kappa}{d} Ra^{2/3} \approx 1.2\,\text{cm/year}
\end{equation}
yielding a characteristic convective turnover timescale $\tau_{\text{overturn}} = d / v_z \approx 5.0 \times 10^5\,\text{years}$.

\subsection*{1. Dynamic Topography \& Crater Resurfacing Equilibrium}
