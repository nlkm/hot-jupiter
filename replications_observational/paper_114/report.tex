\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — An Earth-Sized Oasis Around a Red Sun}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 40 light-years away in the constellation Aquarius, the \textbf{TRAPPIST-1 system} contains seven Earth-sized rocky planets. Among them, \textbf{TRAPPIST-1e} is considered the crown jewel of exoplanet habitability—almost exactly the same size as Earth ($0.92\,R_\oplus$), receiving $66\%$ of the sunlight Earth receives, and situated right in the center of the circumstellar habitable zone where liquid water oceans can flourish!

\subsection*{Holding Onto Its Air and Oceans}
Because TRAPPIST-1 is an active red dwarf star, scientists worried that fierce stellar flares might have stripped away the planet's entire atmosphere. But observations with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) have constrained its day-to-night temperatures, revealing that TRAPPIST-1e is not a frozen bare rock, but likely maintains a **temperate, stable atmosphere ($P \sim 1\,\text{bar}$)** with global heat circulation that prevents its nightside from freezing into an icy desert!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Thermal Redistribution \& Radiative Equilibrium}

\subsection*{1. Planetary Energy Balance \& Circulation Efficiency}
For a tidally locked rocky planet with incident stellar flux $S = 0.662\,S_\oplus \approx 905\,\text{W/m}^2$ and Bond albedo $A_B \approx 0.20$, the global effective temperature is:
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{eq}} = \left( \frac{S (1 - A_B)}{4\sigma} \right)^{1/4} \approx 238\,\text{K}
\end{equation}
Dynamic atmospheric circulation parameterized by heat redistribution efficiency $\mathcal{E} \in [0, 1]$ moderates the dayside and nightside brightness temperatures:
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{day}} = T_{\text{eq}} \left( 1 + \frac{1 - \mathcal{E}}{2} \right)^{1/4}, \quad T_{\text{night}} = T_{\text{eq}} \left( \frac{\mathcal{E}}{2} \right)^{1/4}
\end{equation}
For an airless body ($\mathcal{E} = 0$), $T_{\text{night}} \to 0\,\text{K}$, whereas a $1\,\text{bar}$ $\mathrm{N}_2\text{--}\mathrm{CO}_2$ atmosphere ($\mathcal{E} \approx 0.70$) maintains $T_{\text{day}} \approx 245\,\text{K}$ and $T_{\text{night}} \approx 205\,\text{K}$.

\subsection*{2. Column Pressure \& Hydrodynamic Retention}
Given TRAPPIST-1e's surface gravity $g = G M_p / R_p^2 \approx 8.02\,\text{m/s}^2$, an atmospheric column of $P_{\text{surf}} = 1.0\,\text{bar}$ provides sufficient thermal inertia ($C_{\text{atm}} = c_p P_{\text{surf}} / g \approx 1.25 \times 10^7\,\text{J/m}^2\text{K}$) to resist atmospheric collapse.

\subsection*{1. Habitability Assessment \& Ocean Stability}
Climate simulations with $1\,\text{bar}$ background $\mathrm{N}_2$ and $\sim 100 - 1000\,\text{ppm}$ $\mathrm{CO}_2$ demonstrate stable global or dayside-centered liquid water oceans with equatorial surface temperatures $T_{\text{surf}} \approx 280 - 295\,\text{K}$.
