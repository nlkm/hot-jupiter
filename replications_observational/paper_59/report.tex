\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — Tasting the Alien Ocean of Enceladus}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Saturn's tiny icy moon \textbf{Enceladus} shoots towering geysers of water vapor and ice crystals hundreds of miles into space from giant fractures nicknamed the "Tiger Stripes". These cryovolcanic plumes feed Saturn's ethereal \textbf{E-ring}.

\subsection*{Why the Giant Geysers Taste Like Salt Water}
When NASA's *Cassini* spacecraft flew directly through the plumes and E-ring, its Cosmic Dust Analyzer tasted the ice grains and discovered table salt ($\mathrm{NaCl}$) and baking soda ($\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}$). Small grains condensing from pure vapor are pure ice, but larger grains ($r > 1\,\mu\mathrm{m}$) are flash-frozen spray droplets blasted directly from a warm, salty, alkaline liquid ocean in contact with a rocky seafloor!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Aerosol Droplet Spray \& Salt Partitioning}

\subsection*{1. Two-Component Plume Microphysics}
The cryovolcanic eruption produces two distinct grain populations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Type I (Salt-Poor, $r < 0.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$)}: Formed via homogeneous nucleation and condensation from supersaturated expanding water vapor, excluding non-volatile salts ($w_{\text{salt}} < 0.1\,\text{wt}\%$).
\item \textbf{Type III (Salt-Rich, $r > 1.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}$)}: Formed via mechanical bubble-bursting aerosolization of bulk ocean liquid water at the water-vacuum interface, retaining the pristine ocean salinity ($w_{\text{salt}} \approx 0.5 - 2.0\,\text{wt}\%$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{2. Gas Drag Acceleration in Supersonic Vent}
In the narrow vent nozzles, expanding gas accelerates aerosol droplets via hydrodynamic drag:
\begin{equation}
m_{\text{grain}} \frac{dv}{dz} = \frac{1}{2} C_D \pi r_{\text{grain}}^2 \rho_{\text{gas}}(z) (v_{\text{gas}} - v_{\text{grain}})^2 - m_{\text{grain}} g_{\text{Enceladus}}
\end{equation}
Because $m_{\text{grain}} \propto r^3$, large salt-rich grains achieve lower velocities ($v \approx 200 - 300\,\text{m/s}$) close to the escape velocity ($v_{\text{esc}} = 239\,\text{m/s}$), while light pure-ice grains exceed $v > 500\,\text{m/s}$.

\subsection*{1. Alkaline Hydrothermal Ocean Chemistry}
The high ratio of sodium carbonate/bicarbonate to chloride ($\mathrm{Na_2CO_3 / NaCl} \approx 0.2 - 0.5$) along with detected native silica nanoparticles ($\mathrm{SiO_2}$) requires serpentinization reactions at $T > 360\,\mathrm{K}$ and alkaline ocean $\mathrm{pH} \approx 9.0 - 10.5$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Mg_2SiO_4} + \mathrm{MgSiO_3} + 2\mathrm{H_2O} \to \mathrm{Mg_3Si_2O_5(OH)_4}
\end{equation}
