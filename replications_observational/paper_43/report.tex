\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Exploding Rubble-Pile Asteroid}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
When NASA's OSIRIS-REx spacecraft arrived at near-Earth asteroid \textbf{101955 Bennu} in 2019, it witnessed an active, living asteroid: Bennu was constantly shooting showers of gravel and pebbles straight into space!

\subsection*{Sunlight Popcorn Physics}
As Bennu rotates every 4.3 hours, its dark rocky surface heats up in the daytime and freezes in the shadow of night. This rapid temperature cycling causes boulders to crack and fracture like popcorn kernels popping in a hot pan! Because Bennu's gravity is so weak (barely $1/100,000\text{th}$ of Earth's), even a gentle crack launches pebbles into orbit or out into interplanetary space.

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Micro-Gravity Escape \& Thermal Fatigue Stress}

\subsection*{1. Micro-Gravity Orbital Mechanics on a Rubble Pile}
For an asteroid of mean radius $R = 245\,\text{m}$ and bulk density $\rho = 1190\,\text{kg/m}^3$, the surface gravity and escape velocity are exceptionally small:
\begin{equation}
g_{\text{surf}} = \frac{4}{3} \pi G \rho R \approx 8.1 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{m/s}^2, \quad v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2 g_{\text{surf}} R} \approx 0.20\,\text{m/s} \quad (0.45\,\text{mph})
\end{equation}
Any pebble ejected faster than $0.20\,\text{m/s}$ escapes Bennu's gravitational well completely.

\subsection*{2. Diurnal Thermal Strain Energy \& Micro-Explosions}
Diurnal temperature swings ($\Delta T \approx 100\,\text{K}$) create differential thermal expansion stresses across mineral grain boundaries:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\text{thermal}} = \frac{E \alpha \Delta T}{1 - \nu} \approx 1.2 \times 10^5\,\text{Pa} \quad (1.2\,\text{bar})
\end{equation}
When the thermal stress exceeds the tensile strength of porous carbonaceous chondrite boulders ($\sigma_{\text{tensile}} \approx 10^5\,\text{Pa}$), sudden macroscopic spallation releases stored elastic strain energy, ejecting pebbles at speeds of $v \sim 0.1 - 3.0\,\text{m/s}$.

\subsection*{1. Elastic Energy Partitioning in Brittle Fracture}
The kinetic energy imparted to a spalled fragment of mass $m_p = \frac{4}{3}\pi \rho_p r_p^3$ upon brittle fracture propagation is:
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{2} m_p v_{\text{ej}}^2 = \eta_{\text{kin}} \left( \frac{\sigma_{\text{crit}}^2}{2 E} V_{\text{strain}} - 2 \gamma_{\text{surface}} A_{\text{fracture}} \right)
\end{equation}
Where $\eta_{\text{kin}} \approx 0.05$ is the kinetic energy conversion efficiency and $E \approx 5\,\text{GPa}$ is the Young's modulus of CM carbonaceous chondrites, generating the observed velocity distribution $dN/dv \propto v^{1.5} e^{-v/v_0}$.
