\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Evaporating Giant with a Cometary Tail}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Orbiting a sun-like star 150 light-years away in the constellation Pegasus, \textbf{HD 209458b} (nicknamed ``Osiris'') was the very first exoplanet ever discovered transiting in front of its star. It is a scorching gas giant orbiting just \textbf{4.4 million miles} from its star—eight times closer than Mercury is to the Sun!

\subsection*{A Giant Planet Boiling into Space}
Under the fierce onslaught of high-energy ultraviolet radiation from its star, HD 209458b's upper atmosphere heats to thousands of degrees and boils off into space. When the Hubble Space Telescope watched the planet pass in front of its star in ultraviolet light, it saw a colossal, glowing cloud of hydrogen gas spanning several times the size of Jupiter, trailing behind the planet like a gigantic \textbf{cometary tail}!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Energy-Limited Photoevaporation \& Parker Wind Escape}

\subsection*{1. Energy-Limited Mass Loss Rate}
Extreme ultraviolet (XUV) stellar photons ionize and heat the upper thermosphere. Balancing incoming stellar radiative power with gravitational potential energy yields the energy-limited mass loss rate:
\begin{equation}
\dot{M} = \frac{3 \epsilon F_{\text{XUV}} R_p^3}{4 G M_p K_{\text{tide}}}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon \approx 0.15$ is the heating efficiency and $K_{\text{tide}} \approx 1 - \frac{3}{2\xi} + \frac{1}{2\xi^3} \approx 0.85$ ($\xi = R_{\text{Roche}}/R_p$) accounts for Roche lobe overflow.
For HD 209458b ($M_p = 0.69\,M_{\text{Jup}}$, $R_p = 1.38\,R_{\text{Jup}}$, $F_{\text{XUV}} \approx 3.4 \times 10^4\,\text{erg}/(\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{s})$):
\begin{equation}
\dot{M} \approx 5.0 \times 10^{10}\,\text{g/s} \quad (50{,}000\,\text{metric tons per second})
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Exospheric Expansion \& Lyman-$\alpha$ Optical Depth}
The escaping neutral hydrogen expands to several planetary radii ($R_{\text{exo}} \approx 3 - 4\,R_p$), where the resonant scattering cross-section at Lyman-$\alpha$ ($\lambda_0 = 1215.67\,\text{\AA}$) produces optical depth $\tau_0 \gg 1$, yielding a deep $15.0\%$ ultraviolet transit compared to the $1.5\%$ optical continuum disk transit.

\subsection*{1. Stellar Radiation Pressure Acceleration \& Cometary Tail}
Escaping H I atoms experience repulsive stellar Ly-$\alpha$ radiation pressure acceleration $a_{\text{rad}} = \frac{F_{\text{Ly}\alpha} \sigma_{\text{eff}}}{m_{\text{H}} c} \approx (2 - 4) g_*$, accelerating neutral atoms away from the star at high blue-shifted velocities ($-130\,\text{km/s}$ to $-50\,\text{km/s}$) and creating the extended post-transit absorption tail.
