\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Giant Shiny Mirror World}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 260 light-years away in the constellation Sculptor, \textbf{LTT 9779b} is an ultra-hot, Neptune-sized world that orbits its star in just \textbf{19 hours}. Under normal circumstances, planets this close are so brutally scorched that their clouds evaporate, turning them into pitch-black balls of charcoal that absorb nearly all starlight.

\subsection*{The First Metallic Mirror Exoplanet}
When Europe's CHEOPS space telescope looked at LTT 9779b during secondary eclipse, it found something astonishing: the planet reflects an incredible \textbf{80\% of incoming starlight} (matching Venus as the shiniest planet known in the universe). The planet is so blisteringly hot ($3{,}600^\circ\text{F}$) that its atmosphere became super-saturated with vaporized titanium and glass, forming gleaming, high-altitude metallic silicate clouds that act as a celestial planetary mirror!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Geometric Albedo Inversion \& Secondary Eclipse Photometry}

\subsection*{1. Reflected Starlight Eclipse Depth}
The optical occultation depth $\delta_{\text{occ}}$ measured by CHEOPS comprises reflected starlight and thermal emission:
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\text{occ}} = A_g \left(\frac{R_p}{a}\right)^2 + \frac{B_\lambda(T_{\text{day}})}{I_{*,\lambda}} \left(\frac{R_p}{R_*}\right)^2
\end{equation}
For LTT 9779b ($R_p = 4.72\,R_\oplus$, $a = 0.01679\,\text{AU}$, $R_* = 0.949\,R_\odot$), the CHEOPS bandpass ($\lambda \approx 0.33 - 1.1\,\mu\text{m}$) is dominated by reflected light, where the $225 \pm 20\,\text{ppm}$ eclipse depth requires a record-breaking optical geometric albedo:
\begin{equation}
A_g = 0.80 \pm 0.05
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Super-Saturation \& Cloud Condensation Physics}
At $T_{\text{eq}} \approx 2000\,\text{K}$, silicate and titanate gases ($\mathrm{SiO}_x$, $\mathrm{TiO}_x$, $\mathrm{CaTiO}_3$) exceed their condensation saturation vapor pressure at microbar pressures, precipitating high-reflectivity dielectric condensate layers that cool the dayside from $T_{\text{dark}} \approx 2800\,\text{K}$ down to $T_{\text{day}} \approx 2300\,\text{K}$.

\subsection*{1. Survivability in the Neptune Desert}
LTT 9779b is one of the rare survivors residing inside the extreme ``Neptune Desert''. High-albedo cloud reflection prevents runaway atmospheric thermal stripping, reducing the mass loss rate to $\dot{M} \approx 1.8 \times 10^{10}\,\text{g/s}$, enabling a multi-Gyr planetary lifespan.
