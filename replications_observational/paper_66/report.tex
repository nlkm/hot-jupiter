\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Vanishing Superstorm of Neptune}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
When NASA's *Voyager 2* spacecraft flew past the ice giant Neptune in 1989, it captured stunning images of the \textbf{Great Dark Spot} (GDS-89)—a titanic Earth-sized storm spinning backward through an ocean of deep azure methane clouds surrounded by brilliant white companion cirrus clouds!

\subsection*{How Planetary Spinning Currents Steer and Destroy Dark Storms}
Neptune boasts the fastest jet streams in the solar system ($1{,}000\,\text{mph}$ / $400\,\text{m/s}$). Because Neptune is a rotating sphere, the Coriolis force changes with latitude, pushing the anticyclone slowly toward the equator in a process called \textbf{beta-drift} ($35\,\text{mph}$). Once the storm reaches the equator, the extreme opposing wind shears rip the vortex apart, causing it to completely vanish before new ones form years later!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Potential Vorticity \& Beta-Drift}

\subsection*{1. Quasi-Geostrophic Potential Vorticity Conservation}
The shallow-water potential vorticity $q$ for an anticyclone of relative vorticity $\zeta = \nabla \times \mathbf{u} < 0$ on a $\beta$-plane is:
\begin{equation}
q = \frac{\zeta + f_0 + \beta y}{H_0 + h} = \text{constant}
\end{equation}
where $f = 2\Omega_N \sin\phi$, and $\beta = \frac{df}{dy} = \frac{2\Omega_N \cos\phi}{R_N} \approx 6.4 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{m}^{-1}\text{s}^{-1}$.

\subsection*{2. Equatorward $\beta$-Gyre Migration Velocity}
Advection of ambient planetary vorticity by the vortex circulation generates secondary dipolar $\beta$-gyres that induce a net equatorward and westward drift velocity $c_\beta$:
\begin{equation}
c_\beta = -\beta L_D^2 \approx -(6.4 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{m}^{-1}\text{s}^{-1}) (1.5 \times 10^6\,\text{m})^2 \approx 15\,\text{m/s} \quad (1.3\,\text{km/day})
\end{equation}
where $L_D = \frac{\sqrt{g' H}}{f} \approx 1500\,\text{km}$ is the first baroclinic Rossby deformation radius.

\subsection*{1. Vortex-Jet Interaction \& Shear Disruption}
As the vortex approaches the equator ($\phi \to 0^\circ$), the planetary background shear $\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}$ increases dramatically. The vortex becomes unstable to shearing disruption when the Okubo-Weiss parameter $W = S_n^2 + S_s^2 - \omega^2 > 0$:
\begin{equation}
\left| \frac{\partial \bar{u}}{\partial y} \right| \ge \frac{|\zeta_{\text{vortex}}|}{2} \approx 5 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{equation}
shredding the anticyclonic core into filaments within several orbital turnover periods.
