\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Spinning Rugby Ball with a Ring}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located in the frozen outer fringes of our solar system, \textbf{Haumea} is one of the strangest dwarf planets known. Instead of being round like a marble, it is stretched out like a giant \textbf{cosmic rugby ball} over $1{,}400\,\text{miles}$ long!

\subsection*{A Record-Breaking Spin and a Hidden Ring}
Haumea spins dizzyingly fast—completing a full day in just \textbf{3.9 hours}, faster than any other large body in the solar system. This extreme rotation flattens and stretches it into an ellipsoid. In 2017, when Haumea passed in front of a distant star, astronomers discovered something unprecedented: Haumea is encircled by a \textbf{dense, razor-thin icy ring} orbiting in harmony with the planet's rapid spin!

\subsection*{1. Triaxial Jacobi Ellipsoid Equilibrium}
A self-gravitating fluid body of uniform density $\rho$ and angular velocity $\Omega$ settles into a triaxial Jacobi ellipsoid with semi-axes $a > b > c$ satisfying:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Omega^2}{\pi G \rho} = 2 a b c \int_0^\infty \frac{u \, du}{(a^2 + u)(b^2 + u)\Delta(u)}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta(u) = \sqrt{(a^2 + u)(b^2 + u)(c^2 + u)}$.

\begin{equation}

\end{equation}
