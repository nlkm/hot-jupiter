\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Impossible Ring Defying Physics}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 4 billion miles away in the icy Kuiper Belt, \textbf{(50000) Quaoar} is a frigid dwarf planet about half the size of Pluto, orbited by its small moon Weywot.

\subsection*{Breaking the Golden Rule of Planetary Rings}
For over 170 years, classical astrophysics taught that planetary rings could only survive close to a planet—inside the ``Roche limit''—where tidal gravity tears moons apart. Outside this boundary, ring particles must quickly clump together into a new moon. But in 2023, the European CHEOPS space telescope and ground observatories discovered that Quaoar has a dense, clumpy ring located \textbf{more than double the distance of its Roche limit} ($7.4$ planet radii away)! Resonances and elastic collisions between icy pebbles prevent them from sticking, keeping the impossible ring alive!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Fluid Roche Limits \& Resonant Confinement}

\subsection*{1. Classical Fluid Roche Limit}
For a self-gravitating fluid satellite orbiting a primary of radius $R_Q = 555\,\text{km}$ and bulk density $\rho_Q \approx 2000\,\text{kg/m}^3$:
\begin{equation}
a_{\text{Roche}} = 2.456 R_Q \left(\frac{\rho_Q}{\rho_{\text{ring}}}\right)^{1/3} \approx 1{,}720\,\text{km} \quad (3.1\,R_Q)
\end{equation}
The discovered primary ring Q1R sits at $r = 4{,}100\,\text{km}$ ($7.4\,R_Q$), representing a Roche limit ratio of $r / a_{\text{Roche}} \approx 2.39$.

\subsection*{2. 6:1 Spin-Orbit Resonance Confinement}
Quaoar's non-axisymmetric topography ($C_{22}$) and spin period ($P_{\text{rot}} = 8.84\,\text{hr}$) generate strong Lindblad resonant torques at the $6:1$ spin-orbit resonance radius:
\begin{equation}
r_{6:1} = \left(\frac{G M_Q (6 P_{\text{rot}})^2}{4\pi^2}\right)^{1/3} \approx 4{,}198\,\text{km}
\end{equation}
which injects velocity dispersion into icy collisions, preventing runaway gravitational accretion into a moonlet.
