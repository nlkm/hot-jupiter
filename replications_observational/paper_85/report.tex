\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — A Planet Hotter than Most Stars}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 670 light-years away in the constellation Cygnus, \textbf{KELT-9b} is the undisputed heavyweight champion of extreme worlds: an ultra-hot gas giant orbiting a blistering blue-white star so closely that its dayside temperature exceeds \textbf{7,800$^\circ$F (4,600 K)}—making it hotter than the surfaces of most stars in our galaxy!

\subsection*{The Glowing Atomic Metal Sky}
At these unimaginable temperatures, water, methane, and carbon dioxide molecules cannot exist; they are ripped apart into raw atoms. The planet's upper atmosphere swells into a glowing, 18,000$^\circ$F ($10{,}000\,\text{K}$) thermosphere where vaporized atomic iron, titanium, and hydrogen glow intensely, creating a gigantic halo that blocks extra starlight during transit.

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Thermospheric Scale Height \& Balmer Line Radiative Transfer}

\subsection*{1. Enormous Thermospheric Scale Height}
In the ultra-hot atomic hydrogen thermosphere where molecular hydrogen is fully dissociated ($\mu \approx 0.5\,\text{amu}$) and $T_{\text{therm}} \approx 10{,}000\,\text{K}$, the atmospheric pressure scale height reaches extreme proportions:
\begin{equation}
H = \frac{k_B T_{\text{therm}}}{\mu m_u g_p} \approx \frac{(1.38 \times 10^{-23})(10^4)}{(0.5)(1.66 \times 10^{-27})(20.0)} \approx 8{,}314\,\text{km}
\end{equation}
For comparison, Jupiter's scale height is only $\approx 27\,\text{km}$. This colossal scale height extends the effective planetary radius at the Balmer H$\alpha$ line ($\lambda = 6562.8\,\text{\AA}$) to $R_{\text{eff}} \approx 1.32\,R_p$.

\subsection*{2. Excited $n=2$ Hydrogen Population \& Line Center Absorption}
High stellar near-UV and Lyman-$\alpha$ flux populates the excited $n=2$ level of neutral hydrogen via photoexcitation and recombination, generating excess transit depth:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \delta_{\text{H}\alpha} = \frac{R_{\text{eff}}^2 - R_p^2}{R_*^2} \approx 1.15\,\%
\end{equation}
with Doppler line broadening corresponding to thermal velocity $v_{\text{th}} = \sqrt{2 k_B T / m_H} \approx 12.8\,\text{km/s}$.
