\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Rugby Ball World Bleeding Heavy Metals}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 850 light-years away in the constellation Puppis, \textbf{WASP-121b} is a monstrous, blisteringly hot gas giant orbiting its host star every 30 hours at a distance of just 2.4 million miles.

\subsection*{Stretched by Gravity to the Breaking Point}
The host star's crushing gravitational pull stretches WASP-121b into the oblong shape of a \textbf{rugby ball} ($8\%$ elongation). Even more astonishingly, the planet is so bloated that its upper atmosphere fills \textbf{92\% of its gravitational boundary (the Roche lobe)}. At scorching $4{,}500^\circ\text{F}$ temperatures, heavy metals like iron and magnesium cannot condense into clouds—they vaporize, boil into space at \textbf{100,000 metric tons per second}, and bleed across the solar system in a glowing metallic river!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Prolate Tidal Deformation \& Roche Lobe Overflow}

\subsection*{1. Prolate Tidal Distortion Equilibrium}
In the Roche potential of the synchronous close-in binary system, tidal deformation elongates the planetary sub-stellar and anti-stellar axes:
\begin{equation}
\frac{R_{\text{prolate}}}{R_p} \approx 1 + \frac{h_2}{2} \left(\frac{M_*}{M_p}\right) \left(\frac{R_p}{a}\right)^3 \approx 1.08
\end{equation}
The mean planetary radius $R_p \approx 1.865\,R_{\text{Jup}}$ fills $\approx 92\%$ of the critical Roche equipotential surface $R_L \approx a \frac{0.49 q^{2/3}}{0.6 q^{2/3} + \ln(1 + q^{1/3})}$.

\subsection*{2. Heavy Metal Unbinding \& NUV Opacity}
At $T_{\text{therm}} > 3000\,\text{K}$, iron and magnesium remain in the gas phase as singly ionized species ($\mathrm{Fe~II}$, $\mathrm{Mg~II}$). Hydrodynamic expansion sweeps these heavy species beyond the Roche radius:
\begin{equation}
\dot{M}_{\text{metals}} \approx 1.0 \times 10^{11}\,\text{g/s} \quad (100{,}000\,\text{metric tons per second})
\end{equation}
creating intense resonant absorption in the near-UV ($\Delta\delta \approx 0.85\%$).
