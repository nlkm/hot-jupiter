\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The 300-Mile Deep Monster Storm}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Spanning 10,000 miles (16,000 km) across—large enough to swallow Earth whole with room to spare—Jupiter's **Great Red Spot** is the most famous and longest-lived storm in the Solar System, having raged continuously for at least 350 years.

\subsection*{How Deep Does the Red Spot Go?}
For centuries, scientists wondered if the Great Red Spot was just a thin, shallow layer of clouds or a deep-seated atmospheric engine. When NASA's Juno spacecraft swooped just thousands of miles above the storm, its microwave radiometer peered beneath the opaque ammonia clouds. Juno discovered that the storm's howling \textbf{270 mph (120 m/s) winds plunge 180 to 300 miles (300 to 500 km) deep} into Jupiter's interior, well below the water condensation level into crushing pressures 100 times greater than Earth's surface!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Thermal Wind Balance \& Geostrophic Vorticity}

\subsection*{1. Thermal Wind Equation in Jovian Atmospheres}
In a rapidly rotating, stratified giant planet atmosphere ($f = 2\Omega \sin\phi \approx 3.5 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{s}^{-1}$), geostrophic balance couples vertical shear in tangential wind speed $v_\theta$ to horizontal density and temperature gradients:
\begin{equation}
2\Omega \frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial z} = -\frac{g}{\rho T} \frac{\partial T}{\partial r}
\end{equation}
The maximum tangential collar velocity $v_{\max} \approx 120.0\,\text{m/s}$ at radius $r \approx 6{,}000\,\text{km}$ from the storm center requires a warm, low-density anticyclonic core in the upper troposphere transitioning to a cold anomaly below the water cloud base ($P \sim 5 - 10\,\text{bar}$).

\subsection*{2. Gravity Anomaly \& Penetration Depth}
The mass defect of the Great Red Spot induces a measurable local gravity anomaly $\Delta g \sim 10 - 15\,\text{mGal}$ detected by Juno X-band Doppler tracking, constraining the vertical depth of the vortex to $z_{\text{root}} = 300 \pm 100\,\text{km}$.
