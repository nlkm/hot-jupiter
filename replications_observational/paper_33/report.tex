\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Stripped Giant Planet Core}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
What happens when a gas giant planet as massive as Saturn gets stripped down to its bare bones? In 2020, NASA's TESS planet-hunter telescope found the answer: \textbf{TOI-849b}, an alien world orbiting deep inside the mysterious \textbf{"Neptunian Desert"}—a forbidden zone close to stars where Neptune-sized planets are almost never found!

\subsection*{The Naked Core}
TOI-849b is roughly 40 times more massive than Earth, but only 3.4 times wider. That means it has the same dense, rocky-iron composition as Earth, but scaled up to giant proportions. It is the exposed remnant core of a giant planet whose entire thick atmosphere of hydrogen and helium gas was blasted away by scorching starlight or stripped by giant collisions!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Hydrodynamic Escape \& Mass-Radius Scaling}

\subsection*{1. Energy-Limited Atmospheric Photoevaporation}
The maximum hydrodynamic mass loss rate driven by stellar XUV irradiation $F_{\text{XUV}}$ at semi-major axis $a$ is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{M}_{\text{loss}} \approx \frac{\epsilon \pi R_{\text{XUV}}^3 F_{\text{XUV}}}{G M_p}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon \approx 0.10 - 0.15$ is the photoevaporation efficiency factor. Over a $5\,\text{Gyr}$ lifetime in an ultra-short 18-hour orbit ($a = 0.016\,\text{AU}$), cumulative photoevaporation can strip up to several Earth masses of gas, peeling away primordial envelopes.

\subsection*{2. Polytropic Equation of State for Solid Remnants}
For a degenerate or compressed rocky core governed by the Birch-Murnaghan equation of state, mass-radius scaling follows:
\begin{equation}
R_p \propto M_p^{0.27}
\end{equation}
For $M_p = 39.1\,M_\oplus$, a pure rocky Earth-like core yields $R_p \approx 2.74\,R_\oplus$. The observed radius $R_p = 3.44\,R_\oplus$ constrains the residual volatile envelope to at most $f_{\text{env}} \le 3.8\%$ by mass.

\subsection*{1. 1D Hydrostatic Core-Envelope Structure Equations}
We integrate the coupled differential equations of planetary structure:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dm}{dr} = 4\pi r^2 \rho, \quad \frac{dP}{dr} = -\frac{G m(r) \rho(r)}{r^2}, \quad \frac{dT}{dr} = \frac{T}{P} \frac{dP}{dr} \nabla_{\text{ad}}
\end{equation}
using the CMS19 non-ideal EOS for H/He and Vinet/Birch-Murnaghan EOS for iron-silicate cores. Inversion demonstrates that TOI-849b cannot be a sub-Neptune that failed to accrete gas; rather, it successfully grew a massive core $> 30\,M_\oplus$ that underwent envelope stripping via tidal disruption or photo-evaporative blowout.
