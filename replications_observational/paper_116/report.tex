\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Asteroid That Fires Pebbles Into Space}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
When NASA's **OSIRIS-REx** spacecraft arrived at the near-Earth carbonaceous asteroid **(101955) Bennu** (1,600 feet / 490 meters across), scientists expected a silent, static pile of rubble. Instead, they were stunned to discover that Bennu is an **active asteroid** repeatedly firing showers of pebbles straight into space!

\subsection*{Exploding Rocks in Microgravity}
In Bennu's ultra-weak gravity (less than $1/100,000\text{th}$ of Earth's gravity), even a gentle kick will fling a rock into orbit. As Bennu spins every 4.3 hours, rapid heating and cooling crack surface rocks through **thermal fracturing stress ($\sigma \sim 1.2 \times 10^5\,\text{Pa}$)**, like popping popcorn. These bursts launch marble-sized gravel ($1.5\,\text{cm}$ across) at walking speeds ($1\,\text{mph / }0.5\,\text{m/s}$)—fast enough to escape Bennu's gravity entirely or hop in giant arcs across the asteroid!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Microgravity Ballistics \& Thermal Fracturing}

\subsection*{1. Microgravity Ballistic Dynamics}
In Bennu's surface gravity field ($g_{\text{eff}} \approx 6.0 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{m/s}^2$) with escape velocity $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2GM/R} \approx 0.20\,\text{m/s}$, particle launch velocity $v_0$ determines trajectory fate:
\begin{equation}
z_{\max} = \frac{v_0^2}{2 g_{\text{eff}}}, \quad t_{\text{flight}} = \frac{2 v_0}{g_{\text{eff}}}
\end{equation}
For sub-orbital hops ($v_0 = 0.15\,\text{m/s}$), particles reach $z_{\max} \approx 187.5\,\text{m}$ with flight time $t_{\text{flight}} \approx 5000\,\text{s}$ ($1.39\,\text{hours}$). For $v_0 = 0.50\,\text{m/s} > v_{\text{esc}}$, particles escape permanently into heliocentric orbit.

\subsection*{2. Thermal Fatigue Stress Criterion}
Diurnal temperature swings ($\Delta T \approx 100\,\text{K}$ over $P_{\text{rot}} = 4.297\,\text{hours}$) generate thermoelastic stresses:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\text{th}} = \frac{E \alpha \Delta T}{1 - \nu} \approx 1.2 \times 10^5\,\text{Pa}
\end{equation}
exceeding the tensile strength of hydrated carbonaceous chondrite boulders and triggering explosive exfoliation.
