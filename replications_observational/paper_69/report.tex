\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Stripped Heart of a Giant Planet}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
In the harsh, scorched zone known as the \textbf{Neptunian Desert} where intermediate-sized planets rarely survive, NASA's *TESS* mission and the ESO *HARPS* spectrograph discovered an extraordinary anomaly: \textbf{TOI-849b}.

\subsection*{The Exposed Fossil Core of a Jupiter-Sized Monster}
TOI-849b is roughly the size of Neptune ($3.44\,R_\oplus$), but weighs an astonishing \textbf{40 times more than Earth} ($39.1\,M_\oplus$)—making it twice as massive as any other Neptune-sized planet known! This immense density ($5.5\,\text{g/cm}^3$, identical to solid Earth rock and iron) means TOI-849b is the naked remnant fossil core of a massive gas giant whose titanic hydrogen-helium envelope was completely stripped away by tidal collisions or extreme photoevaporation!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Bulk Density \& Envelope Fraction Limits}

\subsection*{1. Bulk Density \& Core-Envelope Partitioning}
With mass $M_p = 39.1\,M_\oplus$ and transit radius $R_p = 3.44\,R_\oplus$:
\begin{equation}
\bar{\rho} = \frac{3 M_p}{4\pi R_p^3} \approx 5.50\,\text{g/cm}^3
\end{equation}
For comparison, Neptune has $\bar{\rho} \approx 1.64\,\text{g/cm}^3$, and Saturn has $\bar{\rho} \approx 0.69\,\text{g/cm}^3$.

\subsection*{2. Gaseous Envelope Mass Fraction Upper Limit}
Using 1D interior structure equations with an Earth-composition rocky core ($3.10\,R_\oplus$) surrounded by an adiabatic $\text{H/He}$ envelope:
\begin{equation}
f_{\text{env}} = \frac{M_{\text{env}}}{M_p} \le 0.038 \quad (3.8\%)
\end{equation}
A standard Jupiter-like runaway gas accretion scenario requires $f_{\text{env}} > 0.50$ once core mass exceeds $M_{\text{crit}} \approx 10 - 20\,M_\oplus$, proving that TOI-849b either suffered catastrophic post-formation giant impacts or extreme hydrodynamic photoevaporative stripping ($\dot{M} \approx 4.2 \times 10^{10}\,\text{g/s}$).

\subsection*{1. Critical Core Mass \& Accretion Gap Inversion}
Under standard core-nucleated accretion, a $39.1\,M_\oplus$ core in a protoplanetary disk enters runaway accretion:
\begin{equation}
\dot{M}_{\text{gas}} \approx \frac{M_p}{\tau_{\text{KH}}}, \quad \tau_{\text{KH}} = 10^8 \left( \frac{M_p}{M_\oplus} \right)^{-2.5} \kappa_{\text{dust}} \ll 10^5\,\text{yr}
\end{equation}
The survival of a nearly bare core requires disk clearing concurrent with core completion or giant resonant collisions during orbital migration.
