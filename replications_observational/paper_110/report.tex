\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — Tasting the Alien Ocean}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Saturn's tiny icy moon \textbf{Enceladus} (313 miles / 504 km across) is famous for the giant geysers erupting out of ``tiger stripe'' fractures at its south pole, spraying icy crystals hundreds of miles into space to build Saturn's E ring.

\subsection*{A Salty Soda Ocean with Hydrothermal Vents}
When NASA's Cassini spacecraft flew straight through the geyser plumes, its Cosmic Dust Analyzer (CDA) directly ``tasted'' the erupting ice grains. Cassini discovered that the ice particles are packed with **table salt ($\mathrm{NaCl}$) and baking soda ($\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{CO}_3, \mathrm{NaHCO}_3$)**, containing about $1.5\%$ salt mass fraction—very similar to Earth's oceans! The alkaline chemistry ($\mathrm{pH} \approx 9.5$) and discovery of dissolved molecular hydrogen prove that hot hydrothermal vents are actively bubbling on Enceladus's ocean floor, creating a prime habitat for extraterrestrial life!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Droplet Freezing \& Mass Spectrometry Physics}

\subsection*{1. Liquid Spray Atomization \& Salt Fractionation}
During volcanic vent ascent, violent degassing of volatile $\mathrm{CO}_2, \mathrm{CH}_4,$ and $\mathrm{H}_2$ atomizes subsurface brine into micro-droplets ($r_d \approx 1 - 5\,\mu\text{m}$). Slow freezing retains the native ocean salinity:
\begin{equation}
w_{\text{salt}} = \frac{m_{\text{NaCl}} + m_{\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3}}{m_{\text{total}}} \approx 0.015 \quad (1.5\,\text{wt}\%)
\end{equation}
Faster vapor condensation in vents forms salt-free pure ice grains (Type I/II), producing a distinct bimodal grain population.

\subsection*{2. Impact Ionization Time-of-Flight Mass Spectrometry}
Upon high-velocity impact ($v_{\text{imp}} \approx 6 - 18\,\text{km/s}$) onto the CDA rhodium target, ice grains vaporize and ionize. The resulting cation time-of-flight accelerates species toward the multiplier with arrival time:
\begin{equation}
t_{\text{TOF}} = L \sqrt{\frac{m}{2 q U_{\text{acc}}}} \implies m/z \propto t_{\text{TOF}}^2
\end{equation}
resolving diagnostic alkali peaks ($\mathrm{Na}^+ = 23\,\text{Da}, \mathrm{K}^+ = 39\,\text{Da}, \mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{OH}^+ = 63\,\text{Da}, \mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{Cl}^+ = 81\,\text{Da}$).

\subsection*{1. Serpentinization Geochemistry \& Methanogenesis Potential}
Cassini INMS detection of native $\mathrm{H}_2$ ($0.4 - 1.4\%$) combined with CDA alkaline carbonate salts constrains olivine serpentinization ($\mathrm{Mg}_2\mathrm{SiO}_4 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \to \text{serpentine} + \text{brucite} + \mathrm{H}_2$) and provides chemical chemical free energy ($\Delta G \approx -100\,\text{kJ/mol}$) sufficient for methanogenic microbial metabolism.
