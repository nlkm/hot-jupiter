\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Doomed Planet Spiraling into Its Star}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 1,400 light-years away in the constellation Auriga, \textbf{WASP-12b} is one of the most extreme exoplanets ever discovered: an ultra-hot gas giant larger than Jupiter orbiting so ridiculously close to its host star that a single year lasts just \textbf{26 hours}!

\subsection*{The First Planet Caught in a Gravitational Death Spiral}
The star's overwhelming gravity raises titanic tidal bulges that stretch WASP-12b into an egg-like teardrop shape while ripping its upper atmosphere away. Even more dramatically, friction in the star's tidal waves is steadily sapping energy from the planet's orbit. Over 15 years of precision telescope measurements from Hubble, Spitzer, and TESS, astronomers proved that the planet's year is shrinking by \textbf{29 milliseconds per year}—it is in a terminal death spiral and will crash into its star in just a few million years!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Equilibrium Tide Orbital Decay \& Parabolic O-C Ephemeris}

\subsection*{1. Stellar Tidal Torque \& Period Shrinkage}
Because the planet's orbital mean motion $n = 2\pi/P$ exceeds the stellar rotation rate $\Omega_*$, the tidal bulge raised on the star lags behind the planet, exerting a decelerating secular torque:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dot{P}}{P} = -\frac{9}{2} \frac{k_{2,*}}{Q'_*} \left(\frac{M_p}{M_*}\right) \left(\frac{R_*}{a}\right)^5 n
\end{equation}
For WASP-12b ($M_p = 1.46\,M_{\text{Jup}}$, $M_* = 1.36\,M_\odot$, $R_* = 1.59\,R_\odot$, $a = 0.0229\,\text{AU}$), the measured decay rate $\dot{P} = -29.27\,\text{ms/year}$ constrains the modified stellar tidal quality factor to $Q'_* = (1.8 \pm 0.3) \times 10^5$.

\subsection*{2. Quadratic Observed-Minus-Calculated ($O-C$) Timing Shift}
Expanding the transit mid-time $T(N)$ as a function of epoch number $N$:
\begin{equation}
T(N) = T_0 + P_0 N + \frac{1}{2} P_0 \left(\frac{dP}{dN}\right) N^2 \implies (O - C) = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{\dot{P}}{N_{\text{yr}}}\right) N^2
\end{equation}
After 15 years ($N \approx 5000\,\text{epochs}$), the cumulative timing advance reaches $\Delta T \approx -6.0\,\text{minutes}$, clearly distinguishing tidal decay from apsidal precession.

\subsection*{1. Internal Stellar Dissipation \& Remaining Orbital Lifetime}
Integrating the secular orbital evolution equation yields the remaining lifetime before reaching the Roche limit:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\text{remain}} = \frac{2}{13} \frac{a_0}{\dot{a}_0} = \frac{2}{13} \frac{P_0}{|\dot{P}_0|} \approx 3.16\,\text{Myr}
\end{equation}
confirming WASP-12b as the benchmark archetype for direct in situ empirical measurement of high stellar tidal dissipation ($Q'_* \sim 10^5$).
