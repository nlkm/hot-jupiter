\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Cotton Candy World with Volcanic Clouds}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 200 light-years away in the constellation Virgo, \textbf{WASP-107b} is one of the strangest exoplanets ever found: it is nearly as huge as Jupiter, but weighs less than Neptune! Its overall density is so astonishingly low ($\rho = 0.13\,\text{g/cm}^3$) that astronomers affectionately call it a \textbf{``cotton candy'' planet}.

\subsection*{A Giant Puffed-Up Atmosphere Smelling of Burnt Matches}
When the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) aimed its infrared instruments at WASP-107b, it solved two major planetary mysteries:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Why is it so puffed up?} Slight orbital eccentricity causes Saturn-like tidal friction in its deep core, pumping tidal heat ($1.2\,\text{W/m}^2$) upward to prevent the gaseous hydrogen envelope from contracting.
\item \textbf{Active Chemistry in Action}: JWST detected clear chemical fingerprints of \textbf{sulfur dioxide ($\mathrm{SO}_2$)} and silicate sand clouds! Ultraviolet light from its host star breaks down hydrogen sulfide gas in its upper atmosphere, creating active smog like the smell of struck matches.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Tidal Dissipation Heating \& Photochemical Kinetics}

\subsection*{1. Tidal Core Heating \& Envelope Inflation}
WASP-107b has a tiny core ($M_{\text{core}} \approx 12\,M_\oplus$) surrounded by an extended $\mathrm{H/He}$ envelope ($R_p = 0.94\,R_{\text{Jup}}$). The interior tidal dissipation rate driven by eccentricity $e = 0.06$ is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{E}_{\text{tide}} = \frac{21}{2} \frac{k_{2,p}}{Q'_p} \frac{G M_*^2 R_p^5 n e^2}{a^6} \approx 1.2\,\text{W/m}^2
\end{equation}
This internal heat flux keeps the radiative-convective boundary deep ($P_{\text{RCB}} > 100\,\text{bars}$), inflating the outer scale height to $H \approx 500\,\text{km}$.

\subsection*{2. Non-Equilibrium $\mathrm{SO}_2$ Photochemistry}
Host star EUV/NUV photolysis of $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{S}$ and $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$ produces free hydroxyl ($\mathrm{OH}$) and sulfur ($\mathrm{S}$) radicals, initiating the photochemical chain:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{S} + h\nu \to \mathrm{HS} + \mathrm{H}, \quad \mathrm{HS} + \mathrm{OH} \to \mathrm{SO} + \mathrm{H}_2, \quad \mathrm{SO} + \mathrm{OH} \to \mathrm{SO}_2 + \mathrm{H}
\end{equation}
yielding a high steady-state abundance $X_{\mathrm{SO}_2} \approx (2.0 \pm 0.5) \times 10^{-5}$ ($20\,\text{ppm}$).

\subsection*{1. Vigorous Vertical Mixing \& Condensation Suppression}
The lack of detectable methane ($\mathrm{CH}_4 < 1\,\text{ppm}$) combined with abundant water ($\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \approx 0.2\%$) and $\mathrm{SO}_2$ confirms vigorous vertical eddy mixing ($K_{zz} \approx 10^9\,\text{cm}^2/\text{s}$), lofting hot core gases to the observable thermosphere.
