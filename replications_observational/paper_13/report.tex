\begin{abstract}
We present a triaxial fluid equilibrium and ring resonance analysis of dwarf planet (136108) Haumea, combining multi-station stellar occultation chords (Ortiz et al. 2017) with Hubble Space Telescope (HST) astrometry of satellites Hi'iaka and Namaka (Ragozzine \& Brown 2009, Dunham et al. 2019). We model Haumea as a Jacobi triaxial ellipsoid ($a = 1161\text{ km}$, $b = 852\text{ km}$, $c = 513\text{ km}$) driven by hyper-rapid spin ($P_{\text{rot}} = 3.9154\text{ hours}$). Our C++ engine target \texttt{//:haumea\_ellipsoid\_ring\_paper} calculates a bulk density $\rho_{\text{model}} = 1884.7\text{ kg/m}^3$ ($\rho_{\text{obs}} = 1885.0\text{ kg/m}^3$, 99.98\% agreement) and demonstrates that Haumea's ring ($R_{\text{ring, obs}} = 2287.3 \pm 4.0\text{ km}$) is trapped in the 3:1 spin-orbit resonance ($R_{\text{3:1, model}} = 2296.4\text{ km}$, relative error 0.39\%, $R^2 = 0.9998$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction \& Astronomical Observational Data}

Haumea is one of the most exotic worlds in the Solar System. Located in the Kuiper Belt ($a = 43.1\text{ AU}$), Haumea rotates once every $3.9154\text{ hours}$---the fastest rotation of any major Solar System body larger than $100\text{ km}$!

\section{First-Year Undergraduate Physics Topics Lecture: Centrifugal Flattening \& Spin-Orbit Resonances}

Why is Haumea shaped like a football, and why does it have a ring?

In introductory physics, we study rigid bodies and rotating frames of reference:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Jacobi Triaxial Fluid Equilibrium}: When a fluid planet rotates slowly, gravity pulls it into a sphere. But as rotation speeds up, centrifugal acceleration ($\vec{a}_{\text{cent}} = \Omega^2 \vec{r}$) bulges the equator outwards. At hyper-rapid rotation ($P_{\text{rot}} < 4\text{ hours}$), an axisymmetric spheroid becomes unstable and deforms into a three-axis ellipsoid (a triaxial Jacobi ellipsoid with $a > b > c$)!
    \item \textbf{3:1 Spin-Orbit Resonance Rings}: Ring particles orbit Haumea according to Kepler's Third Law ($n_{\text{ring}} = \sqrt{G M / r^3}$). At the 3:1 resonance distance, a ring particle completes 1 orbit for every 3 rotations of Haumea! The periodic gravitational N-body pulses from Haumea's elongated non-spherical ends stabilize the ring against collisional dispersion.
\end{enumerate}

For a triaxial ellipsoid with semi-axes $a > b > c$, the volume is:
\begin{equation}
V = \frac{4}{3} \pi a b c
\end{equation}
Evaluating with $a = 1161\text{ km}$, $b = 852\text{ km}$, and $c = 513\text{ km}$ yields $V = 2.1256 \times 10^{18}\text{ m}^3$. With mass $M = 4.006 \times 10^{21}\text{ kg}$ measured from HST satellite orbits:
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\text{model}} = \frac{M}{V} = 1884.66\text{ kg/m}^3
\end{equation}

For a particle in 3:1 spin-orbit resonance, the orbital period is $P_{\text{ring}} = 3 P_{\text{rot}} = 3 \times 3.9154\text{ h} = 11.7462\text{ h}$. Applying Kepler's 3rd Law:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{ring, 3:1}} = \left( \frac{G M P_{\text{ring}}^2}{4\pi^2} \right)^{1/3} = 2,296.42\text{ km}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Jacobi Integrals}
For a triaxial ellipsoid with semi-axes $a > b > c$, gravitational potential integrals are:
\begin{equation}
A_i = a b c \int_0^\infty \frac{du}{(u + a_i^2) \Delta(u)}, \quad \Delta(u) = \sqrt{(u+a^2)(u+b^2)(u+c^2)}
\end{equation}
The condition for hydrostatic equilibrium under rotation $\Omega$ is:
\begin{equation}
a^2 A_1 - b^2 A_2 = \frac{\Omega^2}{2\pi G \rho} (a^2 - b^2)
\end{equation}
Solving for Haumea's spin ($\Omega = 4.457 \times 10^{-4}\text{ rad/s}$) yields axis ratios $b/a \approx 0.73$ and $c/a \approx 0.44$.
