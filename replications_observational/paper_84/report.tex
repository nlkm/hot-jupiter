\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Four-Part Harmony of Sub-Neptunes}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Orbiting a sun-like star in the constellation Cygnus, \textbf{Kepler-223} hosts a system of four gaseous mini-Neptunes (planets b, c, d, and e) locked in one of the most mathematically pristine orbital configurations ever discovered in astronomy.

\subsection*{The 8:6:4:3 Musical Resonance}
For every \textbf{8 orbits} of innermost planet b, planet c completes \textbf{6 orbits}, planet d completes \textbf{4 orbits}, and outermost planet e completes exactly \textbf{3 orbits}. This 4-planet resonant chain is the exoplanetary equivalent of Jupiter's Galilean moons (Io, Europa, Ganymede), but extended across an entire four-body planetary system that has preserved its gentle disk migration architecture for billions of years!
