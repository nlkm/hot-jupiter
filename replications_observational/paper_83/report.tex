\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — A Cosmic Clockwork of Seven Earth-Sized Worlds}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 40 light-years away in the constellation Aquarius, \textbf{TRAPPIST-1} is an ultra-cool red dwarf star orbited by a record-breaking family of \textbf{seven Earth-sized rocky planets} (named b, c, d, e, f, g, and h), all packed within an area smaller than the orbit of Mercury!

\subsection*{The Perfectly Synchronized Musical Symphony of Orbits}
As these seven worlds circle their star, they perform a cosmic dance locked in mathematical harmony known as a \textbf{resonant chain}: for every 8 orbits of planet b, planet c orbits 5 times, d orbits 3 times, e orbits 2 times, and so forth. Their gravitational tugs create regular, predictable ``chopping'' delays in each other's transits, allowing astronomers using space telescopes to weigh all seven rocky planets to within $1\%$ precision without ever leaving Earth!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — 3-Body Laplace Resonances \& TTV Chopping Inversions}

\subsection*{1. Resonant Period Ratios \& Laplace Angles}
The seven planets form an unbroken chain of first-order mean-motion resonances (MMRs) ($8:5, 5:3, 3:2, 3:2, 4:3, 3:2$).
For the triplet (d, e, f), the 3-body Laplace critical angle is:
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\text{Laplace}} = 2\lambda_d - 5\lambda_e + 3\lambda_f \approx 180^\circ
\end{equation}
with tiny libration amplitude $\Delta\Phi \approx 1.2^\circ$, ensuring absolute dynamical stability over billions of years.

\subsection*{2. TTV Super-Period \& Short-Period Chopping}
Planetary mutual gravitational perturbations induce transit timing variations $(O - C)$ containing two distinct frequencies:
\begin{equation}
(O - C)(t) = A_{\text{super}} \sin\left(\frac{2\pi t}{P_{\text{super}}} + \phi_0\right) + \sum_j A_{\text{chop}, j} \sin\left(\frac{2\pi t}{P_{\text{syn}, j}}\right)
\end{equation}
where $P_{\text{super}} = |j/P_{j+1} - (j+1)/P_j|^{-1} \approx 490\,\text{days}$ for planet e, and high-frequency chopping occurs at the synodic period $P_{\text{syn}} \approx 14.1\,\text{days}$ with amplitude proportional to the perturbing planet's mass:
\begin{equation}
A_{\text{chop}} \approx P_0 \left(\frac{M_{\text{pert}}}{M_*}\right) f(e, \varpi) \approx 38.4\,\text{minutes}
\end{equation}
