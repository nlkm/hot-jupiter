\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — Giant Cliffs of Pure Ice on Mars}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
While Mars looks like a bone-dry desert of red dust and volcanic rock, vast oceans of frozen water are hidden right beneath the surface in the planet's mid-latitudes.

\subsection*{Staring at 400-Foot Cliffs of Blue Ice}
Using the High Resolution Imaging Science Experiment (HiRISE) camera aboard NASA's Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO), scientists discovered steep eroding scarps (cliffs) where the top layer of dry soil has sloughed away. These cliffs expose **towering 400-foot-tall (130-meter) vertical walls of brilliant, pure water ice ($>95\%$ purity)** sitting beneath just 3 to 6 feet (1 to 2 meters) of protective red dust! Deep orbital radar soundings confirm these are massive, clean glaciers from ancient Martian ice ages when the planet was tilted differently toward the Sun!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Radar Dielectrics \& Subsurface Sounding}

\subsection*{1. Electromagnetic Wave Propagation in Dielectric Media}
Orbital radar sounders like SHARAD transmit high-frequency chirps ($15 - 25\,\text{MHz}, \lambda_0 \approx 15\,\text{m}$). The phase velocity in a non-magnetic low-loss medium is:
\begin{equation}
v_{\text{phase}} = \frac{c}{\sqrt{\epsilon_r}}
\end{equation}
For pure water ice, $\epsilon_r \approx 3.15 \pm 0.10$, yielding $v_{\text{phase}} \approx 169\,\text{m/}\mu\text{s}$. The measured two-way travel time delay $\Delta\tau$ to the basal bedrock reflector determines the ice thickness $H_{\text{ice}}$:
\begin{equation}
H_{\text{ice}} = \frac{c \Delta\tau}{2 \sqrt{\epsilon_r}} \approx 130.0\,\text{m}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Sublimation Lag Stability \& Obliquity Cycles}
Under modern Martian atmospheric conditions ($P \approx 6\,\text{mbar}, T \approx 180 - 220\,\text{K}$), pure surface ice sublimates at $\dot{E} \sim 1\,\text{mm/yr}$. A dry lithic lag deposit of thickness $z_{\text{lag}} \ge 1.5\,\text{m}$ acts as a diffusive barrier, reducing water vapor flux by $10^5\times$ and stabilizing the glacier for millions of years across chaotic obliquity variations ($15^\circ - 45^\circ$).
