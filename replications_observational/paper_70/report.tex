\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Celestial Herdsmen of Saturn's Ring}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Saturn's \textbf{F ring} is one of the most dynamic, braided, and knotted ribbon structures in the solar system. Orbiting just beyond the main ring system, it is held in place by two small, potato-shaped icy moons: \textbf{Prometheus} (on the inside) and \textbf{Pandora} (on the outside).

\subsection*{Sculpting Streamers, Kinks, and Baby Snowballs}
Every 68 hours, Prometheus swings slightly outward at its orbital apoapse, plunging its gravitational pull right into the inner edge of the F ring. Like a spoon dragging through coffee cream, Prometheus scoops out thousands of miles of dark channels called \textbf{streamers} and bunches icy pebbles into ephemeral \textbf{moonlets} (nicknamed ``kittens'' by Cassini scientists) that constantly form and shatter!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Gravitational Shepherding \& Lindblad Torques}

\subsection*{1. Goldreich-Tremaine Shepherd Torque Balance}
Resonant shepherding by inner moon (Prometheus, mass $M_1$, semi-major axis $a_1$) and outer moon (Pandora, mass $M_2$, semi-major axis $a_2$) applies one-sided Lindblad resonance torques:
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{inner}} = +\frac{8}{27} \frac{G^2 M_1^2 \sigma r}{\Omega^2 (r - a_1)^4}, \quad T_{\text{outer}} = -\frac{8}{27} \frac{G^2 M_2^2 \sigma r}{\Omega^2 (a_2 - r)^4}
\end{equation}
Equating $T_{\text{inner}} + T_{\text{outer}} = 0$ yields the equilibrium ring core semi-major axis:
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{F-ring}} = \frac{a_1 + (M_1 / M_2)^{1/4} a_2}{1 + (M_1 / M_2)^{1/4}} \approx 140{,}220\,\text{km}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Streamer Channel Deflection Kinematics}
During close orbital encounters ($d_{\text{min}} \approx \Delta a - a_1 e_1 \approx 840\,\text{km}$), Prometheus imparts an impulsive velocity perturbation $\Delta v_r$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta v_r \approx \frac{2 G M_{\text{Prom}}}{v_{\text{rel}} d_{\text{min}}} \implies \Delta r_{\text{streamer}} \approx \frac{2 \Delta v_r}{\Omega} \approx 50\,\text{km}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{1. Synodic Encounter Period \& Clump Gravitational Instability}
The synodic encounter period between Prometheus and the F-ring core is:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{syn}} = \frac{2\pi}{|\Omega_{\text{Prom}} - \Omega_{\text{ring}}|} \approx 68.0\,\text{hours} \quad (2.83\,\text{days})
\end{equation}
Gravitational compression at the channel edge raises local surface density past Toomre critical stability ($Q < 1$), seeding transient clumps with escape velocities $v_{\text{esc}} \sim 1 - 5\,\text{m/s}$.
