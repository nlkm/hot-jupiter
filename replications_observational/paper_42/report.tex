\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Roaring Storms of the Blue Giant}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
When NASA's Voyager 2 spacecraft flew past \textbf{Neptune} in 1989, it discovered an enormous storm large enough to swallow the entire Earth: the \textbf{Great Dark Spot (GDS-89)}. Unlike Jupiter's Great Red Spot which has raged for centuries, Neptune's dark storms appear, drift across the planet, and vanish every few years!

\subsection*{The Fastest Winds in the Solar System}
Neptune has the fiercest winds in the entire Solar System, blowing backwards at over \textbf{900 miles per hour} ($400\,\text{m/s}$)—faster than the speed of sound! As these high-speed winds flow over the dark vortex, air is forced upward into the freezing stratosphere, condensing into brilliant white methane companion clouds that look like soaring alpine mountain clouds on Earth.

\subsection*{1. Quasi-Geostrophic Potential Vorticity Conservation}
The Great Dark Spot is an anticyclonic vortex governed by the conservation of shallow-water potential vorticity $q$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{D q}{Dt} = 0, \quad q = \frac{f + \zeta}{h} \approx \frac{f_0 + \beta y + \left( \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} \right)}{H_0 + h'}
\end{equation}
Because the Coriolis parameter $f = 2\Omega\sin\phi$ varies with latitude ($\beta \equiv df/dy > 0$), the vortex induces an asymmetric Rossby wave radiation field, driving a steady northward/retrograde drift at $u_{\text{drift}} \approx 15\,\text{m/s}$ relative to the ambient zonal flow.

\subsection*{2. Orographic Wave Updraft \& Methane Condensation}
When ambient zonal winds encounter the anticyclonic vortex barrier, vertical deflection creates an orographic gravity wave:
\begin{equation}
w(z) = u_{\text{wind}} \frac{\partial h_{\text{vortex}}}{\partial x} \approx 0.15\,\text{m/s}
\end{equation}
As air rises $50\,\text{km}$ above the tropopause ($T < 55\,\text{K}$), methane vapor rapidly supersaturates ($S \ge 1.05$), condensing into brilliant white cirrus clouds of crystalline methane ice ($\mathrm{CH_4}$).

\begin{equation}
\nabla^2 \psi - \frac{\psi}{L_D^2} = -\frac{\beta + U/L_D^2}{U - c} (\psi - U y)
