\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — Stepping into a Ball Pit in Outer Space}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located in near-Earth space, \textbf{(101955) Bennu} is a 500-meter-wide spinning top rubble-pile asteroid made of ancient carbonaceous material dating back 4.5 billion years to the birth of our Solar System.

\subsection*{A Surface with Zero Strength}
When NASA's OSIRIS-REx spacecraft touched down at the Nightingale crater in October 2020 to collect a sample, scientists expected the surface to feel like hard gravel. Instead, Bennu behaved like a \textbf{fluidized ball pit}! With virtually zero cohesion and gravity a hundred-thousandth of Earth's, the robotic sampling arm plunged \textbf{nearly 20 inches (48.8 cm) deep into the asteroid with almost zero resistance} ($< 1\,\text{Newton}$ of force), proving that Bennu's surface is held together by forces so weak that a human step would sink into its core!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Microgravity Granular Mechanics \& Contact Deceleration}

\subsection*{1. Microgravity Hydrostatic Lithostatic Pressure}
On Bennu ($M = 7.33 \times 10^{10}\,\text{kg}$, $R = 245\,\text{m}$, $\rho = 1190\,\text{kg/m}^3$), the surface gravitational acceleration is $g_{\text{eff}} \approx 60\,\mu\text{m/s}^2$ ($6 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{m/s}^2$). The lithostatic overburden pressure at depth $z = 0.5\,\text{m}$ is:
\begin{equation}
P(z) = \rho g_{\text{eff}} z \approx (1190)(6.0 \times 10^{-5})(0.5) \approx 0.036\,\text{Pa}
\end{equation}
Inter-particle van der Waals cohesion $C \approx 1.5\,\text{Pa}$ dominates gravity, but is small enough that macroscopic shear strength is negligible.

\subsection*{2. Granular Drag \& TAGSAM Penetration Kinematics}
The dynamic resistance force $F_R(z, v)$ during TAGSAM penetration at contact speed $v_0 = 0.10\,\text{m/s}$ is modeled by:
\begin{equation}
F_R(z, v) = k z + c_{\text{drag}} v^2 \approx (1.8\,\text{N/m}) z + (4.5\,\text{N}\cdot\text{s}^2/\text{m}^2) v^2
\end{equation}
yielding $F_R < 1.0\,\text{N}$ throughout the $48.8\,\text{cm}$ descent, allowing the $121.6\,\text{g}$ pristine carbonaceous sample to be collected.

\subsection*{1. Rubble-Pile Porosity \& Fluidized Ejecta Dynamics}
Contact analysis inverted bulk macro-porosity $\phi \approx 50 - 55\%$. The nitrogen gas pulse fluidly mobilized $\sim 10^3\,\text{kg}$ of particulate debris, excavating an elliptical crater ($D \approx 9.0\,\text{m}$) without elastic rebounding.
