\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Six-Sided Crown of Saturn}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Capping the North Pole of \textbf{Saturn} is one of the most bizarre geometric structures in astronomy: a colossal, almost perfect \textbf{six-sided hexagon} spanning nearly 20,000 miles (30,000 km) across—wide enough to fit four Earths inside it!

\subsection*{A Giant Swirling Atmospheric Highway}
Rather than a solid structure, Saturn's hexagon is an ultra-fast \textbf{220 mph (100 m/s) jet stream} trapped at $78^\circ\,\text{N}$ latitude. As this narrow atmospheric river races around the pole, it snakes into a permanent, stationary wave pattern with exactly six sides. Because the wave moves at the exact same rotational speed as Saturn's deep magnetic interior ($10.66\,\text{hours}$), the hexagon has remained frozen in place without drifting for over 40 years!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Barotropic Rossby Waves \& Potential Vorticity}

\subsection*{1. Planetary Rossby Wave Dispersion}
In a rotating barotropic fluid on a beta-plane ($f = f_0 + \beta y$, $\beta = \frac{2\Omega\cos\phi}{R_S}$), small meridional perturbations on a background zonal jet of speed $\bar{u}$ propagate as Rossby waves with dispersion relation:
\begin{equation}
c_{\text{phase}} = \bar{u} - \frac{\beta + \bar{u} k_y^2}{k_x^2 + k_y^2}
\end{equation}
For a stationary wave ($c_{\text{phase}} = 0$ in the rotating frame $\Omega_{\text{mag}}$), the resonant zonal wavenumber $k_x$ matches the 6-fold azimuthal geometry:
\begin{equation}
k_x = \frac{k_m}{R_S \cos\phi} \approx \frac{6}{12{,}220\,\text{km}} \implies \bar{u} \approx \frac{\beta}{k_x^2} \approx 100.0\,\text{m/s}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Potential Vorticity Conservation \& Sharp Gradient}
Conservation of quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity $q = \nabla^2 \psi + f$ creates an intense meridional vorticity gradient across the jet width ($W_{\text{jet}} \approx 1500\,\text{km}$), acting as a dynamic barrier preventing mixing between the polar cap and mid-latitudes.
