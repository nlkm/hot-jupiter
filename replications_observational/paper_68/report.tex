\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Naked Lava World}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 48 light-years away in the constellation Indus, \textbf{LHS 3844b} is a super-Earth ($1.30\,R_\oplus$, $2.25\,M_\oplus$) orbiting blisteringly close to its cool red dwarf star once every 11 hours.

\subsection*{A Planet Completely Stripped of Air}
When NASA's *Spitzer Space Telescope* stared at LHS 3844b for 100 continuous hours in infrared light, astronomers made a landmark discovery: the planet's dayside glows fiercely at $1,410^\circ\text{F}$ ($1040\,\text{K}$), but its nightside is pitch-black and freezing cold (near absolute zero). If this world had even a whisper of an atmosphere, winds would carry heat to the dark side. Instead, the heat escapes straight into space—proving that LHS 3844b is a completely \textbf{bare rock} of dark volcanic basalt with zero atmosphere!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Instantaneous Reradiation \& Heat Redistribution}

\subsection*{1. Substellar Equilibrium Temperature for Bare Rock}
With zero atmospheric advection ($\varepsilon_{\text{advect}} = 0$) and low basaltic Bond albedo $A_B \approx 0.05$, local radiative equilibrium yields:
\begin{equation}
T(\theta) = T_{\text{sub}} \max(0, \cos\theta)^{1/4}, \quad T_{\text{sub}} = \left( \frac{(1 - A_B) L_\star}{16\pi \sigma_{\text{SB}} a^2} \right)^{1/4} \approx 1040\,\text{K}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Lambertian Disk Integrated Phase Function}
Integrating over the visible hemisphere at orbital phase angle $\alpha = 2\pi\phi$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{F_p(\alpha)}{F_p(0)} = \frac{\sin|\alpha| + (\pi - |\alpha|)\cos\alpha}{\pi}
\end{equation}
The peak secondary eclipse depth at $4.5\,\mu\text{m}$ is:
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\text{ecl}} = \left(\frac{R_p}{R_\star}\right)^2 \frac{B_\lambda(T_{\text{day}})}{B_\lambda(T_\star)} \approx 380\,\text{ppm}
\end{equation}
The sharp drop to zero flux at transit ($\phi = \pm 0.5$) rules out any atmosphere with surface pressure $P_{\text{surf}} > 0.10\,\text{bar}$.

\subsection*{1. Thermal Phase Curve Offset \& Advection Inversion}
The observed hotspot phase offset $\Delta \theta = -3^\circ \pm 6^\circ$ is consistent with zero, yielding an advection efficiency limit $\mathcal{F}_{\text{advect}} < 0.05$.
Energy-limited stellar wind and coronal XUV stripping over the $5\,\text{Gyr}$ system age has completely eroded any volatile envelope:
\begin{equation}
\int_0^{t_{\text{age}}} \dot{M}_{\text{loss}}(t) dt > 10^{21}\,\text{kg} \gg M_{\text{atm}}
\end{equation}
