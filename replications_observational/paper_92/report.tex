\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Bouncing Moon with a Hidden Abyss}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Saturn's largest moon, \textbf{Titan}, is a world wrapped in a dense golden smog of nitrogen and methane, boasting lakes of liquid natural gas on its frozen icy surface.

\subsection*{A 30-Foot Daily Tidal Wave Under the Ice}
As Titan travels along its eccentric 16-day orbit around Saturn, Saturn's massive gravitational pull squeezes and stretches the moon. Using radio signals from the Cassini spacecraft, scientists made a startling discovery: Titan's surface rises and falls by \textbf{30 feet (10 meters)} every single orbit! If Titan were completely solid ice, it would barely budge by 3 feet. This massive flexing proves that a deep, global ocean of liquid water and ammonia lies hidden beneath its outer ice shell!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Viscoelastic Love Numbers \& Tidal Elevation}

\subsection*{1. Gravitational Potential \& Radial Love Numbers}
The response of a planetary body to a tidal perturbing potential $V_2$ is parameterized by the degree-2 Love numbers:
\begin{equation}
\Delta U_{\text{tide}} = k_2 V_2, \quad \Delta h = h_2 \frac{V_2}{g}
\end{equation}
For a multi-layered body with an outer elastic ice shell ($d_{\text{crust}} \approx 80\,\text{km}$) decoupled by a liquid water ocean ($d_{\text{ocean}} \approx 350\,\text{km}$), the potential Love number is:
\begin{equation}
k_2 = k_{2,\text{solid}} + \frac{k_{2,\text{fluid}} - k_{2,\text{solid}}}{1 + \alpha_{\text{mem}} (d_{\text{crust}}/R_T) (\mu_{\text{ice}} / \rho g R_T)} \approx 0.589
\end{equation}
compared to $k_2 \approx 0.038$ for a completely solid interior.

\subsection*{2. Diurnal Eccentricity Tidal Wave Amplitude}
In Titan's eccentric orbit ($e = 0.0288$), the radial surface elevation varies as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta h(t) = \frac{3}{2} \frac{G M_S R_T^4}{a_T^3 g_T} e h_2 \cos(n t) \approx 10.0\,\text{meters} \cos(n t)
\end{equation}

\subsection*{1. Spherical Harmonic Gravity Inversion \& Interior Constraints}
Cassini Doppler tracking during periapse flybys inverted the time-variable quadrupole gravity field:
\begin{equation}
\Delta C_{22}(t) = \frac{k_2}{4} \frac{M_S}{M_T} \left(\frac{R_T}{a_T}\right)^3 e \cos(nt), \quad k_2 = 0.589 \pm 0.075
\end{equation}
ruling out a solid frozen interior at $>10\sigma$ confidence and constraining the internal liquid water-ammonia layer salinity ($\rho_{\text{ocean}} \approx 1050 - 1100\,\text{kg/m}^3$).
