\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Exploding Asteroid Fireworks}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
When NASA's *OSIRIS-REx* spacecraft arrived at the near-Earth carbonaceous asteroid \textbf{(101955) Bennu}, navigation cameras caught something astonishing: the asteroid was actively spitting showers of glowing pebbles and rocks out into space like natural planetary \textbf{fireworks}!

\subsection*{How Day-to-Night Temperature Swings Shatter Boulders}
Bennu spins once every 4.3 hours, rapidly cycling its surface between $-100^\circ\text{F}$ and $+250^\circ\text{F}$. This extreme thermal shock causes rocks to expand and contract violently. Microscopic cracks accumulate until a rock suddenly fractures like an exploding glass cup, releasing stored elastic spring energy that flings pebble-sized fragments ($0.5 - 2\,\text{inches}$) into orbit at speeds up to $2\,\text{mph}$!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Thermal Elastic Strain Energy Release}

\subsection*{1. Diurnal Thermal Stress Generation}
Diurnal temperature fluctuations $\Delta T \approx 150\,\text{K}$ generate thermoelastic compressive and tensile hoop stresses:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\text{thermal}} = \frac{E \alpha \Delta T}{1 - \nu} \approx \frac{(15\,\text{GPa})(1.0 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{K}^{-1})(150\,\text{K})}{1 - 0.25} \approx 30\,\text{MPa}
\end{equation}
far exceeding the macroscopic tensile strength of carbonaceous CM chondrites ($\sigma_{\text{tensile}} \approx 1 - 5\,\text{MPa}$).

\subsection*{2. Elastic Strain Energy to Kinetic Energy Conversion}
The strain energy density stored in the pre-fracture rock volume $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r_p^3$ is $u_{\text{strain}} = \frac{\sigma^2}{2 E}$.
Upon catastrophic brittle exfoliation, a fraction $\epsilon_{\text{mech}} \approx 0.01 - 0.05$ converts to kinetic energy:
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{2} m_p v_{\text{launch}}^2 = \epsilon_{\text{mech}} \left( \frac{\sigma^2}{2 E} \right) \left( \frac{4}{3}\pi r_p^3 \right) \implies v_{\text{launch}} = \sigma \sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\text{mech}}}{\rho E}} \approx 0.15 - 1.0\,\text{m/s}
\end{equation}
Because Bennu's escape velocity is only $v_{\text{esc}} \approx 0.20\,\text{m/s}$, many pebbles permanently escape into interplanetary space, while others orbit for days before softly re-impacting.
