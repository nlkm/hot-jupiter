\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Moon That Plucks Saturn's Ring Like a Harp}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Saturn's \textbf{F Ring} is a dynamic, braided ribbon of ice and dust located just outside the bright main rings, watched over by two shepherd moons: Prometheus and Pandora.

\subsection*{A 30-Mile Cosmic Scar Every 68 Hours}
Every $68\,\text{hours}$, the potato-shaped inner moon \textbf{Prometheus} reaches the closest point in its eccentric orbit and plunges directly toward the F ring. Its gravity acts like a giant celestial rake: it pulls icy particles toward itself, carving a \textbf{30-mile-wide (50 km) dark gouge or channel} through the ring and leaving behind a spectacular, curving streamer of glittering dust behind it, constantly sculpting Saturn's most active ring!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Resonant Gravitational Impulses \& Hill Sphere Dynamics}

\subsection*{1. Shepherd Orbital Mechanics \& Synodic Encounters}
Prometheus ($a_p = 139{,}380\,\text{km}$, $e_p = 0.0022$) and the F-ring core ($a_r = 140{,}220\,\text{km}$) have mean orbital motions $n_p$ and $n_r$. At Prometheus's apoapse, the radial separation shrinks to just $\Delta r_{\min} = a_r - a_p(1 + e_p) \approx 530\,\text{km}$, which is comparable to Prometheus's Hill sphere radius:
\begin{equation}
R_H = a_p \left(\frac{M_{\text{prom}}}{3 M_S}\right)^{1/3} \approx 45\,\text{km}
\end{equation}
The synodic period between successive close encounters is:
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{syn}} = \frac{2\pi}{|n_p - n_r|} \approx 68.0\,\text{hours} \quad (2.83\,\text{days})
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Gravitational Perturbation Impulse}
During close passage, Prometheus exerts a transverse and radial gravitational acceleration impulse $\Delta v_r \approx \int F_r \, dt$, exciting eccentricities $\Delta e \approx 4 \times 10^{-4}$ in ring particles, displacing them by $\Delta r \approx -50\,\text{km}$ into Keplerian shear trajectories that form trailing streamer-channel patterns.

\subsection*{1. Streamer Shearing \& Channel Rejuvenation}
Keplerian differential shear linearly elongates streamers at rate $\frac{d\lambda}{dt} = -\frac{3}{2} n_r \frac{\Delta a}{a_r} \approx 3.2^\circ/\text{day}$, which dissipates the channel over $\sim 10$ orbits until the next Prometheus encounter recreates a fresh morphology.
