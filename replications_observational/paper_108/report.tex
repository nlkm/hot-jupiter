\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Kidnapped World of Neptune}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Neptune's largest moon, \textbf{Triton} (1,680 miles / 2,706 km across), is the only giant moon in the Solar System that orbits \textbf{backwards (retrograde)} relative to its planet's spin!

\subsection*{How a Stolen Dwarf Planet Melted from the Inside Out}
Billions of years ago, Triton was a dwarf planet orbiting peacefully in the Kuiper Belt with a twin companion (like Pluto and Charon). When it passed too close to Neptune, Neptune's gravity pulled Triton away from its partner and captured it into a chaotic, highly elongated backwards orbit. For 100 million years, Neptune's immense tidal gravity squeezed and kneaded Triton so violently that its entire interior \textbf{melted into a global boiling magma-water ocean}, completely destroying any original moons and wiping its surface clean into the dimpled ``cantaloupe terrain'' and erupting active 5-mile-tall nitrogen geysers we see today!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Binary Exchange Capture \& Viscoelastic Circularization}

\subsection*{1. Three-Body Binary Exchange Capture Mechanics}
Triton was captured via three-body gravitational exchange when a Kuiper Belt binary ($M_1 + M_2$) grazed Neptune ($M_N = 1.024 \times 10^{26}\,\text{kg}$), ejecting the companion and binding Triton on a retrograde orbit ($i = 156.8^\circ$) with initial eccentricity $e_0 \approx 0.99$ and periapse $q = a(1-e) \approx 354{,}760\,\text{km}$.

\subsection*{2. Tidal Energy Dissipation \& Resurfacing Heat Flux}
Under orbital angular momentum conservation ($h = \sqrt{G M_N a(1 - e^2)} = \text{const}$), tidal orbital circularization dissipated total orbital energy:
\begin{equation}
\Delta E_{\text{tide}} = \frac{G M_N M_T}{2 a_f} \left( \frac{1}{1 - e_0} - 1 \right) \approx 5.2 \times 10^{28}\,\text{J}
\end{equation}
Over a circularization timescale $\tau_{\text{circ}} \approx 100\,\text{Myr}$, the average tidal surface heat flux:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{tide}} \approx \frac{\Delta E_{\text{tide}}}{4\pi R_T^2 \tau_{\text{circ}}} \sim 10^3 - 10^4\,\text{W/m}^2
\end{equation}
exceeded the solar insolation ($F_\odot \approx 1.5\,\text{W/m}^2$) by four orders of magnitude, causing complete differentiation and global melting.
