\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — A Real-Life Earth Twin with Seven Moons}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 40 light-years away in the constellation Aquarius, \textbf{TRAPPIST-1e} is one of the most promising potentially habitable exoplanets ever discovered. It is nearly identical to Earth in size and mass, orbits in the temperate habitable zone of an ultra-cool red dwarf star, and may harbor vast liquid water oceans!

\subsection*{Why TRAPPIST-1e Doesn't Turn Into a Volcanic Hell}
Because TRAPPIST-1e orbits close to its star in a resonant chain of 7 planets, gravitational tides flex its rocky mantle. If these tides generated too much friction, the oceans would boil away in an explosive runaway volcanic catastrophe like Jupiter's volcanic moon Io. Fortunately, our physics shows that the planet's solid rock mantle dampens these tides safely, generating just enough gentle internal warmth ($F_{\text{tide}} \approx 0.04\,\text{W/m}^2$) to stabilize temperate surface oceans without boiling the planet dry!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Viscoelastic Tidal Heating \& Orbital Resonances}

\subsection*{1. Orbital Mean Motion \& Tidal Forcing}
TRAPPIST-1e orbits at semi-major axis $a = 0.02928\,\text{AU}$ with orbital period $P = 6.10\,\text{days}$ ($n_{\text{orb}} = \sqrt{G M_\star / a^3} \approx 1.19 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{rad/s}$) and eccentricity $e \approx 0.0051$ locked by 3-body Laplace resonances.

\subsection*{2. Tidal Heating Power \& Complex Love Number}
The tidal heat dissipation rate $E_{\text{tide}}$ in an eccentric synchronous orbit is:
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{tide}} = \frac{21}{2} \frac{G M_\star^2 R_p^5}{a^6} e^2 n_{\text{orb}} \operatorname{Im}[k_2(\omega, T)]
\end{equation}
For an Andrade viscoelastic mantle with compliance $J(\omega) = \frac{1}{\mu} - \frac{i}{\omega\eta} + \beta (i\omega)^{-\alpha}$, the imaginary Love number is $\operatorname{Im}[k_2] \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-4}$.
The resulting global surface tidal heat flux is:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{tide}} = \frac{E_{\text{tide}}}{4\pi R_p^2} \approx 0.042\,\text{W/m}^2
\end{equation}
This is well below the desiccation threshold ($F_{\text{crit}} \approx 0.10\,\text{W/m}^2$) and over $50\times$ lower than Io ($F_{\text{Io}} \approx 2.5\,\text{W/m}^2$), ensuring climate stability.

The complex shear modulus $\mu^*(\omega) = 1/J(\omega)$ incorporates transient power-law creep:
\begin{equation}
J(\omega) = \frac{1}{\mu_0} + \beta \omega^{-\alpha} \left( \cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2} - i \sin\frac{\alpha\pi}{2} \right) - \frac{i}{\omega\eta(T)}

with $\alpha = 0.30$ and activation energy $E_{\text{act}} = 300\,\text{kJ/mol}$, broadening the dissipation spectrum by two orders of magnitude compared to Maxwell models.
