\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Planet Where It Rains Liquid Iron}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 640 light-years away in the constellation Pisces, \textbf{WASP-76b} is an ultra-hot Jupiter tidally locked to its scorching parent star. Its dayside reaches staggering temperatures exceeding $4,300^\circ\text{F}$ ($2400\,\text{C}$)—so blazing hot that metals like \textbf{iron vaporize} into gaseous iron steam!

\subsection*{How Iron Vapor Turns into Liquid Metal Rain}
Powerful $10,000\,\text{mph}$ equatorial jet streams blow vaporized iron gas across the evening terminator boundary toward the cool nightside ($1400\,\text{K}$). As the temperature plummets below iron's condensation point ($1800\,\text{K}$), the iron vapor condenses into liquid metal droplets, unleashing torrential \textbf{liquid iron rainstorms} on the dark side of the planet!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Metal Condensation \& Asymmetric Terminators}

\subsection*{1. Iron Vapor Pressure \& Condensation Temperature}
The saturation vapor pressure of iron $P_{\text{sat, Fe}}(T)$ follows the empirical relation:
\begin{equation}
\log_{10} P_{\text{sat, Fe}}(\text{bar}) = 6.49 - \frac{20{,}990}{T\,(\text{K})}
\end{equation}
At atmospheric pressures $P \sim 1 - 10\,\text{mbar}$, the iron condensation temperature is $T_{\text{cond}} \approx 1800\,\text{K}$.
On the dayside ($T_{\text{day}} \approx 2500\,\text{K}$), iron is $100\%$ vaporized ($\mathrm{Fe\,I}$ gas).
On the nightside ($T_{\text{night}} \approx 1400\,\text{K}$), $P_{\text{sat, Fe}} \ll P_{\text{partial}}$, causing complete condensation into liquid droplets $\mathrm{Fe\,(l)}$.

\subsection*{2. Asymmetric Cross-Correlation Signal}
Because the eastward jet carries hot vapor from the dayside across the trailing evening limb, strong $\mathrm{Fe\,I}$ absorption appears at orbital phase $\phi > 0$ with a blueshift of $v_{\text{wind}} \approx -5\,\text{km/s}$.
Across the leading morning limb ($\phi < 0$), all iron has rained out on the nightside, causing the atomic $\mathrm{Fe\,I}$ signal to completely disappear!

\subsection*{1. Doppler Velocity Cross-Correlation Line Profiles}
The high-resolution transmission flux ratio $F_{\text{in}} / F_{\text{out}}(\lambda, \phi)$ is cross-correlated with an atomic $\mathrm{Fe\,I}$ template $T_{\text{Fe}}(\lambda)$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CCF}(v, \phi) = \int \left( 1 - \frac{F_{\text{in}}(\lambda, \phi)}{F_{\text{out}}(\lambda)} \right) T_{\text{Fe}}\left( \lambda \left[ 1 + \frac{v - v_{\text{orb}}(\phi) - v_{\text{jet}}}{c} \right] \right) d\lambda
\end{equation}
revealing an asymmetric line depth contrast of $\Delta \mathrm{CCF} = 4500\,\text{ppm}$ on the evening limb and $< 100\,\text{ppm}$ on the morning limb.
