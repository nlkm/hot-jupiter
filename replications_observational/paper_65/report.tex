\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Nearest Alien Earth in the Line of Fire}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
At a distance of just 4.2 light-years, \textbf{Proxima Centauri b} is our nearest known exoplanet neighbor. It has a mass similar to Earth ($1.17\,M_\oplus$) and orbits comfortably in the temperate habitable zone of its red dwarf star where liquid water could theoretically exist.

\subsection*{The Violent Wrath of an Active Red Dwarf}
Unfortunately, Proxima Centauri is an energetic flare star that frequently erupts with monstrous \textbf{superflares}—blasting the planet with thousands of times more ionizing ultraviolet radiation and X-rays than Earth ever receives from the Sun. Without a powerful planetary magnetic shield, these explosive blasts strip away the atmosphere like a supersonic blowtorch, boiling off surface water in just a hundred million years!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Energy-Limited Photoevaporation}

\subsection*{1. Extreme Ultraviolet (XUV) Flare Fluence}
Proxima b orbits at semi-major axis $a = 0.0485\,\text{AU}$.
During superflare events detected by Evryscope and ALMA, the XUV irradiance reaches:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{XUV}} \approx 2.5 \times 10^4\,\text{erg}/(\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{s}) \quad (25\,\text{W/m}^2)
\end{equation}
a factor of $>1000\times$ higher than modern Earth.

\subsection*{2. Hydrodynamic Mass-Loss Rate \& Depletion Timescale}
Under energy-limited escape, the mass-loss rate $\dot{M}_{\text{loss}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{M}_{\text{loss}} = \frac{\eta_{\text{eff}} \pi R_p R_{\text{XUV}}^2 F_{\text{XUV}}}{G M_p K_{\text{tide}}}
\end{equation}
With photoevaporation efficiency $\eta_{\text{eff}} \approx 0.15$:
\begin{equation}
\dot{M}_{\text{loss}} \approx 1.3 \times 10^3\,\text{kg/s} \quad (4.1 \times 10^{10}\,\text{kg/year})
\end{equation}
For an initial 1-bar secondary atmosphere ($M_{\text{atm}} \approx 5.1 \times 10^{18}\,\text{kg}$), the characteristic depletion timescale is:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\text{strip}} = \frac{M_{\text{atm}}}{\dot{M}_{\text{loss}}} \approx 120\,\text{Myr}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{1. Coronal Mass Ejection (CME) Magnetospheric Compression}
Dynamic pressure from associated CMEs $P_{\text{ram}} = \rho_{\text{CME}} v_{\text{CME}}^2 \approx 10^{-7}\,\text{Pa}$ compresses the magnetopause standoff radius:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{mp}} = \left( \frac{B_p^2}{2\mu_0 P_{\text{ram}}} \right)^{1/6} R_p \le 1.1\,R_p
\end{equation}
causing direct magnetospheric reconnection and ion pickup stripping across the dayside exobase.
