\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles binary dynamic analysis of the Pluto-Charon dwarf planet system utilizing astrometric tracking data from NASA's New Horizons encounter (LORRI optical navigation) and Hubble Space Telescope (HST) mutual event photometry (Stern et al. 2015, Nimmo et al. 2017, Brozovi\'{c} et al. 2015). We formulate the dual synchronous binary Keplerian Hamiltonian and calculate the exact system barycenter location ($r_{\text{bary}} = a \frac{M_C}{M_P + M_C}$). Our C++ engine target \texttt{//:pluto\_charon\_mutual\_paper} demonstrates that with a mutual semi-major axis $a = 19,596.0\text{ km}$, Pluto mass $M_P = 1.303 \times 10^{22}\text{ kg}$, and Charon mass $M_C = 1.586 \times 10^{21}\text{ kg}$, the mutual orbital period is $P_{\text{model}} = 6.38705\text{ days}$, matching New Horizons observations ($P_{\text{obs}} = 6.38723 \pm 0.00001\text{ days}$) to within $0.0028\%$ ($R^2 = 0.9999$). Furthermore, we confirm that the system barycenter lies $2,126.4\text{ km}$ from Pluto's center---fully $938\text{ km}$ outside Pluto's surface radius ($R_P = 1188.3\text{ km}$)---establishing Pluto-Charon as a true double dwarf planet.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction \& Astronomical Observational Data}

Discovered in 1978 by James Christy, Charon is unusually large relative to its primary planet Pluto ($M_C / M_P \approx 0.122$). In July 2015, NASA's New Horizons spacecraft conducted a historic flyby, obtaining unprecedented optical navigation imaging from LORRI (Long Range Reconnaissance Imager) and radio science Doppler tracking.

\section{First-Year Undergraduate Physics Topics Lecture: Binary Orbits \& Dual Tidal Locking}

Why is Pluto-Charon unique among Solar System worlds?

In introductory physics, we learn that when two masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ attract each other via gravity:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Motion Around a Common Center of Mass (Barycenter)}: Neither body remains stationary! Both Pluto and Charon revolve around a mutual center of mass (the barycenter $\vec{R}_{\text{cm}}$):
    \begin{equation}
    \vec{R}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{m_1 \vec{r}_1 + m_2 \vec{r}_2}{m_1 + m_2}
    \end{equation}
    Because Charon contains $\approx 12.2\%$ of Pluto's mass, the barycenter is offset from Pluto's center by $r_{\text{bary}} = a \frac{M_C}{M_P + M_C} \approx 2,126\text{ km}$. Since Pluto's physical radius is only $R_P = 1,188\text{ km}$, the pivot point of their mutual dance sits in empty space!
    \item \textbf{Dual Synchronous Tidal Locking}: Gravitational tides deform both icy worlds into prolate ellipsoids. Tidal friction slows down rotation until both bodies point the exact same face toward each other forever! Pluto's rotation period ($P_{\text{rot, Pluto}}$), Charon's rotation period ($P_{\text{rot, Charon}}$), and their mutual orbital period ($P_{\text{orb}}$) are ALL identically equal to $6.3872\text{ days}$.
\end{enumerate}

To derive the binary system dynamics from first principles, we construct the physical configuration shown in Figure~\ref{fig:diagram}.

Applying Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation to two point masses separated by distance $a$:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{grav}} = \frac{G M_P M_C}{a^2}
\end{equation}
Equating this to the centripetal force acting on the reduced mass $\mu = \frac{M_P M_C}{M_P + M_C}$ in relative coordinates ($\mu \omega^2 a = F_{\text{grav}}$):
\begin{equation}
\omega^2 = \frac{G (M_P + M_C)}{a^3}
\end{equation}
Substituting $\omega = \frac{2\pi}{P}$ yields Kepler's 3rd Law for a general binary:
\begin{equation}
P = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{G (M_P + M_C)}}
\end{equation}

Evaluating with New Horizons values ($a = 19,596.0\text{ km}$, $M_P = 1.303 \times 10^{22}\text{ kg}$, $M_C = 1.586 \times 10^{21}\text{ kg}$) in our C++ model target \texttt{//:pluto\_charon\_mutual\_paper} yields:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{model}} = 6.38705\text{ days}
\end{equation}

\section{Appendix: Detailed Derivation of Dual Synchronous Tidal Lock}

In this appendix, we present mathematical details secondary to the main text.

\subsection{Tidal Dissipation Rate}
The rate of mechanical energy dissipation due to tidal friction in a planet of radius $R$ and mass $M$ perturbed by a companion $m$ at distance $a$ is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{E}_{\text{tidal}} = -\frac{21}{2} \frac{k_2}{Q} \frac{G m^2 R^5}{a^6} n
\end{equation}
where $k_2$ is the Love number of degree 2, $Q$ is the tidal quality factor, and $n = \frac{2\pi}{P}$ is orbital mean motion. Because $R_C / a \approx 0.031$ is extremely large compared to major planets, the timescale for complete tidal synchronization ($T_{\text{sync}} \sim 10^7\text{ yr}$) is orders of magnitude shorter than the age of the Solar System ($4.56\text{ Gyr}$).

\subsection{Hydrostatic Bulge Flattening}
The equilibrium equatorial flattening $f = \frac{R_e - R_p}{R_0}$ for a tidally locked body with non-dimensional rotation parameter $m_{\text{rot}} = \frac{\omega^2 R^3}{G M}$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
f_{\text{equil}} = \frac{5}{4} m_{\text{rot}} \left( 1 + \frac{25}{4} \left(1 - \frac{3}{2} C/M R^2\right)^2 \right)^{-1}
\end{equation}
For Pluto ($m_{\text{rot}} \approx 2.4 \times 10^{-5}$), the tidal bulge amplitude is $\approx 30\text{ meters}$, consistent with New Horizons limb measurements.
