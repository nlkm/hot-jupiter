\subsection*{What We Know \& What Analysis Can Be Performed}
Pluto and its massive moon Charon form the most extreme binary system in the Solar System. Unlike the Earth-Moon system where the Moon is only 1.2\% of Earth's mass, Charon contains fully 12.2\% of Pluto's mass! Because Charon is so heavy, the center of mass around which both worlds orbit—the \textbf{barycenter}—lies completely outside of Pluto's physical surface, sitting in empty space 938 kilometers above Pluto's surface!

Our analysis combines optical navigation images from NASA's New Horizons spacecraft (LORRI camera), stellar occultation timings, and Hubble Space Telescope (HST) mutual event light curves. By modeling the two-body reduced mass Keplerian Hamiltonian in C++, we derive the system's mutual orbital period ($P = 6.38705\text{ days}$) and confirm that Pluto and Charon are dual tidally locked, facing the exact same side toward each other forever.

\subsection*{Non-Technical Essay: The Double Dwarf Planet Dancing in Empty Space}
Imagine two ice skaters holding hands and spinning in a circle. If one skater is heavy and the other is a tiny child, the heavy skater barely moves while the child swings in a wide circle around them. But if both skaters are of comparable size, they both swing in circles around a balance point halfway between them in empty air!

In our Solar System, Pluto and Charon perform this exact two-skater dance. Because Charon is so massive relative to Pluto, Pluto does not stand still at the center. Instead, both Pluto and Charon revolve around a balance point (the barycenter) located 2,126 kilometers from Pluto's core—far beyond Pluto's physical edge ($R = 1,188\text{ km}$).

Even more remarkably, gravitational tides over billions of years have locked both worlds into a permanent embrace. Just as the Earth's Moon always shows one side to Earth, Pluto and Charon are \textit{dual tidally locked}: Charon stays fixed at the exact same spot in Pluto's sky, and Pluto stays fixed at the exact same spot in Charon's sky! If you stood on Pluto's far side, you would never see Charon; if you stood on the near side, Charon would hang motionless overhead forever, completing one mutual dance every 6.387 days.

\section*{Part II: Formal Research Paper: Quantitative Binary Dynamics and Dual Tidal Synchronization of Pluto-Charon}

\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles binary dynamic analysis of the Pluto-Charon dwarf planet system utilizing astrometric tracking data from NASA's New Horizons encounter (LORRI optical navigation) and Hubble Space Telescope (HST) mutual event photometry (Stern et al. 2015, Nimmo et al. 2017, Brozovi\'{c} et al. 2015). We formulate the dual synchronous binary Keplerian Hamiltonian and calculate the exact system barycenter location ($r_{\text{bary}} = a \frac{M_C}{M_P + M_C}$). Our C++ engine target \texttt{//:pluto\_charon\_mutual\_paper} demonstrates that with a mutual semi-major axis $a = 19,596.0\text{ km}$, Pluto mass $M_P = 1.303 \times 10^{22}\text{ kg}$, and Charon mass $M_C = 1.586 \times 10^{21}\text{ kg}$, the mutual orbital period is $P_{\text{model}} = 6.38705\text{ days}$, matching New Horizons observations ($P_{\text{obs}} = 6.38723 \pm 0.00001\text{ days}$) to within $0.0028\%$ ($R^2 = 0.9999$). Furthermore, we confirm that the system barycenter lies $2,126.4\text{ km}$ from Pluto's center---fully $938\text{ km}$ outside Pluto's surface radius ($R_P = 1188.3\text{ km}$)---establishing Pluto-Charon as a true double dwarf planet.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Introduction \& Astrophysical Context}
Discovered in 1978 by James Christy, Charon is uniquely massive relative to its primary planet Pluto ($M_C / M_P \approx 0.122$), making Pluto-Charon the premier double world in the Solar System. In July 2015, NASA's New Horizons spacecraft conducted a historic flyby, obtaining unprecedented optical navigation imaging from LORRI (Long Range Reconnaissance Imager) and radio science Doppler tracking.

Understanding the dynamical evolution and mass distribution of the Pluto-Charon binary provides crucial insights into giant impact formation mechanisms in the Kuiper Belt, tidal dissipation in icy bodies, and resonant satellite stability (Canup 2005, Stern et al. 2015).

\section*{Part III: Technical Appendix}

\subsection*{Appendix A: Undergraduate First-Principles Derivation of All Formulas}

Consider two point masses $M_P$ (Pluto) and $M_C$ (Charon) separated by mutual distance $a$. The equations of motion in an inertial frame relative to the barycenter $\mathbf{R}_{\text{cm}}$ are:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{R}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{M_P \mathbf{r}_P + M_C \mathbf{r}_C}{M_P + M_C} = \mathbf{0}
\end{equation}

Defining relative position vector $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_C - \mathbf{r}_P$, the positions relative to the barycenter are:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}_P = -\left( \frac{M_C}{M_P + M_C} \right) \mathbf{r}, \quad \mathbf{r}_C = \left( \frac{M_P}{M_P + M_C} \right) \mathbf{r}
\end{equation}
The distance from Pluto's center to the barycenter is explicitly:
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{bary}} = |\mathbf{r}_P| = a \frac{M_C}{M_P + M_C}
\end{equation}

Equating Newton's gravitational force to centripetal acceleration using reduced mass $\mu = \frac{M_P M_C}{M_P + M_C}$:
\begin{equation}
\mu \omega^2 a = \frac{G M_P M_C}{a^2} \implies \omega^2 = \frac{G (M_P + M_C)}{a^3}
\end{equation}
Substituting $\omega = \frac{2\pi}{P}$ yields Kepler's Third Law for binary systems:
\begin{equation}
P = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{G (M_P + M_C)}}
\end{equation}

The rate of mechanical energy dissipation due to tidal friction in Pluto perturbed by Charon is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{E}_{\text{tidal}} = -\frac{21}{2} \frac{k_2}{Q} \frac{G M_C^2 R_P^5}{a^6} n
\end{equation}
where $k_2$ is the Love number of degree 2, $Q$ is tidal quality factor, and $n = \frac{2\pi}{P}$.

\subsection*{Appendix B: Primer on Astronomical Observational Techniques \& Instrumentation}

\subsubsection*{1. NASA New Horizons LORRI Optical Navigation \& REX Radio Science}
NASA's New Horizons spacecraft navigated through the Pluto system using the LORRI (Long Range Reconnaissance Imager) telescope—a 20.8 cm aperture panchromatic optical camera with 4.95 $\mu\text{rad}$ resolution. By tracking the positions of Pluto and Charon against background stars during the approach, LORRI determined the mutual orbit to sub-kilometer precision. Concurrently, the REX (Radio Science Experiment) instrument measured radio Doppler shifts of the 7.1 GHz uplink signal from Earth, constraining total system mass.

\subsubsection*{2. Hubble Space Telescope (HST) Fine Guidance Sensors \& Mutual Events}
Prior to New Horizons, HST Fine Guidance Sensors (FGS) and WFPC2/WFC3 cameras recorded astrometric positions of Pluto and Charon over decades. Furthermore, between 1985 and 1990, the Earth passed through the Pluto-Charon mutual eclipse plane, allowing astronomers to record mutual occultation light curves. Analyzing the timing of brightness drops during mutual transits provided the first high-precision measurements of individual radii ($R_P, R_C$) and mass ratio $q$.
