\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — A Glowing Infrared Cloud of Escaping Helium}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 123 light-years away in the constellation Cygnus, \textbf{HAT-P-11b} is a warm, Neptune-sized world orbiting an active orange dwarf star every 4.9 days, complete with powerful magnetic fields and starspots.

\subsection*{The Infrared Beacon of Planetary Evaporation}
For years, astronomers searched for escaping gas using ultraviolet light, which is heavily blocked by interstellar gas. In 2018, the Hubble Space Telescope and Spain's CARMENES spectrograph detected a brilliant signal in near-infrared light: a glowing cloud of excited \textbf{metastable helium gas} ($10830\,\text{\AA}$) streaming off the planet at \textbf{25,000 metric tons per second}, extending over $2.5$ times the radius of the planet into an elongated tail!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Metastable He I ($2^3S$) Populating Kinetics \& Transit Depth}

\subsection*{1. EUV Ionization \& Recombination Cascade Kinetics}
Neutral helium atoms are pumped into the long-lived metastable triplet state $\mathrm{He}(2^3S)$ (radiative lifetime $\tau \approx 2.2\,\text{hours}$) through photoionization of ground-state $\mathrm{He}(1^1S)$ by stellar extreme-UV radiation ($\lambda < 504\,\text{\AA}$) followed by radiative recombination:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dn_{2^3S}}{dt} = n_e n_{\text{He}^+} \alpha_{\text{eff}} - n_{2^3S} \left( \Phi_{\text{mid-UV}} \sigma_{\text{ion}} + n_e q_{\text{coll}} \right) = 0
\end{equation}
Active K-dwarf stars like HAT-P-11 provide the optimal spectral energy distribution: high EUV flux to ionize helium combined with low near-UV flux ($\lambda \sim 2600\,\text{\AA}$) to prevent mid-UV depopulation.

\subsection*{2. Hydrodynamic Mass Loss \& Triplet Line Absorption}
Driven by stellar EUV heating ($F_{\text{EUV}} \approx 1.2 \times 10^4\,\text{erg}/(\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{s})$), hydrodynamic escape carries mass into the exosphere:
\begin{equation}
\dot{M} = \frac{3 \epsilon F_{\text{EUV}} R_p^3}{4 G M_p K_{\text{tide}}} \approx 2.5 \times 10^{10}\,\text{g/s}
\end{equation}
producing an excess transit absorption depth of $\Delta\delta_{10830} = 1.08 \pm 0.05\,\%$ at the resolved He I triplet.

\subsection*{1. 3D Exospheric Cometary Tail Kinematics}
The resolved triplet lines show a net blueshift of $v_{\text{tail}} = -3.0 \pm 0.4\,\text{km/s}$ and an asymmetric red wing, demonstrating that stellar coronal wind ram pressure channels the escaping helium into a trailing magnetospheric tail extending out to $R_{\text{tail}} \approx 2.5\,R_p$.
