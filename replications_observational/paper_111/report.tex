\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Planet Where It Rains Iron}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 640 light-years away in the constellation Pisces, \textbf{WASP-76b} is an ultra-hot Jupiter tidally locked so close to its star that its year lasts only 1.8 Earth days.

\subsection*{Vaporized Metal by Day, Liquid Iron Rain by Night}
On WASP-76b's permanent dayside, searing temperatures soar above \textbf{4,300$^\circ$F (2,400$^\circ$C / 2,700 K)}—hot enough to vaporize solid metals into atmospheric gas! Super-fast 11,000 mph jet streams sweep this gaseous iron eastward toward the cooler nightside. As the iron vapor crosses the evening twilight zone into the 2,000$^\circ$F (1,400 K) nightside, it condenses into liquid iron droplets, literally causing **liquid iron rain** to fall out of the alien sky!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — High-Resolution Doppler Cross-Correlation}

\subsection*{1. Phase-Resolved Transmission Spectroscopy}
High-resolution spectrographs ($R = \lambda / \Delta\lambda \approx 140{,}000$) resolve individual atomic iron lines ($\text{Fe I}$). Cross-correlating observed spectra against synthetic templates isolates the planetary absorption signal as a function of orbital phase $\phi$:
\begin{equation}
v_{\text{obs}}(\phi) = v_{\text{sys}} + K_p \sin(2\pi \phi) + v_{\text{wind}}
\end{equation}
where the day-to-night eastward equatorial jet induces a net blueshift $v_{\text{wind}} \approx -11.0\,\text{km/s}$.

\subsection*{2. Thermal Condensation Equilibrium}
Gaseous iron condenses into liquid droplets when local partial pressure exceeds saturation vapor pressure $P_{\text{sat}}(T)$:
\begin{equation}
\log_{10} P_{\text{sat}} = -\frac{A}{T} + B
\end{equation}
At $T_{\text{cond}} \approx 1800\,\text{K}$, gaseous iron depletes to zero, causing the absorption signal to vanish at the morning limb ($\delta_{\text{Fe}} = 0.00\%$) while remaining prominent at the evening limb ($\delta_{\text{Fe}} = 0.45\%$).
