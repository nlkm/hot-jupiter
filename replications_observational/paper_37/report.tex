\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — Tasting the Salt of an Alien Ocean}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
When NASA's Cassini spacecraft flew past Saturn's icy moon \textbf{Enceladus}, its \textbf{Cosmic Dust Analyzer (CDA)} instrument scooped up thousands of microscopic ice grains orbiting in Saturn's glowing E-Ring. 

\subsection*{Salty Sea Spray}
Cassini discovered that roughly $99\%$ of the ice grains are made of pure frozen water vapor. But deep in the core of the plume, about $1\%$ of the grains are rich in \textbf{table salt} ($\mathrm{NaCl}$) and \textbf{baking soda} ($\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$). This proves the geysers are not just melting ice—they are erupting from a vast, alkaline, saltwater ocean in direct contact with a rocky seafloor!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Aerosol Bubble Bursting \& Salt Partitioning}

\subsection*{1. Liquid Aerosol Droplet Formation}
When gas bubbles rise through the liquid ocean and burst at the vacuum interface inside tiger stripe fissures, capillary waves launch tiny liquid droplets (aerosols) into the escaping vapor stream:
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{drop}} \approx 0.05 \left( \frac{\sigma_{\text{surface}}}{\rho_{\text{liq}} g} \right)^{1/2}
\end{equation}
Because these droplets freeze directly from bulk ocean water, their salt concentration $S_{\text{grain}} = 0.5 - 2.0\,\text{wt}\%$ matches the pristine salinity of the subsurface ocean.

\subsection*{2. Aerodynamic Grain Acceleration via Gas Drag}
Escaping water vapor accelerating through supersonic vents exerts Epstein aerodynamic drag on the ice grains:
\begin{equation}
m_{\text{grain}} \frac{dv}{dt} = \pi r_{\text{grain}}^2 \rho_{\text{gas}} (v_{\text{gas}} - v_{\text{grain}})^2 C_D
\end{equation}
Small grains ($r < 1\,\mu\mathrm{m}$) are accelerated past escape velocity ($v_{\text{esc}} = 239\,\text{m/s}$), escaping into orbit to feed Saturn's E-Ring, while larger grains fall back onto the south polar terrain.

\subsection*{1. Impact Ionization Saha-Langmuir Thermodynamics}
Upon high-velocity impact ($v_{\text{rel}} \approx 7 - 18\,\text{km/s}$) onto the rhodium target, the ice grain vaporizes into a dense impact plasma. The alkali ion ionization fraction $\alpha_i = n_i^+ / n_i^0$ is given by the Saha-Langmuir equation:
\begin{equation}
\frac{n_{\mathrm{Na}}^+}{n_{\mathrm{Na}}^0} = \frac{g_+}{g_0} \exp\left( \frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{work}} - I_{\mathrm{Na}}}{k_B T_{\mathrm{plasma}}} \right)
\end{equation}
With sodium's low ionization potential ($I_{\mathrm{Na}} = 5.14\,\text{eV}$), sodium atoms ionize with $> 90\%$ efficiency, producing the dominant $m/z = 23\,\text{u}$ ($\mathrm{Na}^+$) and $m/z = 63\,\text{u}$ ($\mathrm{Na_2OH}^+$) cluster peaks.
