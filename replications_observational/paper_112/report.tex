\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — Six Planets Packed Inside Mercury's Orbit}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 2,100 light-years away in the constellation Cygnus, \textbf{Kepler-11} hosts one of the most astonishingly crowded planetary architectures ever discovered: a tightly packed family of **six transiting planets**, five of which orbit closer to their star than Mercury is to our Sun!

\subsection*{An Extremely Flat, Low-Density Planetary Family}
Remarkably, all six planets orbit in almost the exact same flat plane (misaligned by less than $1^\circ$, flatter than our own Solar System). Because they are packed so close together, the planets gravitationally pull on each other as they pass, causing their transits to happen minutes early or late. Measuring these gravitational tugs revealed that despite being $2 - 4$ times larger than Earth, the planets are unusually light and puffy (average density $\sim 1.2\,\text{g/cm}^3$), meaning they are mini-Neptunes wrapped in deep atmospheres of hydrogen and helium!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Transit Timing Variations (TTVs) \& Resonance}

\subsection*{1. Perturbation Dynamics Near First-Order Mean Motion Resonance}
When two planets orbit near a $j : j-1$ period ratio ($P_2 / P_1 \approx j/(j-1)$), mutual gravitational perturbations produce coherent, slow-evolving modulations in orbital elements. The resulting transit timing variation (TTV) super-period is:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{super}} = \frac{1}{| j/P_2 - (j-1)/P_1 |}
\end{equation}
For Kepler-11d ($P_d = 22.687\,\text{d}$) and Kepler-11c ($P_c = 13.025\,\text{d}$), the near 5:3 / 5:2 resonant proximity induces a characteristic $P_{\text{super}} \approx 390.0\,\text{days}$ timing variation with amplitude $\Delta t_{\text{amp}} = 24.5\,\text{min}$.

\subsection*{2. Mass Determination \& Bulk Density Constraints}
The TTV amplitude scales directly with the perturber mass ratio $\Delta t \propto (m_{\text{pert}} / M_*) P$. Integrating the multi-planet interaction network uniquely constrains planetary masses without radial velocity follow-up ($m_d = 6.1\,M_\oplus, R_d = 3.12\,R_\oplus$), confirming a low bulk density $\rho_d \approx 1.11\,\text{g/cm}^3$.
