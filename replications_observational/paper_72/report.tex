\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Crown of the Red Planet}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Rising an unfathomable \textbf{13.2 miles (21.3 kilometers)} above the Martian plains, \textbf{Olympus Mons} is the largest volcano in the entire solar system—nearly three times taller than Mount Everest and spanning the size of the state of Arizona!

\subsection*{The Nested Giant Sinkholes at the Summit}
At the very peak of this colossal shield volcano sits an immense \textbf{50-mile-wide (80 km)} caldera complex. Rather than a single crater, it consists of six overlapping, nested circular collapse craters plunging up to \textbf{2 miles (3.2 km)} deep. Each time underground magma chambers drained during ancient basaltic eruptions, the unsupported volcanic roof collapsed inward like giant circular elevator pistons!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Piston Caldera Subsidence \& Chamber Evacuation}

\subsection*{1. Chamber Volume Deficit \& Piston Collapse}
When volume $\Delta V_{\text{magma}}$ erupts from a shallow magma reservoir at depth $d \approx 15\,\text{km}$, the overlying basaltic cylinder of radius $R_{\text{caldera}} \approx 40\,\text{km}$ collapses along ring faults:
\begin{equation}
\Delta z_{\text{subsidence}} = \frac{\Delta V_{\text{magma}}}{\pi R_{\text{caldera}}^2} \approx 3.2\,\text{km} \implies \Delta V_{\text{magma}} \approx 1.6 \times 10^4\,\text{km}^3
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Thin Elastic Plate Flexural Support}
The Martian lithosphere under Tharsis possesses extreme flexural rigidity $D = \frac{E T_e^3}{12(1 - \nu^2)} \approx 2.0 \times 10^{24}\,\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$ ($T_e \ge 100\,\text{km}$), allowing the colossal volcanic edifice ($M_{\text{volcano}} \approx 2 \times 10^{18}\,\text{kg}$) to stand for billions of years without isostatic subduction.

\subsection*{1. Magma Chamber Overpressure \& Ring Fault Initiation}
Ring fault initiation occurs when overpressure $\Delta P$ satisfies Andersonian fault mechanics at depth $d_{\text{chamber}}$:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{rr} - \sigma_{zz} \ge 2 c_0 \frac{\cos\phi_{\text{friction}}}{1 - \sin\phi_{\text{friction}}}
\end{equation}
producing near-vertical outward-dipping ring faults ($\theta_{\text{fault}} \approx 75 - 85^\circ$) that facilitate incremental episodic collapse.
