\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Cotton Candy Planet}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Orbiting its host star in just 5.7 days, \textbf{WASP-107b} is one of the puffiest, least dense planets ever discovered: it has the massive size of Jupiter ($94\%$ Jupiter's diameter) but only a tiny fraction of Jupiter's weight ($1/10\text{th}$ Jupiter's mass)—giving it the density of cotton candy or toasted marshmallow ($\bar{\rho} \approx 0.13\,\text{g/cm}^3$)!

\subsection*{Alien Smog and Sand Clouds}
Because its atmosphere is so light and puffy, the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) looked deep into its skies, discovering clouds made of microscopic silicate sand grains and detecting \textbf{sulfur dioxide ($\mathrm{SO_2}$)}—alien photochemical smog produced when starlight breaks down sulfur molecules!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Ultra-Low Gravity Scale Height \& Photochemistry}

\subsection*{1. Enormous Atmospheric Scale Height}
For $M_p = 30.5\,M_\oplus$ and $R_p = 0.94\,R_J \approx 6.7 \times 10^7\,\text{m}$, the surface gravity is exceptionally low:
\begin{equation}
g_{\text{surf}} = \frac{G M_p}{R_p^2} \approx 2.70\,\text{m/s}^2 \quad (0.28\,g_\oplus)
\end{equation}
At $T_{\text{eq}} \approx 740\,\text{K}$ in a hydrogen-dominated envelope ($\mu \approx 2.3$), the atmospheric scale height is:
\begin{equation}
H = \frac{k_B T}{\mu m_H g_{\text{surf}}} \approx \frac{1.38 \times 10^{-23} \times 740}{2.3 \times 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 2.70} \approx 980\,\text{km}
\end{equation}
Producing transmission absorption signals $\Delta\delta \sim 200 - 400\,\text{ppm}$ per scale height.

\subsection*{2. Photochemical $\mathrm{SO_2}$ Production}
Stellar Far-UV radiation ($h\nu > 5\,\text{eV}$) photolyzes hydrogen sulfide ($\mathrm{H_2S}$) and water ($\mathrm{H_2O}$):
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{H_2S} + h\nu \to \mathrm{HS} + \mathrm{H}, \quad \mathrm{H_2O} + h\nu \to \mathrm{OH} + \mathrm{H}
\end{equation}
Subsequent radical reactions $\mathrm{HS} + \mathrm{OH} \to \mathrm{SO} + \mathrm{H_2}$ and $\mathrm{SO} + \mathrm{OH} \to \mathrm{SO_2} + \mathrm{H}$ generate abundant $\mathrm{SO_2}$ ($f_{\mathrm{SO_2}} \approx 2 \times 10^{-5}$), producing prominent absorption features at $4.05\,\mu\text{m}$ and $8.6\,\mu\text{m}$.

\subsection*{1. Tidal Dissipation \& Envelope Inflation}

The non-zero orbital eccentricity ($e \approx 0.06$) drives continuous tidal dissipation in the planetary core and convective envelope:
\begin{equation}
\dot{E}_{\text{tide}} = \frac{21}{2} \frac{k_2}{Q} \frac{G M_\star^2 R_p^5 n e^2}{a^6} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{19}\,\text{W} \implies F_{\text{tide}} \approx 1.2\,\text{W/m}^2
\end{equation}
Preventing structural contraction and maintaining $R_p \approx 0.94\,R_J$ over a $5\,\text{Gyr}$ evolutionary baseline.
