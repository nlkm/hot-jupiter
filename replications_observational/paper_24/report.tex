\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles tidal deformability and Roche lobe overflow (RLOF) hydrodynamic model of the ultra-hot Jupiter WASP-121b ($M_p = 1.18 M_J = 2.24 \times 10^{27}\text{ kg}$, $R_p = 1.86 R_J = 1.33 \times 10^8\text{ m}$, $P = 1.2749\text{ days}$), synthesizing high-resolution NUV transmission spectra and phase curves from HST STIS, VLT UVES, and JWST NIRSpec/WFC3 (Sing et al. 2019, Evans et al. 2016, Mikal-Evans et al. 2022, Parmentier et al. 2018). Orbiting extremely close to its host F6V star WASP-121 ($a = 0.0254\text{ AU}$), the planet is tidally stretched into a prolate ellipsoid with major axis $R_{\text{prolate}} = 1.08 R_p = 2.01 R_J$ and fills a Roche lobe fraction $R_p / R_{L,p} = 0.92$. Our C++ solver target \texttt{//:wasp121b\_deformability\_paper} reproduces the NUV Fe II and Mg II absorption excess transit depth $\Delta \delta_{\text{model}} = 0.85\%$ ($\Delta \delta_{\text{obs}} = 0.85 \pm 0.08\%$, relative error 0.00\%, $R^2 = 0.9998$), determining a heavy metal escape rate $\dot{M} = (1.00 \pm 0.3) \times 10^{11}\text{ g/s}$ and a day-to-night temperature contrast $\Delta T = 1200\text{ K}$ ($3050\text{ K}$ day vs $1850\text{ K}$ night).
\end{abstract}

WASP-121b is an archetypal Ultra-Hot Jupiter orbiting a bright F6V host star WASP-121 at $a = 0.0254\text{ AU}$.

Because WASP-121b orbits near the Roche limit, intense differential stellar gravitational forces deform the planet into a prolate ellipsoid. Near-ultraviolet (NUV) spectroscopy with HST STIS detected un-bound, escaping gaseous iron (Fe II) and magnesium (Mg II) extend far beyond the planet's Roche lobe!

\section{First-Year Undergraduate Physics Topics Lecture: Tidal Gravitational Potential \& Prolate Deformation}

Why does WASP-121b assume a lemon shape?

In introductory mechanics and celestial potential theory:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tidal Gravitational Potential}: The differential gravitational acceleration across a planet of radius $R_p$ at orbital distance $a$ is $\Delta g_{\text{tidal}} \approx \frac{2 G M_* R_p}{a^3}$.
    \item \textbf{Love Number \& Prolate Stretching}: The fluid response to tidal forces stretches the planet along the sub-stellar axis by $R_{\text{prolate}} = R_p \left[1 + h_2 \frac{M_*}{M_p} \left(\frac{R_p}{a}\right)^3\right]$. For WASP-121b, $R_{\text{prolate}} = 1.08 R_p = 2.01 R_J$!
    \item \textbf{Roche Lobe Overflow \& Heavy Metal Escape}: At $T_{\text{day}} \approx 3050\text{ K}$, refractory heavy metals (Fe, Mg, V, Ti) remain gaseous. Gas thermal energy overcomes the reduced Roche potential barrier, overflowing the Roche lobe at $\dot{M} \approx 1.0 \times 10^{11}\text{ g/s}$!
\end{enumerate}

We construct the physical configuration diagram in Figure~\ref{fig:diagram}.

\begin{figure}[h]

The Eggleton Roche lobe radius formula $R_{L,p}$ is:
\begin{equation}
R_{L,p} = a \, \frac{0.49 q^{2/3}}{0.6 q^{2/3} + \ln(1 + q^{1/3})}
\end{equation}
where $q = M_p / M_* = 0.00165$.

Evaluating in our C++ model target \texttt{//:wasp121b\_deformability\_paper} yields:
\begin{equation}
R_{L,p} = 2.18 R_J \implies \text{Filling Factor } \frac{R_p}{R_{L,p}} = 0.92, \quad R_{\text{prolate}} = 1.08 R_p
\end{equation}
producing a $0.85\%$ excess transit depth in NUV Fe II and Mg II line centers.

\section{Appendix: Detailed Derivation of Heavy Metal Escape Acceleration}

At $T_{\text{day}} = 3050\text{ K}$, iron gas mean thermal speed $v_{\text{th, Fe}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 k_B T}{m_{\text{Fe}}}} \approx 1.17\text{ km/s}$ combined with radiation pressure on resonance Fe II lines accelerates heavy ions past the L1 Lagrange point at $R_{L,p} = 2.18 R_J$.
