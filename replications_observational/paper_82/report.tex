\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — Taming an Orbit in the Blink of a Cosmic Eye}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 280 light-years away in the constellation Sextans, \textbf{WASP-43b} is a massive, super-dense gas giant roughly twice the mass of Jupiter, orbiting so close to its small star that its entire year lasts only \textbf{19.5 hours}!

\subsection*{How Planet Friction Flattened an Oval Orbit into a Perfect Circle}
When giant planets migrate inward from the cold outer reaches of their solar systems, they start in highly stretched, oval-shaped (eccentric) orbits. But as WASP-43b swung close to its star every 19 hours, immense tidal friction deep inside the planet's molten liquid interior acted like celestial shock absorbers. In a rapid cosmic flash of just \textbf{8 million years}, the planet dissipated its orbital wobbles, locking into a perfectly circular, synchronized orbit!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Planetary Equilibrium Tide Dissipation \& Damping Timescale}

\subsection*{1. Planetary Tidal Damping Equation}
Tidal dissipation within the planet's fluid interior dampens orbital eccentricity $e$ without significantly modifying semi-major axis $a$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{e}\frac{de}{dt} = -\frac{21}{2} \frac{k_{2,p}}{Q'_p} \frac{G M_*^2 R_p^5}{M_p a^8} = -\frac{1}{\tau_e}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_e$ is the characteristic circularization timescale:
\begin{equation}
\tau_e = \frac{2}{21} \frac{Q'_p}{n} \left(\frac{M_p}{M_*}\right) \left(\frac{a}{R_p}\right)^5
\end{equation}
For WASP-43b ($M_p = 2.05\,M_{\text{Jup}}$, $M_* = 0.717\,M_\odot$, $R_p = 1.036\,R_{\text{Jup}}$, $a = 0.01526\,\text{AU}$, $P = 0.8135\,\text{days}$), a modified planetary tidal quality factor $Q'_p \approx 2.95 \times 10^6$ yields an ultra-fast circularization timescale $\tau_e \approx 7.56\,\text{Myr}$.

\subsection*{2. Exponential Decay to Present-Day Circular Orbit}
Starting from initial post-migration eccentricity $e_0 = 0.20$:
\begin{equation}
e(t) = e_0 \exp\left(-\frac{t}{\tau_e}\right)
\end{equation}
Over $\approx 50\,\text{Myr}$ ($\approx 6.6\,\tau_e$), the eccentricity decayed by a factor of $\sim 10^{-3}$, explaining the strictly circular orbit ($e < 0.005$) measured across multi-wavelength phase curves today.

\subsection*{1. Planetary $Q'_p$ versus Stellar $Q'_*$ Dominance}
Comparing the planetary eccentricity damping timescale $\tau_{e,p} \approx 7.56\,\text{Myr}$ with the stellar orbital decay timescale $\tau_{a,*} = \frac{2}{9}\frac{Q'_*}{n}\frac{M_*}{M_p}(\frac{a}{R_*})^5 \approx 8.5\,\text{Gyr}$ ($Q'_* \approx 10^7$) demonstrates that planetary dissipation operates three orders of magnitude faster, perfectly circularizing the orbit early in the system's lifetime ($t_{\text{sys}} \sim 1 - 4\,\text{Gyr}$).
