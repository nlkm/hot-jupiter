\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Stealth Ocean Inside the Death Star Moon}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Saturn's small icy moon \textbf{Mimas} (123 miles / 198 km radius) looks like the ultimate frozen, dead world, famously resembling the ``Death Star'' due to its colossal Herschel impact crater.

\subsection*{A Secret Liquid Ocean Born Just Yesterday}
Because Mimas is heavily cratered with zero geysers or surface cracks, planetary scientists assumed it was frozen solid to its core. But when NASA's Cassini spacecraft tracked Mimas's orbital wobbles with pinpoint precision, it discovered that the moon wobbles back and forth twice as much as a solid body should! This extra wobble proves that Mimas's outer ice crust ($15\,\text{miles / }25\,\text{km}$ thick) is completely detached and floating over a **global liquid water ocean 28 miles (45 km) deep** that melted just 15 million years ago due to tidal friction from Saturn!

\section*{Level 2: For the Undergrad — Forced Longitudinal Libration \& Shell Decoupling}

\subsection*{1. Forced Libration Dynamics}
Due to its orbital eccentricity ($e = 0.0202$), Mimas experiences periodic gravitational torques from Saturn. In the presence of a global subsurface ocean, the rigid ice shell of thickness $z_{\text{shell}} \approx 25\,\text{km}$ decouples from the silicate core, reducing the effective moment of inertia $C_{\text{shell}} \ll C_{\text{tot}}$. The forced physical libration amplitude is:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{\text{lib}} = \frac{2 e}{1 - (C_{\text{shell}} / 3(B - A))} \approx 49.3\,\text{arcsec}
\end{equation}
For a solid interior, $\theta_{\text{lib}} \approx 24.5\,\text{arcsec}$, ruling out a solid body at $>5\sigma$.

\subsection*{2. Tidal Heating \& Young Ocean Evolution}
Tidal dissipation power $P_{\text{tide}} \approx \frac{21}{2} \frac{k_2}{Q} \frac{G M_S^2 R_M^5 n e^2}{a^6} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{10}\,\text{W}$ ($12\,\text{GW}$) thinned the ancient ice shell within the past $t_{\text{age}} < 25\,\text{Myr}$, before fractures reached the surface.
