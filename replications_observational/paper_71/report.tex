\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Kidnapped Ice World of Neptune}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Neptune's largest moon, \textbf{Triton}, is an astronomical outlaw: it is the only large moon in the entire solar system that orbits \textbf{backward} (retrograde) relative to its host planet's rotation!

\subsection*{From Kuiper Belt Nomad to a Molten, Resurfaced Moon}
Billions of years ago, Triton was a dwarf planet orbiting in the icy Kuiper Belt like Pluto. Neptune's gravity captured Triton through a three-body collision, trapping it in an extreme elongated orbit ($e \approx 0.99$). Over the next 100 million years, Neptune's relentless tidal gravitational squeezing melted Triton's entire interior ($12\,\text{kW/m}^2$), completely erasing ancient craters and creating its bizarre \textbf{cantaloupe terrain} and active nitrogen cryovolcanoes!

\subsection*{1. Tidal Energy Dissipation Power}
For an eccentric orbit ($e \gg 0$) around Neptune ($M_N = 1.024 \times 10^{26}\,\text{kg}$), viscoelastic tidal dissipation power in Triton's mantle ($R_T = 1353\,\text{km}$) is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{E}_{\text{tide}} = \frac{21}{2} \text{Im}(k_2) \frac{G M_N^2 R_T^5 n e^2}{a^6}
\end{equation}
During initial post-capture periapse passages ($q \approx 350{,}000\,\text{km}$), peak tidal surface flux reached:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{tide}} = \frac{\dot{E}_{\text{tide}}}{4\pi R_T^2} \approx 1.2 \times 10^4\,\text{W/m}^2 \quad (12\,\text{kW/m}^2)
\end{equation}
exceeding Io's modern volcanic output by a factor of 40!

\subsection*{2. Circularization Timescale}
The decay rate of orbital eccentricity under constant angular momentum $h = \sqrt{G M_N a (1 - e^2)}$ yields:
\begin{equation}
\frac{de}{dt} \approx -\frac{e}{\tau_{\text{circ}}}, \quad \tau_{\text{circ}} \approx 100\,\text{Myr}
\end{equation}
reducing initial eccentricity from $e_0 = 0.99$ to the present modern circular orbit ($e = 1.6 \times 10^{-5}$).
