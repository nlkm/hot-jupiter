\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Spinning Spinning-Top Asteroid Propelled by Sunlight}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Asteroid \textbf{(162173) Ryugu} is a half-mile-wide ($900\,\text{meters}$) spinning rubble pile shaped like a giant celestial spinning top, composed of carbon-rich organic boulders and water-bearing clay minerals.

\subsection*{How Afternoon Heat Pushes Asteroids Through the Solar System}
As Ryugu absorbs harsh sunlight on its dayside and rotates backward (retrograde), its warm rocky surface radiates infrared heat into space during its asteroid ``afternoon.'' This uneven thermal radiation acts like a continuous, ultra-gentle photon rocket thruster (the \textbf{Yarkovsky effect}), steadily pushing Ryugu's entire orbit inward toward the Sun at a rate of \textbf{700 feet (215 meters) every single year}!

\subsection*{1. Diurnal Thermal Lag \& Force Vector}
For a spherical body of radius $R = 448\,\text{m}$, thermal inertia $\Gamma = \sqrt{\rho c k} \approx 225\,\text{J}/(\text{m}^2\text{K}\cdot\text{s}^{1/2})$, and retrograde spin obliquity $\gamma = 171.6^\circ$, the thermal lag angle $\Theta$ produces an along-track force:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{Yark}} = \frac{4}{9} \pi R^2 \frac{\Phi_0}{c} \left( \frac{1\,\text{AU}}{a} \right)^2 \alpha \cos\gamma \sin\Theta
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Secular Semi-Major Axis Drift Rate}
Applying Gauss's perturbation equations over an orbital period $P = 1.30\,\text{years}$ ($a = 1.1896\,\text{AU}$):
\begin{equation}
\frac{da}{dt} = \frac{2}{n a} F_{\text{along-track}} \approx -215.0\,\text{m/year} \quad (-1.44 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{AU/Myr})
\end{equation}
Over a century ($100\,\text{years}$), Ryugu migrates inward by $\Delta a = -21.5\,\text{km}$, producing a cumulative quadratic along-track orbital timing advance $\Delta \lambda \approx 120\,\text{km}$.

Hayabusa2 TIR measurements constrain Ryugu's surface thermal inertia to $\Gamma = 225 \pm 45\,\text{J}/(\text{m}^2\text{K}\cdot\text{s}^{1/2})$, indicating extremely porous carbonaceous chondrite boulders ($p \approx 40 - 50\%$):
\begin{equation}
\Gamma = \sqrt{\rho_{\text{bulk}} C_p \kappa_{\text{eff}}}, \quad \kappa_{\text{eff}} \approx 0.10\,\text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}
\end{equation}
This moderate thermal inertia maximizes the diurnal thermal lag $\sin\Theta$, driving Ryugu from the main asteroid belt into the near-Earth population within $\sim 100\,\text{Myr}$.
