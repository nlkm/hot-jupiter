\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Smoggy Steam World}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 48 light-years away, \textbf{GJ 1214b} is the prototypical "water world" or "sub-Neptune" exoplanet, about $2.7$ times the diameter of Earth. For over a decade, telescopes trying to measure its atmosphere saw only a flat, featureless line.

\subsection*{High-Altitude Hydrocarbon Smog}
When the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) watched GJ 1214b complete an entire 38-hour orbit in infrared light, it finally cracked the case! The planet is enveloped in a dense layer of high-altitude photochemical smog (similar to Titan's orange haze) suspended in an atmosphere rich in heavy molecules like water vapor, carbon dioxide, and methane.

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Aerosol Extinction \& Atmospheric Scale Height}

\subsection*{1. Atmospheric Scale Height \& High Metallicity}
The apparent transmission spectrum transit depth variations $\Delta\delta$ scale with the atmospheric scale height $H$:
\begin{equation}
H = \frac{k_B T}{\mu m_H g_{\text{surf}}}
\end{equation}
For a pure $\mathrm{H_2}$ atmosphere ($\mu \approx 2.3$), $H \approx 200\,\text{km}$, which would produce prominent molecular absorption features ($\Delta\delta \approx 0.1\%$). For a heavy water-rich atmosphere ($Z \approx 500\times$ solar, $\mu \ge 12$), the scale height shrinks to $H < 40\,\text{km}$, flattening spectral transmission features.

\subsection*{2. Rayleigh-Mie Aerosol Scattering Slope}
Sub-micron hydrocarbon aerosol grains ($a_{\text{haze}} \approx 0.05\,\mu\text{m}$) introduce optical extinction scaling as:
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\text{ext}}(\lambda) = \kappa_0 \left( \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_0} \right)^{-\alpha}
\end{equation}
With $\alpha \approx 4$ in the Rayleigh regime ($2\pi a / \lambda \ll 1$), which transitions to grey geometric cross-section in the infrared ($\alpha \to 0$), capping transmission depth across HST WFC3 wavelengths.

\subsection*{1. Equatorial Superrotation \& Hotspot Offset}
Coupling primitive hydrodynamic equations with non-equilibrium aerosol microphysics yields the Matsuno-Gill wave resonance driving an equatorial superrotating jet:
\begin{equation}
\bar{u}_{\text{jet}} \approx \frac{R_p}{\tau_{\text{wave}}} \approx 850\,\text{m/s}
\end{equation}
Advecting the dayside thermal peak eastward by $\Delta\phi = +57^\circ \pm 10^\circ$, in exact agreement with JWST MIRI phase curve retrievals.
