\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Mysterious Interstellar Visitor}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
In October 2017, astronomers discovered \textbf{1I/'Oumuamua}, the very first macroscopic object ever detected entering our Solar System from the vast spaces between the stars. As this elongated, reddish messenger sped away from the Sun, high-precision telescopes revealed that it was speeding up faster than gravity alone could explain!

\subsection*{The Invisible Rocket Engine of Cryogenic Ice}
Because no visible dust or cometary gas was seen, some wondered if it was an alien lightsail. However, astrophysics provides a natural first-principles answer: 'Oumuamua is a piece of cryogenic nitrogen ($\mathrm{N_2}$) or molecular hydrogen ($\mathrm{H_2}$) ice born in an alien exoplanetary system. As sunlight warmed the surface, the evaporating pure gas acted as an invisible rocket thruster, smoothly pushing the object forward without releasing detectable dust!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Cryogenic Sublimation Rocket Thrust}

\subsection*{1. Sublimation Flux \& Gas Dynamics}
At heliocentric distance $r$, solar energy conservation for a sublimating cryogenic volatile ice with latent heat $L_{\text{sub}}$ and albedo $A_0$ is:
\begin{equation}
(1 - A_0) \frac{L_\odot}{4\pi r^2} \cos\theta = \epsilon \sigma_{\text{SB}} T^4 + Z(r) L_{\text{sub}}
\end{equation}
For highly volatile ices ($\mathrm{N_2}$ or $\mathrm{H_2}$), sublimation dominates radiative cooling, yielding mass-loss flux:
\begin{equation}
Z(r) \approx \frac{(1 - A_0) L_\odot}{4\pi r^2 L_{\text{sub}}} \propto r^{-2}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2. Non-Gravitational Acceleration Equation}
The rocket recoil acceleration $a_{\text{ng}}$ imparted by the escaping gas at thermal exhaust velocity $v_{\text{th}} = \sqrt{8 k_B T / (\pi m_{\text{mol}})}$ with anisotropic beaming efficiency $f_{\text{aniso}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
a_{\text{ng}}(r) = \frac{f_{\text{aniso}} Z(r) v_{\text{th}} A_{\text{cross}}}{M_{\text{body}}} = \frac{3 f_{\text{aniso}} Z(r) v_{\text{th}}}{4 R_{\text{eff}} \rho_{\text{bulk}}}
\end{equation}
For an ultra-porous nitrogen ice matrix ($\rho \approx 300\,\text{kg/m}^3$, $\phi \approx 0.75$, $R_{\text{eff}} \approx 100\,\text{m}$), at $r = 1.4\,\text{AU}$, $a_{\text{ng}} \approx 2.5 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{m/s}^2$, exactly matching observed astrometric orbital departures.

\subsection*{1. Outgassing Spin-Up Torque \& Tensile Cohesion}
Anisotropic gas venting produces a spin-up torque $\mathcal{T}_{\text{outgas}} = \xi_{\text{arm}} R_{\text{eff}} \cdot F_{\text{thrust}}$. The rotational angular velocity $\omega(t)$ accelerates as:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\omega}{dt} = \frac{\mathcal{T}_{\text{outgas}}}{I_{\text{spin}}} \approx \frac{5 \xi_{\text{arm}} f_{\text{aniso}} Z(r) v_{\text{th}}}{R_{\text{eff}} \rho_{\text{bulk}} (a/b)}
\end{equation}
To resist rotational centrifugal fission ($\sigma_{\text{cent}} = \frac{1}{4} \rho \omega^2 a_{\text{long}}^2$), the required tensile cohesion is $\sigma_{\text{tensile}} \ge 8.5\,\text{Pa}$.
