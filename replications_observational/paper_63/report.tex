\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Shrouded Mystery World}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Located 48 light-years away in the constellation Ophiuchus, \textbf{GJ 1214b} is the prototype "super-Earth" ($2.74\,R_\oplus$, $8.17\,M_\oplus$). For over a decade, telescopes like Hubble tried to peer into its atmosphere, but found only a featureless, completely flat "chalkboard" spectrum!

\subsection*{How JWST Pierced the Smog}
Using the *James Webb Space Telescope*, astronomers tracked GJ 1214b during its entire 38-hour orbit in thermal infrared light. JWST revealed that the planet is blanketed by a dense, shiny, pearlescent smog layer composed of sub-micron organic hydrocarbon aerosols ($\sim 0.05\,\mu\mathrm{m}$). Even more remarkable, the atmosphere is heavy and metallic ($>500\times$ Solar metallicity), rich in water vapor and carbon dioxide rather than lightweight hydrogen!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Aerosol Extinction \& Atmospheric Metallicity}

\subsection*{1. Rayleigh-Mie Slant Optical Depth \& Flat Transmission}
Transmission spectra probe the high-altitude slant optical depth $\tau_{\text{slant}}(\lambda) = 1$.
The slant path enhancement factor is $\xi = \sqrt{2\pi R_p / H} \approx 40$.
The slant extinction coefficient $\kappa_{\text{ext}}(\lambda) = \kappa_{\text{gas}}(\lambda) + \kappa_{\text{haze}}(\lambda)$ is dominated by sub-micron aerosols ($r_h \approx 0.05\,\mu\text{m}$):
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\text{haze}}(\lambda) = \xi \int Q_{\text{ext}}(r_h, \lambda) \pi r_h^2 n_h(z) dz \gg \tau_{\text{gas}}(\lambda)
\end{equation}
completely masking molecular spectral features across optical and near-IR bands.

\subsection*{2. Thermal Phase Curve Amplitude \& Day-Night Contrast}
In the mid-infrared ($5 - 12\,\mu\text{m}$), JWST probes thermal emission directly.
The planet's observed brightness temperature varies from $T_{\text{day}} = 553\,\text{K}$ on the dayside to $T_{\text{night}} = 437\,\text{K}$ on the nightside.
The thermal flux contrast ratio is:
\begin{equation}
\frac{F_p}{F_\star}(\phi) \approx \left(\frac{R_p}{R_\star}\right)^2 \frac{B_\lambda(T_b(\phi))}{B_\lambda(T_\star)} \approx 200 - 300\,\text{ppm}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{1. Atmospheric Mean Molecular Weight \& Metallicity Constraints}
The relatively large day-night temperature difference ($\Delta T = 116 \pm 20\,\text{K}$) and moderate phase offset ($\Delta \theta_{\text{east}} \approx 18^\circ$) require low equatorial wind speeds and inefficient heat recirculation, confirming high atmospheric mean molecular weight ($\mu \ge 5\,\text{g/mol}$) corresponding to $Z \approx 500\times\,\text{solar}$.
