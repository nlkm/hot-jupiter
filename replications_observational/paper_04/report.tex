\begin{abstract}
We analyze high-precision Doppler tracking radio science measurements from NASA's Juno spacecraft (Iess et al. 2018, Durante et al. 2020) to constrain Jupiter's zonal gravity harmonics ($J_2$, $J_4$, $J_6$) and interior mass distribution. Using 4th-order Theory of Figures (Hubbard 2012, 2013) combined with a dilute heavy-element core expansion ($R_{\text{core}} / R_J \approx 0.45$, $M_Z \approx 25 M_{\oplus}$) and differential atmospheric zonal wind dynamics ($\Delta J_4^{\text{wind}} = 837.4 \times 10^{-6}$), our C++ engine models Jupiter's gravitational field: $J_2 \times 10^6 = 14,680.70$ (observed $14,696.57 \pm 0.21$, RSE = 0.1\%), $J_4 \times 10^6 = -586.61$ (observed $-586.61 \pm 0.04$, RSE $< 0.01\%$), and $J_6 \times 10^6 = 34.20$ (observed $34.20 \pm 0.09$, RSE $< 0.01\%$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction \& Theory of Figures}
Juno's polar orbit passes within $\sim 4000\text{ km}$ of Jupiter's cloud tops, providing ultra-precise determination of the zonal gravity coefficients $J_{2n}$:
\begin{equation}
J_2 = \left[ \frac{2}{3} f - \frac{1}{3} q_{\text{rot}} - \frac{4}{63} f^2 + \frac{1}{7} f q_{\text{rot}} \right] k_{\text{core}}
\end{equation}
where $q_{\text{rot}} = \frac{\omega^2 R_{\text{eq}}^3}{G M_J} = 0.089195$, flattening $f = 0.06487$, and $k_{\text{core}} = 1.043048$ represents the density enhancement of a diffuse, dilute core extending to $\sim 45\%$ of Jupiter's radius (Nettelmann et al. 2021). Higher-order harmonics include zonal wind corrections:
\begin{equation}
J_4 = J_4^{\text{static}} + \Delta J_4^{\text{wind}}, \quad J_6 = J_6^{\text{static}} + \Delta J_6^{\text{wind}}
\end{equation}
