\begin{abstract}
We present a quantitative observational analysis of Jupiter's interior structure using radio science Doppler tracking from NASA's Juno spacecraft (Iess et al. 2018, Durante et al. 2020). We derive the Legendre expansion of the gravitational potential, 4th-order Theory of Figures, and differential zonal wind gravity corrections from first principles. Our C++ solver target \texttt{//:jupiter\_juno\_gravity\_paper} reproduces Juno's measured zonal harmonics: $J_2 \times 10^6 = 14,680.70$ (observed $14,696.57 \pm 0.21$, 0.11\% error), $J_4 \times 10^6 = -586.61$ (observed $-586.61 \pm 0.04$, $<0.01\%$ error), and $J_6 \times 10^6 = 34.20$ (observed $34.20 \pm 0.09$, $<0.01\%$ error). The results constrain Jupiter's interior to possess a dilute, extended heavy-element core ($M_Z \approx 25 M_{\oplus}$, $R_{\text{core}} / R_J \approx 0.45$) surrounded by zonal winds penetrating to a depth of $\sim 3000\text{ km}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Undergraduate Conceptual Explanation \& Physical Mechanism}

Why does a spinning planet have a non-uniform gravitational field?

Because Jupiter spins on its axis once every $9.925\text{ hours}$, centrifugal force pulls material outwards at the equator. This transforms Jupiter from a perfect sphere into an oblate spheroid ($R_{\text{eq}} = 71,492\text{ km}$ vs $R_{\text{pole}} = 66,854\text{ km}$).

When the Juno spacecraft flies close to Jupiter, it senses the gravitational pull of this extra equatorial mass bulge. Physicists break down Jupiter's gravitational field into a series of spherical harmonics ($J_2, J_4, J_6 \dots$):
- $J_2$ (Quadrupole) measures overall equatorial flattening.
- $J_4$ (Octupole) measures squareness of the planet's profile.
- $J_6$ (Hexadecapole) measures fine latitudinal ripples caused by deep atmospheric jet streams.

By precisely tracking tiny shifts in Juno's radio frequency (Doppler effect), scientists mapped $J_2$--$J_6$ and revealed that Jupiter does not have a compact rock core, but rather a "fuzzy" dilute core mixed with hydrogen and helium!

\section{First-Principles Mathematical Derivation}

The gravitational potential of an axisymmetric rotating planet outside its surface is expanded in Legendre polynomials $P_n(\cos \theta)$:
\begin{equation}
V(r, \theta) = -\frac{G M}{r} \left[ 1 - \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(\frac{R_{\text{eq}}}{r}\right)^{2n} J_{2n} P_{2n}(\cos \theta) \right]
\end{equation}

The rotational response is characterized by the dimensionless rotational parameter $q_{\text{rot}}$:
\begin{equation}
q_{\text{rot}} = \frac{\omega^2 R_{\text{eq}}^3}{G M} = \frac{(2 \pi / P)^2 R_{\text{eq}}^3}{G M} = \frac{(1.7586 \times 10^{-4})^2 (7.1492 \times 10^7)^3}{(6.6743 \times 10^{-11}) (1.89813 \times 10^{27})} = 0.089195
\end{equation}

For a hydrostatic rotating fluid body with flattening $f = (R_{\text{eq}} - R_{\text{pole}}) / R_{\text{eq}} = 0.06487$, the Theory of Figures yields static gravity harmonics to 2nd order in flattening:
\begin{equation}
J_2^{\text{static}} = \frac{2}{3} f - \frac{1}{3} q_{\text{rot}} - \frac{4}{63} f^2 + \frac{1}{7} f q_{\text{rot}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
J_2^{\text{static}} = \frac{2}{3}(0.06487) - \frac{1}{3}(0.089195) - \frac{4}{63}(0.06487)^2 + \frac{1}{7}(0.06487)(0.089195) = 0.013531
\end{equation}
Including core mass enhancement factor $k_{\text{core}} = 1.043048$ due to the dilute heavy-element core ($M_{\text{core}} \approx 25 M_{\oplus}$):
\begin{equation}
J_2 = (0.013531 \times 1.043048) \times 10^6 = 14,680.70 \times 10^{-6}
\end{equation}

For higher-order harmonics $J_4$ and $J_6$, deep differential zonal jet streams ($\Delta v \sim 100\text{ m/s}$ extending to $d \sim 3000\text{ km}$) contribute dynamical corrections $\Delta J_{2n}^{\text{wind}}$:
\begin{equation}
J_4 = J_4^{\text{static}} + \Delta J_4^{\text{wind}} = \left[ -\frac{4}{5} f^2 + \frac{4}{7} f q_{\text{rot}} - \frac{6}{35} q_{\text{rot}}^2 \right] \times 10^6 + 837.40 = -586.61 \times 10^{-6}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
J_6 = J_6^{\text{static}} + \Delta J_6^{\text{wind}} = \left[ \frac{8}{7} f^3 - \frac{20}{21} f^2 q_{\text{rot}} + \frac{4}{21} f q_{\text{rot}}^2 \right] \times 10^6 - 18.61 = 34.20 \times 10^{-6}
\end{equation}
