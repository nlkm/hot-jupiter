\section*{Level 1: For the Non-Scientist — The Planet Where It Rains Liquid Iron}

\subsection*{The Big Picture}
Imagine a world so close to its star that daytime temperatures soar past $4,300^\circ\text{F}$ ($2,400^\circ\text{C}$)—hot enough to vaporize solid metal into gas! This is \textbf{WASP-76b}, an ultra-hot gas giant located 640 light-years away in the constellation Pisces.

\subsection*{Liquid Iron Rain}
Because the planet is tidally locked (one side always faces the star), iron boils into vapor on the blazing day side. Howling supersonic winds blow this iron vapor toward the night side. As the metal-rich air crosses into the freezing darkness ($-1,500^\circ\text{C}$), the temperature plummets below iron's condensation point. The iron gas condenses into clouds and pours down as \textbf{liquid iron rain}!

\section*{Level 2: For the First-Year Undergrad — Clausius-Clapeyron Vapor Pressure \& Cold Trapping}

\subsection*{1. Iron Vapor Phase Equilibrium}
The equilibrium vapor pressure of atomic iron is given by the Clausius-Clapeyron equation:
\begin{equation}
\ln P_{\text{sat}}(\mathrm{Fe}) = -\frac{\Delta H_{\text{sub}}}{R_g T} + C_0
\end{equation}
With $\Delta H_{\text{sub}} \approx 415\,\text{kJ/mol}$, at the substellar point ($T_{\text{day}} = 2500\,\text{K}$), $P_{\text{sat}} \approx 10^{-2}\,\text{bar}$, making iron gas a major atmospheric constituent. On the nightside ($T_{\text{night}} = 1400\,\text{K}$), $P_{\text{sat}}$ collapses to $< 10^{-8}\,\text{bar}$, driving total condensation into liquid iron droplets.

\subsection*{2. Asymmetric Cross-Correlation Doppler Signals}
During transit, starlight filters through the planet's atmospheric limb. The observed absorption signal across the planetary disk is:
\begin{equation}
\Delta F(\lambda, t) = \iint_{\text{annulus}} \left( 1 - e^{-\tau(\lambda, \theta)} \right) I_\star(\theta)\,dA
\end{equation}
Because iron vapor only exists on the eastward-flowing evening limb ($\theta > 0$), high-resolution spectrographs detect strong Doppler-shifted $\mathrm{Fe\,I}$ absorption during ingress, which vanishes during egress as the morning limb crosses the stellar disk.

\subsection*{1. Advection-Condensation Time-Scale Balance}
The steady-state distribution of atomic iron mixing ratio $\chi_{\mathrm{Fe}}$ is governed by the zonal advection-diffusion-condensation equation:
\begin{equation}
u \frac{\partial \chi_{\mathrm{Fe}}}{\partial x} + w \frac{\partial \chi_{\mathrm{Fe}}}{\partial z} = -\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{Fe}} - \chi_{\mathrm{sat}}(T)}{\tau_{\mathrm{cond}}} - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(v_{\mathrm{settle}} \chi_{\mathrm{cond}})
\end{equation}
Where the supersonic jet speed $u \approx 5.3\,\text{km/s}$ advects iron across the evening terminator before condensation occurs ($\tau_{\mathrm{dyn}} < \tau_{\mathrm{cond}}$). On the nightside, rapid droplet nucleation and gravitational settling ($v_{\mathrm{settle}} \approx 10\,\text{m/s}$) completely sequester iron below the observable photosphere ($\tau_{\mathrm{cond}} \ll \tau_{\mathrm{dyn}}$).
