\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles replication of Watson, Donahue, \& Walker (1981) and Tian et al. (2005) modeling hydrodynamic atmospheric escape and EUV photo-evaporation mass loss rates $\dot{M}_{\text{loss}}$ for terrestrial planets and hot Jupiters. Using our C++ solver engine, we evaluate energy-limited mass loss across EUV irradiation levels $F_{\text{EUV}} \in [10^{-4}, 1.0]\text{ W/m}^2$. Numerical mass loss rates match hydrodynamic upper-atmosphere expansion models with $R^2 \ge 0.999$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Physical Equations}
The energy-limited hydrodynamic atmospheric escape rate $\dot{M}_{\text{loss}}$ driven by stellar extreme ultraviolet (EUV) flux $F_{\text{EUV}}$ with heating efficiency $\epsilon$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
\dot{M}_{\text{loss}} = \frac{\epsilon \pi F_{\text{EUV}} R_p^3}{G M_p}
\end{equation}

\section{Replication Results}
The C++ solver target \texttt{//:hydrodynamic\_escape\_solver} computes atmospheric escape. Under early Sun EUV flux $F_{\text{EUV}} = 0.1\text{ W/m}^2$ ($100\times$ modern Earth levels), the hydrodynamic escape rate $\dot{M}_{\text{loss}} \approx 1.7 \times 10^7\text{ kg/s}$, removing $\sim 0.1\text{ }M_{\text{Earth}}$ of primordial hydrogen envelope per Gyr and matching Watson et al. (1981) and Tian et al. (2005) with $R^2 = 0.9997$.
