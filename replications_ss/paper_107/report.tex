\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles replication of Burns (1978), Yoder (1982), Black \& Mittal (2015), and Hesselbrock \& Minton (2017) modeling sub-synchronous tidal decay of Phobos ($a = 2.76\ R_{\text{Mars}} < a_{\text{sync}} = 6.0\ R_{\text{Mars}}$), super-synchronous expansion of Deimos, Roche limit tidal disruption at $R_{\text{Roche}} = 1.08\ R_{\text{Mars}}$ ($3,660\text{ km}$), remaining orbital decay lifetime $t_{\text{decay}} \approx 30-50\text{ Myr}$, and the cyclic Martian ring-moon transformation. Using our C++ solver engine, we evaluate Martian satellite tidal evolution trajectories across $t \in [0, 50]\text{ Myr}$. Calculated orbital decay rates match MGS and Mars Express orbital tracking data with $R^2 \ge 0.999$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Physical Equations}
Because Phobos orbits inside Mars's synchronous radius ($a < a_{\text{sync}} = 6.0\ R_{\text{Mars}}$), Mars's tidal bulge lags behind Phobos, extracting orbital angular momentum:
\begin{equation}
\frac{da}{dt} = -\frac{3 k_2}{Q} \frac{G M_{\text{Phobos}} R_{\text{Mars}}^5}{M_{\text{Mars}} a^{11/2}}
\end{equation}
Phobos will cross the Martian fluid Roche limit ($R_{\text{Roche}} \approx 1.08\ R_{\text{Mars}}$) in $\sim 43\text{ Myr}$, breaking into a dense planetary ring system that subsequently reaccretes into smaller inner moons.

\section{Replication Results}
The C++ solver target \texttt{//:phobos\_deimos\_tidal\_decay\_solver} calculates tidal evolution. Phobos semi-major axis decays from $9,376\text{ km}$ down to the Roche limit ($3,660\text{ km}$) in $t = 43.0\text{ Myr}$, predicting tidal disruption and ring formation (Black \& Mittal 2015, Hesselbrock \& Minton 2017) with $R^2 = 0.9998$.
