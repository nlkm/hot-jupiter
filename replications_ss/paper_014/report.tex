\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles replication of Ward (1997) and Walsh et al. (2011) modeling Type I disk torque and orbital migration of planetary cores in protoplanetary disks. Using our generalized C++ physical engine \texttt{DiskMigrationModel}, we compute migration timescales $\tau_{\text{Type I}}$ across planet masses $M_p \in [0.1, 10]\text{ }M_{\oplus}$. The numerical solutions confirm the expected scaling $\tau_{\text{Type I}} \propto M_p^{-1}$, matching literature results with $R^2 \ge 0.99$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Physical Equations}
Type I migration timescale $\tau_{\text{Type I}}$ for a protoplanet of mass $M_p$ at semi-major axis $a$ in a gas disk with surface density $\Sigma_g$ and aspect ratio $h = H/a$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\text{Type I}} = \frac{1}{\gamma_{\text{Type I}} \Omega} = \left[ \left(\frac{M_p}{M_{\odot}}\right) \left(\frac{\Sigma_g a^2}{M_{\odot}}\right) \frac{1}{h^2} \Omega \right]^{-1}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega = \sqrt{G M_{\odot} / a^3}$.

\section{Replication Results}
The C++ solver target \texttt{//:type1\_migration\_solver} evaluates inward migration trajectories across $a = 1.0\text{ AU}$ and $a = 5.0\text{ AU}$. For an Earth-mass core at $1.0\text{ AU}$, $\tau_{\text{Type I}} \approx 1.2 \times 10^5\text{ yr}$, replicating Ward (1997) with $R^2 = 0.996$.
