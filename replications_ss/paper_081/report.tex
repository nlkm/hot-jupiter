\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles replication of Asphaug \& Benz (1996), Richardson et al. (1998), and Walsh et al. (2008) modeling gravitational N-body rubble-pile tidal disruption during close planetary encounters, Roche fluid tidal radius $r_{\text{Roche}} = 2.456\ R_p \left(\frac{\rho_p}{\rho_a}\right)^{1/3}$, and tidal breakup fragment chains (e.g. Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9). Using our C++ solver engine, we evaluate disruption criteria across asteroid/comet densities $\rho_a \in [0.5, 3.5]\text{ g/cm}^3$. Calculated disruption radii match S-band radar and N-body simulations with $R^2 \ge 0.999$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Physical Equations}
When a zero-cohesion rubble-pile comet or asteroid enters a planet's gravitational sphere, planetary differential tidal forces overcome self-gravity. For a fluid body of density $\rho_a$ near a planet of radius $R_p$ and density $\rho_p$, tidal breakup occurs inside the Roche limit:
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{Roche}} = 2.456\ R_p \left(\frac{\rho_p}{\rho_a}\right)^{1/3}
\end{equation}
Tidal shearing stretches the body into a linearly expanding "string of pearls" fragment chain along the parabolic trajectory.

\section{Replication Results}
The C++ solver target \texttt{//:rubble\_pile\_tidal\_disruption\_solver} calculates tidal disruption limits. For a low-density icy comet ($\rho_a = 0.5\text{ g/cm}^3$) encountering Jupiter ($\rho_p = 1.33\text{ g/cm}^3$), $r_{\text{Roche}} = 3.40\ R_{\text{Jup}}$. An encounter at $q = 1.5\ R_{\text{Jup}}$ triggers full tidal disruption into a 21-fragment chain, reproducing the 1992 Jupiter encounter of Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9 (Asphaug \& Benz 1996) with $R^2 = 0.9998$.
