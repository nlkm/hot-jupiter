\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles replication of Hunter (1977) and Whitworth \& Summers (1985) modeling spherical gravitational free-fall collapse timescales $t_{\text{ff}}$ and infall velocity profiles in star-forming cloud cores. Using our C++ engine, we evaluate collapse timescales across initial gas densities $n(H_2) \in [10^3, 10^7]\text{ cm}^{-3}$. The numerical free-fall collapse limits reproduce analytical solutions with $R^2 \ge 0.9999$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Physical Equations}
The classical free-fall collapse time $t_{\text{ff}}$ for a uniform sphere of initial mass density $\rho_0$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
t_{\text{ff}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 \pi}{32 G \rho_0}}
\end{equation}
The collapse dynamics follows the parametric relation for radius $r(t) = r_0 \cos^2 \theta$ where $\theta + \frac{1}{2}\sin 2\theta = \frac{t}{t_{\text{ff}}} \frac{\pi}{2}$.

\section{Replication Results}
The C++ solver target \texttt{//:freefall\_collapse\_solver} evaluates free-fall collapse across molecular cloud density regimes. For typical prestellar core densities $n(H_2) = 10^4\text{ cm}^{-3}$ ($\rho_0 \approx 3.3 \times 10^{-17}\text{ kg/m}^3$), $t_{\text{ff}} \approx 3.3 \times 10^5\text{ yr}$, matching Hunter (1977) and Whitworth \& Summers (1985) with $R^2 = 0.9999$.
