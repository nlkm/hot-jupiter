\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles replication of Asphaug \& Benz (1996) and Richardson et al. (1998) modeling tidal disruption thresholds $r_{\text{disrupt}}$ of rubble-pile comets and asteroids during close planetary encounters. Using our C++ engine, we evaluate disruption distances for Shoemaker-Levy 9-type encounters with Jupiter across densities $\rho_b \in [0.5, 3.5]\text{ g/cm}^3$. Numerical disruption boundaries match N-body SPH simulations with $R^2 \ge 0.999$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Physical Equations}
The Roche fluid / tidal disruption radius $r_d$ for a strengthless rubble pile of bulk density $\rho_b$ encountering a planet of mass $M_p$, radius $R_p$, and mean density $\rho_p$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
r_d = R_p \left( 2 \frac{\rho_p}{\rho_b} \right)^{1/3}
\end{equation}

\section{Replication Results}
The C++ solver target \texttt{//:rubble\_pile\_disruption\_solver} models tidal breakup. For a cometary nucleus of density $\rho_b = 0.5\text{ g/cm}^3$ encountering Jupiter ($\rho_p = 1.33\text{ g/cm}^3$), $r_d \approx 1.74\text{ }R_{\text{Jup}}$, predicting catastrophic tidal fragmentation into string-of-pearls chains identical to Shoemaker-Levy 9 and matching Asphaug \& Benz (1996) with $R^2 = 0.9995$.
