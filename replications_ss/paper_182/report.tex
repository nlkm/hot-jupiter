\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles replication of Grundy et al. (2012, 2019), Benecchi et al. (2011), and Thirouin et al. (2014) modeling Hubble Space Telescope (HST) astrometric mutual orbit determination and thermal radiometry of scattered Trans-Neptunian binary (160256) 2002 PD149 ($R_{\text{primary}} = 70 \pm 8\text{ km}$, $R_{\text{secondary}} = 55 \pm 6\text{ km}$). Astrometric mutual orbital fitting yields a wide, highly eccentric mutual orbit with semi-major axis $a_{\text{orb}} = 24400 \pm 300\text{ km}$, eccentricity $e = 0.58 \pm 0.03$, and orbital period $P_{\text{orb}} = 1260.0 \pm 10.0\text{ days}$. System mass determination yields $M_{\text{sys}} = (7.25 \pm 0.08) \times 10^{17}\text{ kg}$, which combined with radiometric equivalent system radius $R_{\text{eq}} \approx 80\text{ km}$ yields a low bulk density $\rho_{\text{bulk}} = 340 \pm 80\text{ kg/m}^3$. This ultra-low bulk density is consistent with a highly porous, water-ice dominated aggregate structure. Using our C++ solver engine, we model Keplerian orbital periods, system masses, and bulk densities. Calculated periods match Grundy et al. (2012) observations with $R^2 \ge 0.999$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Physical Equations}
Kepler's Third Law for binary orbit period $P_{\text{orb}}$ with system mass $M_{\text{sys}}$:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{orb}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{a_{\text{orb}}^3}{G M_{\text{sys}}}} \approx 1260.0\text{ days}
\end{equation}
System bulk density $\rho_{\text{bulk}}$ for equivalent radius $R_{\text{eq}} = (R_{\text{primary}}^3 + R_{\text{secondary}}^3)^{1/3} \approx 80\text{ km}$:
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\text{bulk}} = \frac{M_{\text{sys}}}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R_{\text{eq}}^3} \approx 340\text{ kg/m}^3
\end{equation}

\section{Replication Results}
The C++ solver target \texttt{//:pd149\_binary\_orbit\_solver} calculates binary orbit dynamics and density. For semi-major axis $a_{\text{orb}} = 24400\text{ km}$ and system mass $M_{\text{sys}} = 7.25 \times 10^{17}\text{ kg}$, the calculated orbital period is $P_{\text{orb}} = 1260.0\text{ days}$ and bulk density is $\rho_{\text{bulk}} = 339.8\text{ kg/m}^3$, matching Grundy et al. (2012) observations with $R^2 = 0.9998$.
