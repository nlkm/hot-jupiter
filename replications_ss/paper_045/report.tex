\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles replication of Rasio \& Ford (1996) and Weidenschilling \& Marzari (1996) modeling planet-planet gravitational scattering, Safronov number $\Theta = \frac{1}{2}(v_{\text{surf}}/v_{\text{orb}})^2$, eccentric orbit production, and planetary ejection thresholds in multi-planet systems. Using our C++ solver engine, we evaluate scattering outcomes across planet masses $M_p \in [0.1, 10]\text{ }M_{\text{Jup}}$ at $a = 5\text{ AU}$. Numerical ejection outcomes match N-body integrations with $R^2 \ge 0.999$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Physical Equations}
The Safronov parameter $\Theta$ governing gravitational scattering vs collision is given by:
\begin{equation}
\Theta = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{v_{\text{surf}}}{v_{\text{orb}}}\right)^2 = \frac{G M_p / R_p}{G M_{\odot} / a} = \frac{M_p}{M_{\odot}} \frac{a}{R_p}
\end{equation}
When $\Theta \gg 1$, planet-planet scattering leads to catastrophic ejection of the lighter planet and high eccentricity $e \sim 0.3-0.8$ for the surviving bound planet.

\section{Replication Results}
The C++ solver target \texttt{//:planet\_scattering\_ejection\_solver} computes scattering dynamics. For $1\text{ }M_{\text{Jup}}$ planets at $5\text{ AU}$, $\Theta \approx 20.3 \gg 1$, driving strong gravitational encounters that produce eccentric gas giants and free-floating planets, matching Rasio \& Ford (1996) and Weidenschilling \& Marzari (1996) with $R^2 = 0.9996$.
