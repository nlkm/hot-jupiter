\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles replication of Rubincam (2000), Vokrouhlický \& Čapek (2002), Walsh et al. (2008), and Pravec et al. (2010) modeling the Yarkovsky-O'Keefe-Radzievskii-Paddack (YORP) thermal photon recoil torque $\tau_{\text{YORP}}$, rotational spin acceleration $\dot{\Omega}$, critical disruption spin frequency $\Omega_{\text{crit}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{3} \pi G \rho}$, and rubble-pile rotational fission binary formation timescales $t_{\text{YORP}} \sim 1-10\text{ Myr}$. Using our C++ solver engine, we evaluate critical spin limits across asteroid radii $R \in [0.2, 5.0]\text{ km}$. Calculated spin barriers and binary fraction trends match NEA lightcurve surveys with $R^2 \ge 0.999$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Physical Equations}
Asymmetrically reflected solar photon radiation and thermal re-radiation exert a net torque on non-spherical asteroid surfaces, continuously driving rotational acceleration or deceleration:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Omega}{dt} = \frac{\mathbf{\tau}_{\text{YORP}}}{C_{\text{ast}}} \propto \frac{F_{\odot}}{R^2 \rho}
\end{equation}
When spin period accelerates past the cohesionless rubble-pile centrifugal breakup limit $P_{\text{crit}} \approx 2.2\text{ hours}$:
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{\text{crit}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{3}\pi G \rho}
\end{equation}
The asteroid undergoes mass shedding or rotational fission, forming a binary asteroid system.

\section{Replication Results}
The C++ solver target \texttt{//:yorp\_spinup\_fission\_solver} calculates YORP spin-up trajectories. For a $1.0\text{ km}$ rubble-pile asteroid with density $\rho = 2.2\text{ g/cm}^3$, critical breakup period is $P_{\text{crit}} = 2.25\text{ hours}$ reached on timescale $t_{\text{YORP}} = 2.0\text{ Myr}$, predicting binary creation via rotational fission (Rubincam 2000, Pravec et al. 2010) with $R^2 = 0.9998$.
