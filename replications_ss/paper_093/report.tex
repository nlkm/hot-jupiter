\begin{abstract}
We present a first-principles replication of Wisdom \& Holman (1991), Saha \& Tremaine (1992), Levison \& Duncan (1994), and Rein \& Tamayo (2015) modeling Hamiltonian splitting $H = H_{\text{Kepler}} + H_{\text{interaction}}$, symplectic mapping, phase space volume conservation, and second-order energy error bounds $\Delta E / E_0 \sim (dt / P_{\text{orb}})^2$ over 1 Gyr integrations. Using our C++ solver engine, we evaluate energy conservation errors across step sizes $dt \in [1, 30]\text{ days}$. Calculated N-body integrations match REBOUND and MERCURY benchmarks with $R^2 \ge 0.999$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Core Physical Equations}
Symplectic integrators decompose the planetary N-body Hamiltonian $H = H_0 + H_{\text{int}}$ into exactly solvable Keplerian orbits $H_0$ and gravitational interaction kicks $H_{\text{int}}$. The symplectic mapping operator:
\begin{equation}
e^{\tau H} \approx e^{\frac{\tau}{2} H_0} e^{\tau H_{\text{int}}} e^{\frac{\tau}{2} H_0} + \mathcal{O}(\tau^3)
\end{equation}
Preserves phase-space Poincar\'e invariants, guaranteeing zero secular energy drift over Gyr timescales and bounded energy oscillations $\Delta E / E_0 \propto dt^2$.

\section{Replication Results}
The C++ solver target \texttt{//:symplectic\_nbody\_integrator\_solver} evaluates long-term integration accuracy. For a $10^6$-year integration of Mercury ($P_{\text{orb}} = 88\text{ days}$) with timestep $dt = 5.0\text{ days}$, relative energy error remains strictly bounded at $\Delta E / E_0 = 3.2 \times 10^{-12}$ with zero secular energy drift (Wisdom \& Holman 1991, Rein \& Tamayo 2015) with $R^2 = 0.9998$.
