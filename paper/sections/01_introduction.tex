\section{Introduction \& Literature Context}

\subsection{The Hot Jupiter Radius Inflation Anomaly}
Since the discovery of transiting Hot Jupiters (e.g., HD 209458b, WASP-12b, WASP-17b, WASP-121b), observations have consistently revealed giant planets with anomalously large radii ($R_p \approx 1.3 - 2.0\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$). Standard non-irradiated evolutionary models (e.g., Marley et al. 2007; Fortney et al. 2007) predict that Jupiter-mass planets contract to $R_p \approx 1.0 - 1.1\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$ within $t \sim 0.5 - 1.0\text{ Gyr}$. While surface stellar irradiation ($F_{\text{inc}}$) retards atmospheric cooling by flattening the radiative temperature gradient (Guillot 2010), surface irradiation alone cannot explain the largest observed radii ($R_p > 1.4\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$) because heat must be deposited directly into the deep convective interior ($P > 1 - 10\text{ bar}$) to inflate the envelope adiabat.

\subsection{Proposed Inflation Mechanisms in Literature}
Several physical mechanisms have been proposed to explain deep interior heating:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tidal Dissipation (Bodenheimer et al. 2001; Jackson et al. 2008; Miller et al. 2009)}: Damping of orbital eccentricity ($e$) deposits tidal friction power into the convective envelope.
    \item \textbf{Ohmic Dissipation (Batygin \& Stevenson 2010; Menou 2012)}: Thermally ionized alkali metals in hot atmospheres ($T_{\text{eq}} > 1400\text{ K}$) move through planetary magnetic fields via zonal winds, generating electric currents that dissipate Joule heat into the deep interior.
    \item \textbf{Heavy-Element Core Scaling (Thorngren et al. 2016; Thorngren \& Fortney 2018)}: Host star metallicity $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$ dictates core mass $M_c$, introducing systematic structural density variations.
    \item \textbf{Observational Transit Selection Effects (Demory \& Seager 2011; Kipping 2014)}: Transit probability ($P_{\text{geo}} \propto R_p / a$) and transit depth ($\delta \propto R_p^2$) strongly oversample larger inflated planets in transit surveys.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Novelty \& Distinction from Existing Studies}
Prior studies in giant planet evolution generally fall into two categories:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single-Planet 1D Evolvers} (e.g., MESA, CEPAM, BARS): Detailed physical solvers for individual planets that do not run demographic population synthesis or account for survey selection functions.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Parametric Regressions} (e.g., Demory \& Seager 2011; Thorngren \& Fortney 2018): Statistical fits of deposited power fractions without dynamically integrating 1D hydrostatic differential equations forward over gigayears.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What is New in This Study}:
Our work bridges this gap by introducing a **6-Stage Incremental Hierarchy (Stages 0 $\to$ 5)** that systematically evaluates the marginal statistical contribution of each physical mechanism:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 0 (Non-irradiated Base)}: Standard cooling ($F_{\text{inc}} = 0, P_{\text{extra}} = 0$).
    \item \textbf{Stage 1 (Stellar Irradiation)}: Guillot (2010) irradiated atmospheric boundary.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2 (Irradiation + Metallicity Core Mass)}: Thorngren et al. (2016) core scaling $M_c([\mathrm{Fe/H}], M_p)$.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3 (Irradiation + Core Mass + Tidal Heating)}: Eccentricity damping power $P_{\mathrm{tidal}}$.
    \item \textbf{Stage 4 (Full Physical Model)}: Tidal heating + Ohmic dissipation $P_{\mathrm{Ohmic}}(T_{\text{eq}})$.
    \item \textbf{Stage 5 (Full Model + Transit Selection)}: Transit probability $W_{\mathrm{transit}} = P_{\mathrm{geo}} \cdot P_{\mathrm{det}}(\text{SNR})$.
\end{enumerate}
By computing two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) tests against NASA Exoplanet Archive observations at each stage, we quantify the step-by-step statistical progression toward demographic alignment.
