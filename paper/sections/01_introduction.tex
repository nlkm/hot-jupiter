\section{Introduction \& Literature Context}

\subsection{The Hot Jupiter Radius Inflation Anomaly}
Since the discovery of transiting Hot Jupiters (e.g., HD 209458b, WASP-12b, WASP-17b, WASP-121b), observations have consistently revealed giant planets with anomalously large radii ($R_p \approx 1.3 - 2.0\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$). Standard non-irradiated evolutionary models (e.g., Marley et al. 2007; Fortney et al. 2007; Chabrier \& Baraffe 1997) predict that Jupiter-mass planets contract to $R_p \approx 1.0 - 1.1\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$ within $t \sim 0.5 - 1.0\text{ Gyr}$. While surface stellar irradiation ($F_{\text{inc}}$) retards atmospheric cooling by flattening the radiative temperature gradient (Guillot 2010; Hansen 2008; Parmentier \& Guillot 2014), surface irradiation alone cannot explain the largest observed radii ($R_p > 1.4\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$) because heat must be deposited directly into the deep convective interior ($P > 1 - 10\text{ bar}$) to inflate the envelope adiabat.

\subsection{Proposed Inflation Mechanisms in Literature}
Several physical mechanisms have been proposed to explain deep interior heating:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tidal Dissipation (Bodenheimer et al. 2001; Goldreich \& Soter 1966; Hut 1981; Eggleton et al. 1998; Jackson et al. 2008; Miller et al. 2009; Leconte et al. 2010)}: Damping of orbital eccentricity ($e$) and asynchronous spin ($\Omega_{\mathrm{rot}}$) deposits tidal friction power into the convective envelope.
    \item \textbf{Ohmic Dissipation (Batygin \& Stevenson 2010; Menou 2012; Ginzburg \& Sari 2016)}: Thermally ionized alkali metals (Na, K) in hot atmospheres ($T_{\text{eq}} > 1200\text{ K}$) move through planetary magnetic fields via zonal winds, generating electric currents that dissipate Joule heat into the deep interior.
    \item \textbf{Heavy-Element Core Mass Scaling (Miller \& Fortney 2011; Thorngren et al. 2016; Thorngren \& Fortney 2018)}: Host star metallicity $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$ dictates core mass $M_c$, introducing systematic structural density variations.
    \item \textbf{Observational Transit Selection Effects (Borucki \& Summers 1984; Demory \& Seager 2011; Kipping et al. 2014)}: Transit probability ($P_{\text{geo}} \propto R_p / a$) and transit depth ($\delta \propto R_p^2$) strongly oversample larger inflated planets in transit surveys.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Broad Model Applicability Across 10 Physical Regimes}
To demonstrate that our numerical framework functions accurately across a diverse range of planetary environments, we validate it under ten distinct physical benchmark scenarios:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Jupiter Benchmark}: Evolving a cold giant baseline ($a = 5.204\text{ AU}, M_p = 1.00\,M_{\mathrm{Jup}}$) converging to $R_p = 1.000\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 124.4\text{ K}$ at $4.56\text{ Gyr}$ (Nettelmann et al. 2012).
    \item \textbf{Single-Planet Coupled Orbital \& Spin Dynamics}: Simultaneous thermal, orbital ($a, e$), and 3D spin vector ($\Omega_{\mathrm{rot}}, \varepsilon$) evolution.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Planet Secular Perturbations}: Laplace-Lagrange interaction matrices ($A_{ij}$) driving forced eccentricity floors ($e_{\mathrm{forced}} \approx 0.02 - 0.05$) and sustained tidal power $P_{\mathrm{tidal}, b}(t)$ (Murray \& Dermott 1999; Wu \& Lithwick 2011).
    \item \textbf{High Obliquity Tilt Dynamics}: Tidal damping of high initial spin axis tilt ($\varepsilon_0 = 45^\circ$) and asynchronous rotation shear.
    \item \textbf{Stellar Spin-Orbit Misalignment}: Comparative Rossiter-McLaughlin orbital evolution for aligned ($\psi_* = 0^\circ$), polar ($80^\circ$), and retrograde ($135^\circ$) orbits.
    \item \textbf{Stellar Rotation Driven Tides}: Sub-synchronous inward orbital decay ($P_* = 25\text{ d}$) vs super-synchronous outward orbital expansion ($P_* = 1.5\text{ d}$) (Ferraz-Mello et al. 2008; Ogilvie 2014).
    \item \textbf{Roche Lobe Overflow (RLOF) Mass Loss}: Mass stripping through inner Lagrange point $L_1$ ($\dot{M}_{\mathrm{RLOF}}$) when thermal inflation fills Roche lobe ($R_p \ge R_{\mathrm{Roche}}$) (Eggleton 1983; Li et al. 2010).
    \item \textbf{Demographic Population Synthesis}: 6-Stage Monte Carlo population comparison against empirical Kepler/WASP Hot Jupiters ($D_{\mathrm{KS}} = 0.080, p = 0.910$).
    \item \textbf{JWST Scale Height \& Transit Depth Inflation}: Interior heating expanding atmospheric scale height ($H: 420 \to 780\text{ km}$) and transit depth signal variations ($\Delta \delta: 140 \to 360\text{ ppm}$).
    \item \textbf{Coupled XUV Photoevaporation \& RLOF}: Simultaneous energy-limited stellar XUV photoevaporative mass loss ($\dot{M}_{\mathrm{XUV}}$) and hydrodynamic Roche overflow ($\dot{M}_{\mathrm{RLOF}}$) (Watson et al. 1981; Owen \& Wu 2013; Lopez \& Fortney 2014).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Novelty \& Distinction from Existing Studies}
Prior studies in giant planet evolution generally fall into two categories:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single-Planet 1D Evolvers} (e.g., MESA, CEPAM, BARS): Detailed physical solvers for individual planets that do not run demographic population synthesis or account for survey selection functions.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Parametric Regressions} (e.g., Demory \& Seager 2011; Thorngren \& Fortney 2018): Statistical fits of deposited power fractions without dynamically integrating 1D hydrostatic differential equations forward over gigayears.
\end{itemize}

Our work bridges this gap by introducing a \textbf{6-Stage Incremental Hierarchy (Stages 0 $\to$ 5)} that systematically evaluates the marginal statistical contribution of each physical mechanism across demographic populations of $N = 10,000$ synthetic systems ($60,000$ total 4.56-Gyr evolutionary tracks).
