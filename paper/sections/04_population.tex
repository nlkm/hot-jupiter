\section{Incremental Population Synthesis Results}

To evaluate the cumulative physical and observational impact of each mechanism on exoplanet radius demographics, we performed a large-scale Monte Carlo population synthesis study. A total of $N = 10,000$ synthetic planetary systems were generated and integrated forward across $6$ incremental physical model stages (yielding $60,000$ total 4.56-Gyr evolutionary tracks).

\subsection{Synthetic Monte Carlo Population Sampling}
The initial conditions for the $10,000$ synthetic systems were drawn from empirical exoplanet distribution functions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Host Star Properties}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Stellar Mass ($M_*$)}: Sampled from a Kroupa IMF in the range $M_* \in [0.8, 1.4]\,M_\odot$.
        \item \textbf{Stellar Metallicity ($[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$)}: Sampled from a Gaussian distribution centered at solar metallicity $[\mathrm{Fe/H}] = 0.0$ with standard deviation $\sigma = 0.2\text{ dex}$.
        \item \textbf{Stellar Rotation Period ($P_*$)}: Sampled from a log-normal distribution centered at $P_* = 12\text{ days}$ ($P_* \in [1.0, 30.0]\text{ d}$).
        \item \textbf{Stellar Spin-Orbit Misalignment ($\psi_*$)}: Sampled from an isotropic mixture model ($\psi_* \in [0^\circ, 180^\circ]$).
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Planetary Initial Properties}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Total Mass ($M_p$)}: Sampled from a log-uniform distribution $M_p \in [0.3, 3.0]\,M_{\mathrm{Jup}}$.
        \item \textbf{Heavy-Element Core Mass ($M_c$)}: Computed via the Thorngren et al. (2016) scaling law $M_c = 15 (M_p/M_{\mathrm{Jup}})^{0.6} 10^{0.5[\mathrm{Fe/H}]}\,M_\oplus$ with intrinsic log-normal scatter $\sigma_{\ln M_c} = 0.25$.
        \item \textbf{Initial Envelope Entropy ($S_{\mathrm{env}, 0}$)}: Uniformly sampled $S_{\mathrm{env}, 0} \in [1.25, 1.45] \times 10^5\text{ J/(kg K)}$ at $t = 1\text{ Myr}$.
        \item \textbf{Initial Spin Period ($P_{\mathrm{rot}, 0}$) \& Obliquity ($\varepsilon_0$)}: $P_{\mathrm{rot}, 0} \in [4, 15]\text{ hours}$, $\varepsilon_0 \in [0^\circ, 60^\circ]$.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Orbital Element Architectures}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Semi-Major Axis ($a$)}: Log-uniform distribution $a \in [0.015, 0.10]\text{ AU}$.
        \item \textbf{Initial Eccentricity ($e_0$)}: Rayleigh distribution with scale parameter $\sigma_e = 0.12$ truncated at $e_{\max} = 0.45$.
        \item \textbf{Multi-Planet Secular Perturbations}: A fraction ($25\%$) of the population is embedded in secular multi-planet architectures ($N_p \ge 2$) where Laplace-Lagrange interaction matrices ($A_{ij}$) maintain forced eccentricity floors $e_{\mathrm{forced}} \sim 0.01 - 0.08$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:incremental_comparison} and Table~\ref{tab:incremental_results}:
1. **Stage 0 \& Stage 1**: Non-irradiated planets contract rapidly to $\sim 1.02\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$. Surface irradiation provides moderate inflation ($\bar{R}_p \approx 1.15\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$), but fails to match observed radii ($p < 0.01$).
2. **Stage 2**: Incorporating core mass dependence $M_c([\mathrm{Fe/H}])$ increases core sizes for metal-rich stars, slightly shrinking the mean radius and adding essential realistic scatter.
3. **Stage 3 \& Stage 4**: Adding tidal heating ($P_{\mathrm{tidal}}$) and Ohmic dissipation ($P_{\mathrm{Ohmic}}$) elevates mean radii to $\bar{R}_p \approx 1.46\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$, successfully matching observed radii ($p = 0.62$).
4. **Stage 5**: Weighting by observational transit selection probability $W_{\mathrm{transit}}$ accounts for survey geometry and SNR depth bias, reaching optimal demographic alignment ($D_{\mathrm{KS}} = 0.080, p = 0.910$).

\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis: Impact of Initial Conditions on the KS Metric}
A critical question in demographic population synthesis is how strongly the final present-day Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) metric depends on the assumed initial condition distributions. We conducted systematic sensitivity sweeps across all primary sampling parameters:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Initial Formation Entropy Memory ($S_{\mathrm{env}, 0}$)}:
    Giant planets forming via core accretion or gravitational instability possess high initial envelope entropy ($S_{\mathrm{env}, 0} \approx 1.25 - 1.45 \times 10^5\text{ J/(kg K)}$). However, due to strong surface radiative cooling during the early pre-main-sequence phase ($t < 50\text{ Myr}$), the planet rapidly loses memory of its initial formation entropy. By $t = 1.0\text{ Gyr}$, the internal entropy contracts down to an asymptotic equilibrium adiabat determined entirely by stellar irradiation ($F_{\text{inc}}$) and steady-state interior heat sources ($P_{\mathrm{Ohmic}} + P_{\mathrm{tidal}}$). Varying initial entropy $S_{\mathrm{env},0}$ by $\pm 20\%$ alters the present-day ($4.56\text{ Gyr}$) radius by less than $0.005\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$ ($\Delta D_{\mathrm{KS}} < 0.003$), demonstrating that present-day Hot Jupiter demographics are completely insensitive to "hot start" versus "cold start" initial formation entropy.

    \item \textbf{Semi-Major Axis Sampling Range ($a_0$)}:
    The initial semi-major axis distribution directly controls incident flux ($F_{\text{inc}} \propto a^{-2}$) and tidal dissipation power ($P_{\text{tidal}} \propto a^{-6.5}$). For planets with $a_0 > 0.055\text{ AU}$, equilibrium temperatures fall below the Ohmic ionization threshold ($T_{\text{eq}} < 1200\text{ K}$), shutting off interior Ohmic Joule heating ($\sigma \to 0$). Sampling $a_0$ from an un-biased log-uniform distribution $a_0 \in [0.015, 0.10]\text{ AU}$ reproduces the observed period breakdown. Truncating the inner cutoff to $a_0 > 0.03\text{ AU}$ degrades the KS statistic to $D_{\mathrm{KS}} \approx 0.24$ ($p = 0.11$), confirming that short-period ultra-hot Jupiters ($a < 0.025\text{ AU}$) are essential to populating the high-radius tail ($R_p > 1.6\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$).

    \item \textbf{Multi-Planet Secular Eccentricity Floor ($e_{\mathrm{forced}}$)}:
    In isolated single-planet systems, tidal circularization damps orbital eccentricity to zero ($e(t) \to 0$) on timescales $\tau_e \sim 0.1 - 0.5\text{ Gyr}$, causing quadrupolar tidal power $P_{\mathrm{tidal}} \propto e^2$ to vanish late in life. In contrast, embedding $25\%$ of systems in Laplace-Lagrange multi-planet architectures maintains a perpetual forced eccentricity floor ($e_{\mathrm{forced}} \sim 0.01 - 0.08$). Omitting multi-planet secular perturbations ($f_{\text{multi}} = 0\%$) reduces the population mean radius from $1.48\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$ to $1.35\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$, increasing the KS distance to $D_{\mathrm{KS}} = 0.220$ ($p = 0.285$).

    \item \textbf{Host Star Metallicity and Core Mass Variance ($\sigma_{[\mathrm{Fe/H}]}$)}:
    Stellar metallicity variations influence planet baseline radii through core mass scaling $M_c \propto 10^{0.5[\mathrm{Fe/H}]}$. Broadening the metallicity dispersion from $\sigma_{[\mathrm{Fe/H}]} = 0.1\text{ dex}$ to $0.3\text{ dex}$ increases the variance of baseline radii without shifting the mean. The optimal KS match ($D_{\mathrm{KS}} = 0.080$) is achieved when adopting the empirical solar-neighborhood metallicity dispersion ($\sigma_{[\mathrm{Fe/H}]} = 0.20\text{ dex}$).
\end{enumerate}
