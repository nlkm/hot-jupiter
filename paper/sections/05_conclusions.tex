\section{Conclusions}

In this work, we presented a modular physical evolution framework and population synthesis suite (\texttt{hot\_jupiter}) that evaluates the cumulative impact of core radiogenic decay, equilibrium tidal flexure, obliquity damping, stellar rotation tides, Roche lobe overflow (RLOF) mass loss, and atmospheric MHD Ohmic dissipation on giant exoplanet radii. By integrating 1D hydrostatic envelope contraction with 6D orbital elements and 3D spin vector dynamics, we conducted dynamic 4.56-Gyr evolutionary simulations for individual benchmark targets and a synthetic Monte Carlo population of $N = 10,000$ planets ($60,000$ total 4.56-Gyr evolutionary tracks across 6 incremental model stages).

Our main physical and statistical conclusions are summarized as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inadequacy of Surface Irradiation Alone}:
    Surface stellar irradiation ($F_{\mathrm{inc}}$) flattens the radiative atmospheric temperature gradient at optical depth $\tau \sim 2/3$, retarding Kelvin-Helmholtz cooling. However, surface irradiation alone cannot deposit energy into the deep convective envelope ($P > 1 - 10\text{ bar}$). Pure irradiation models (Stage 1) yield a mean radius of $\bar{R}_p = 1.15 \pm 0.11\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$, failing to match the observed Hot Jupiter radius catalog ($D_{\mathrm{KS}} = 0.520, p = 0.003$).

    \item \textbf{Necessity of Deep Interior Heat Deposition}:
    To explain giant planets with radii $R_p > 1.4\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$, heat must be deposited directly into the deep interior. Incorporating quadrupolar orbital tidal flexure ($P_{\mathrm{tidal}} \propto (R_p/a)^5 e^2$) and magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) Ohmic Joule dissipation ($P_{\mathrm{Ohmic}} \propto \int J^2/\sigma\,dV$) elevates envelope entropy ($S_{\mathrm{env}}$) and halts volume contraction. The full physical model (Stage 4) achieves a mean radius of $\bar{R}_p = 1.46 \pm 0.24\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$, successfully matching observed exoplanet radius distributions ($D_{\mathrm{KS}} = 0.150, p = 0.620$).

    \item \textbf{Multi-Planet Secular Eccentricity Maintenance}:
    In isolated single-planet systems, tidal circularization rapidly damps orbital eccentricity ($e \to 0$) within $\sim 100 - 500\text{ Myr}$, turning off tidal dissipation. In multi-planet architectures ($N_p \ge 2$), Laplace-Lagrange secular interactions ($A_{ij}$) continuously pump angular momentum back into the inner Hot Jupiter, establishing a forced eccentricity floor ($e_{\mathrm{forced}} \approx 0.02 - 0.05$). This secular coupling maintains ongoing tidal dissipation ($P_{\mathrm{tidal}} > 10^{18}\text{ W}$) for $t = 4.56\text{ Gyr}$, explaining long-lived inflated planets in multi-body systems.

    \item \textbf{Stellar Rotation Tides, Roche-Modified Hydrostatics \& Hydrodynamic Mass Stripping}:
    Host star rotation frequency $\Omega_* = 2\pi/P_*$ dictates orbital migration direction. Sub-synchronous stars ($P_* = 25\text{ d}, n > \Omega_*$) produce a lagging stellar tidal bulge ($\Delta\phi_* < 0$) driving inward orbital decay ($a: 0.030 \to 0.005\text{ AU}$). To account for Roche lobe distortion without artificial clamps, we derived a Roche-modified 1D hydrostatic equilibrium equation $g_{\mathrm{eff}}(r) = g_{\mathrm{iso}}(r)[1 - (r/R_{\mathrm{Roche}})^3]$. Near Roche overfilling ($r \to R_{\mathrm{Roche}}$), $g_{\mathrm{eff}} \to 0$ and atmospheric scale height diverges ($H \to \infty$), naturally driving hydrodynamic $L_1$ nozzle mass loss ($\dot{M}_{\mathrm{RLOF}}$) based on Lubow \& Shu (1975) physics.

    \item \textbf{Observational Transit Selection Geometry}:
    Photometric transit surveys strongly over-sample larger, short-period planets due to geometric alignment probability ($P_{\mathrm{geo}} \propto R_p/a$) and signal-to-noise ratio depth scaling ($\mathrm{SNR} \propto R_p^2$). Weighting the $N = 10,000$ synthetic population by the observational selection function $W_{\mathrm{transit}} = P_{\mathrm{geo}} \cdot P_{\text{det}}(\mathrm{SNR})$ (Stage 5) brings the model into optimal demographic alignment with confirmed Kepler/WASP observations ($\bar{R}_p = 1.48 \pm 0.25\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$, $D_{\mathrm{KS}} = 0.080$, $p = 0.910$).

    \item \textbf{First-Principles C++ / Bazel 8 Reproducibility}:
    All evolution models and population synthesis tools are implemented in high-performance C++ built with Bazel 8 Bzlmod rules (\texttt{//:unit\_tests}, passing 12/12 physical unit test targets). Every equation in the manuscript is accompanied by a self-contained, first-principles derivation from fundamental physics principles.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future Work \& Next Steps}
Several promising avenues remain for future investigation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{3D Atmospheric Circulation \& Zonal Superrotation}: Coupling 3D General Circulation Model (GCM) wind profiles $\mathbf{v}(\theta, \phi)$ into the Ohmic dissipation solver to model advective day-to-night temperature offsets and magnetic drag.
    \item \textbf{Double-Diffusive Composition Gradients}: Extending the 1D hydrostatic integrator to simulate semi-convection and compositional transport ($\nabla_\mu > 0$), evaluating how heavy-element core erosion traps primordial heat.
    \item \textbf{Photoevaporative Mass Loss}: Coupling Roche lobe overflow with stellar XUV photoevaporation ($\dot{M}_{\mathrm{XUV}}$) to explore the boundary between inflated Hot Jupiters and evaporated ultra-short-period sub-Neptunes.
    \item \textbf{JWST \& ESA Ariel Population Validation}: Applying the population synthesis suite to predict atmospheric scale height inflation ($H = k_B T / \mu g$) and transmission spectrum transit depth variations ($\Delta \delta$) for upcoming JWST and Ariel spectroscopic surveys.
\end{itemize}
