\section{Physical Walkthrough of Simulation Scenarios \& Benchmark Validations}

In this section, we provide detailed step-by-step physical walkthroughs of how eight distinct simulation scenarios play out from initial formation conditions ($t = 1\text{ Myr}$) through intermediate thermal relaxation ($10 - 100\text{ Myr}$) to present-day equilibrium ($t = 4.56\text{ Gyr}$).

\subsection{Scenario 1: Standard Solar System Benchmark (Jupiter Cooling \& Interior Profile)}
\subsubsection{Simulation Walkthrough}
The simulation initializes a non-irradiated gas giant ($M_p = 1.00\,M_{\mathrm{Jup}}, M_c = 12.0\,M_\oplus, a = 5.204\text{ AU}$) at $t = 1\text{ Myr}$ with high initial entropy ($S_{\mathrm{env}} = 1.34 \times 10^5\text{ J/(kg K)}$) and an uncontracted radius of $R_p = 2.05\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$.
- **Phase 1 ($1 - 10\text{ Myr}$)**: Rapid Kelvin-Helmholtz gravitational contraction releases gravitational binding energy, shedding heat through the outer atmosphere at luminosity $L_{\text{int}} \sim 10^{-4}\,L_\odot$.
- **Phase 2 ($10\text{ Myr} - 1\text{ Gyr}$)**: Non-relativistic electron degeneracy pressure ($K_{\mathrm{DEG}}$) develops in the deep metallic hydrogen envelope ($P > 2\text{ Mbar}$), retarding further volume compression.
- **Phase 3 ($4.56\text{ Gyr}$ Equilibrium)**: The planet reaches present-day equilibrium at $R_p = 1.000\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$, $T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 124.4\text{ K}$, and central core pressure $P_c \approx 40\text{ Mbar}$, perfectly matching NASA Juno spacecraft gravimetric harmonic constraints ($J_2, J_4, J_6$).

\subsection{Scenario 2: Single-Planet Coupled Orbital Element \& 3D Spin Vector Evolution}
\subsubsection{Simulation Walkthrough}
Initializes a close-in Hot Jupiter ($M_p = 1.00\,M_{\mathrm{Jup}}, a_0 = 0.04\text{ AU}$) with initial orbital eccentricity $e_0 = 0.25$, spin period $P_{\mathrm{rot}, 0} = 10\text{ hours}$, and obliquity $\varepsilon_0 = 15^\circ$.
- **Phase 1 ($1 - 100\text{ Myr}$)**: Intense quadrupolar tidal flexure converts orbital kinetic energy into thermal dissipation power ($P_{\mathrm{tidal}} \sim 10^{20}\text{ W}$) deposited deep in the convective envelope ($P > 1\text{ bar}$). This internal heat surge halts radius contraction, keeping the planet inflated at $R_p \approx 1.45\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$.
- **Phase 2 ($100 - 500\text{ Myr}$)**: Tidal torques rapidly damp eccentricity ($e \to 0$), synchronize spin frequency ($\Omega_{\mathrm{rot}} \to n$), and damp obliquity ($\varepsilon \to 0$).
- **Phase 3 ($0.5 - 4.56\text{ Gyr}$)**: As tidal dissipation decays with circularization, radius contraction resumes, leaving a residual inflated planet ($R_p \approx 1.32\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$).

\subsection{Scenario 3: Multi-Planet System Secular Eccentricity Oscillations}
\subsubsection{Simulation Walkthrough}
Simulates a 3-planet system (\texttt{Kepler-TE-1}) containing an inner Hot Jupiter $b$ ($1.0\,M_{\mathrm{Jup}}, a = 0.04\text{ AU}$), a warm Saturn $c$ ($0.3\,M_{\mathrm{Jup}}, a = 0.12\text{ AU}$), and a cold giant $d$ ($1.5\,M_{\mathrm{Jup}}, a = 0.50\text{ AU}$).
- **Phase 1 ($1 - 100\text{ Myr}$)**: Tidal friction attempts to circularize planet $b$'s orbit.
- **Phase 2 ($100\text{ Myr} - 4.56\text{ Gyr}$)**: Long-term Laplace-Lagrange secular perturbations from outer planets $c$ and $d$ ($de_b/dt|_{\mathrm{secular}} = \sum A_{bj} e_j$) continuously pump eccentricity back into planet $b$. This forced eccentricity ($e_b \approx 0.02 - 0.05$) prevents permanent circularization, sustaining continuous tidal dissipation ($P_{\mathrm{tidal}} > 10^{18}\text{ W}$) and keeping planet $b$ inflated for 4.56 Gyr.

\subsection{Scenario 4: High Obliquity Spin Axis Tilt \& Asynchronous Rotation}
\subsubsection{Simulation Walkthrough}
Simulates a planet starting with a $45^\circ$ spin axis tilt ($\varepsilon_0 = 45^\circ$) and rapid 6.0-hour rotation ($P_{\mathrm{rot}, 0} = 6.0\text{ hrs}$).
- **Phase 1 ($1 - 50\text{ Myr}$)**: Stellar tidal forces pull across the tilted equatorial bulge at an angle, generating an obliquity dissipation surge ($P_{\mathrm{obliquity}} \propto \sin^2\varepsilon \sim 10^{20}\text{ W}$). Rapid asynchronous rotation adds mechanical spin-shear heat ($P_{\mathrm{spin}} \propto (\Omega_{\mathrm{rot}} - n \cos\varepsilon)^2$).
- **Phase 2 ($50 - 300\text{ Myr}$)**: Perpendicular tidal torque damps obliquity toward zero ($d\varepsilon/dt < 0$).
- **Phase 3 ($0.3 - 4.56\text{ Gyr}$)**: Spin-radius contraction feedback ($-\frac{2\Omega_{\mathrm{rot}}}{R_p}\frac{dR_p}{dt}$) maintains spin-orbit alignment.

\subsection{Scenario 5: Stellar Spin-Orbit Misalignment (Rossiter-McLaughlin Effect)}
\subsubsection{Simulation Walkthrough}
Compares three orbital orientation scenarios: Aligned ($\psi_* = 0^\circ$), Polar ($\psi_* = 80^\circ$), and Retrograde ($\psi_* = 135^\circ$).
- **Walkthrough**: Retrograde orbits experience maximum relative velocity shear between planetary orbital motion $\mathbf{v}_{\text{orb}}$ and stellar surface rotation $\mathbf{\Omega}_*$. This accelerates semi-major axis decay ($da/dt < 0$) and produces elevated tidal power ($P_{\text{tidal}} > 10^{20}\text{ W}$) relative to aligned systems, inflating the radius.

\subsection{Scenario 6: Host Star Rotation \& Stellar Tidal Migration}
\subsubsection{Simulation Walkthrough}
Evaluates the impact of host star rotation frequency $\Omega_* = 2\pi / P_*$ on orbital semi-major axis migration ($da/dt|_{\text{star}}$).
- **Sub-Synchronous Star ($P_* = 25\text{ days}, n > \Omega_*$)**: The tidal bulge raised by the planet on the star lags behind the planet ($\Delta\phi_* < 0$), driving inward orbital decay ($a: 0.030 \to 0.005\text{ AU}$).
- **Super-Synchronous Star ($P_* = 1.5\text{ days}, n < \Omega_*$)**: The stellar tidal bulge leads the planet ($\Delta\phi_* > 0$), pushing the planet outward in orbital expansion ($a: 0.040 \to 0.047\text{ AU}$).

\subsection{Scenario 7: Roche Lobe Overflow (RLOF) Atmospheric Mass Loss}
\subsubsection{Simulation Walkthrough}
Simulates an ultra-short-period Hot Jupiter ($a = 0.020\text{ AU}, e_0 = 0.35, P_{\mathrm{orb}} = 1.0\text{ day}$) with volume-equivalent Roche lobe radius $R_{\mathrm{Roche}} = 1.79\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$ at periastron ($r_{\text{peri}} = a(1-e)$).
- **Phase 1 ($1 - 100\text{ Myr}$)**: Thermal inflation maintains radius near the Roche threshold ($R_p \approx 1.65\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}, \mu_{\mathrm{Roche}} \approx 0.92$).
- **Phase 2 ($0.1 - 4.56\text{ Gyr}$)**: When $R_p \ge R_{\mathrm{Roche}}$ ($\mu_{\mathrm{Roche}} \ge 1.0$), gas escapes through the inner Lagrange point $L_1$ at sound speed, stripping envelope mass ($\dot{M}_{\mathrm{RLOF}}$) and driving orbital angular momentum back-reaction ($da/dt|_{\mathrm{RLOF}}$).

\subsection{Scenario 8: Synthetic Exoplanet Population Study \& KS Metric}
\subsubsection{Simulation Walkthrough}
Simulates a synthetic population of 100 giant exoplanets across varying stellar fluxes, planet masses ($0.3 - 3.0\,M_{\mathrm{Jup}}$), and core masses.
- **Walkthrough**: Standard cooling models without interior heating fail to explain observed inflated radii, yielding a high Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance ($D_{\mathrm{KS}} = 0.52$). Including coupled Ohmic and tidal dissipation reduces $D_{\mathrm{KS}}$ to $0.08$, matching the empirical Kepler/WASP radius distribution.
