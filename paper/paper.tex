\begin{abstract}
Observations of close-in giant exoplanets (Hot Jupiters) consistently reveal radii significantly larger than predicted by standard non-irradiated evolution models ($R_p \approx 1.3 - 2.0\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$ vs baseline $1.0 - 1.1\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$). While surface stellar irradiation retards atmospheric cooling, it cannot explain deep envelope inflation without additional interior heat deposition. In this work, we present a modular C++/Bazel 8 physical solver and Monte Carlo population framework to systematically evaluate six incremental physical inflation stages across $N = 10,000$ synthetic evolutions ($60,000$ total 4.56-Gyr tracks). We model 1D hydrostatic differential structure, Saumon-Chabrier EoS, Guillot double-gray radiative boundaries, quadrupolar tidal flexure, Ohmic dissipation, Laplace-Lagrange secular perturbations, stellar rotation tides, Roche lobe mass loss, and JWST scale height variations across 10 physical scenarios. Incorporating physical Ohmic dissipation thresholds ($T_{\mathrm{eq}} > 1200\text{ K}$) and multi-planet forced eccentricity floors ($e_{\mathrm{forced}} \sim 0.02 - 0.07$) coupled with survey transit selection geometry ($W_{\mathrm{transit}} = P_{\text{geo}} \cdot P_{\text{det}}$) shifts the population mean radius to $\bar{R}_p = 1.48 \pm 0.25\,R_{\mathrm{Jup}}$, achieving outstanding demographic alignment with all $N = 342$ confirmed NASA Exoplanet Archive Hot Jupiters ($D_{\mathrm{KS}} = 0.080, p = 0.910$).
\end{abstract}
